Today’s payroll functions are striving to balance day-to-day 
administrative and compliance needs with more strategic 
approaches. While the payroll functions in most organizations 
excel at the former, only a quarter operate at higher strategic 
levels, according to our latest research. 
Why is a more strategic approach important? After all, payroll 
has always been about the practicalities of getting people paid 
accurately and on time while staying compliant with rules and 
regulations. 
“Amid persistent impediments to achieving efficiency and risk 
mitigation in processing payroll, payroll professionals also face 
a necessity and opportunity to become strategic in the eyes 
of organizational leadership, ” says Brent Skinner, Executive 
Community Leader of the Future of Payroll and Workforce 
Management at HR.com. “The key to their success lies in finding 
modern, innovative solutions for these operational, security 
and compliance-related challenges even while leveraging their 
newfound time and mental space to lift themselves out of a purely 
reactive and process-focused mindset. "
Challenges such as competitive pay, pay equity, and economic 
pressures are just a few of the factors driving payroll departments 
to take more strategic points of view. In doing so, payroll can 
increasingly improve the employee experience, optimize costs, 
ensure compliance, and support better decision-making. 
Integrating payroll with broader workforce and financial strategies 
enhances its value as a driver of business success.
Executive Summary
About the Survey
This study is based on 
HR.com’s “Future of Payroll 
2025” survey, which ran from 
November 2024 to January 
2025. We gathered responses 
from 206 HR professionals 
in virtually every industry 
vertical. Respondents are 
from all over the world, with 
the majority from North 
America, especially the United 
States. 
The respondents represent 
organizations with a broad 
cross-section of employees 
ranging from small 
businesses with fewer than 50 
employees to enterprises with 
over  20,000 employees. About 
70% of our respondents were 
from organizations with over 
100 employees.

but the proportion of users is growing.
 ● Close to two-thirds of organizations say they do not use EOR and

The State of Today’s Payroll Functions
Finding: Most payroll departments 
are neither highly transformative nor 
completely transactional but find 
themselves somewhere in between 
We asked respondents to rate their payroll function on the HR Research 
Institute’s Payroll Maturity Model (see model below). The results 
illustrate a spectrum of payroll sophistication across organizations, 
with over half (52%) falling into the “Managed” category. While this 
reflects consistent processes and a notable level of automation, it also 
indicates that many organizations are yet to fully leverage payroll as a 
strategic tool. 
“In payroll, ‘tweeners’ are halfway to becoming fully mature in their 
payroll journey, ” says Brent Skinner, Executive Community Leader of the 
Future of Payroll and Workforce Management at HR.com. “The question 
is whether they see new opportunities appearing on the horizon. Too 
many payroll managers, overly focused on eliminating inefficiencies, fail 
to recognize the need to pivot to imagining payroll as a more strategic 
function. Absent a new mindset as they optimize operations, they 
run the risk of engineering themselves out of existence rather than 
recasting themselves as trusted advisors to organizational leadership. ”
A smaller portion (25%) operates at the “Optimized” or “Strategic” levels, 
showcasing advanced capabilities such as integration with business 
systems and predictive analytics. However, only 6% of organizations 
have achieved the highest level, where payroll actively contributes 
to workforce planning and broader business objectives. On the other 
end of the spectrum, 23% of organizations remain in the “Ad-hoc” or 
“Reactive” stages, reflecting significant vulnerabilities in efficiency, 
compliance, and scalability.
For the purpose of this report, 
large organizations have 1,000 
or more employees, midsize 
organizations have 100 to 
999 employees, and small 
organizations have 99 or 
fewer employees.

Payroll-based cohorts
To better analyze the data from our survey on people analytics, we broke our respondents into groups or 
cohorts based on the previous question. 
Transactional payroll functions:  this category includes respondents who indicated that their 
organizations’ payroll functions are at the “Ad-hoc” or “Reactive” stages.  
Transformative payroll functions:  this category includes respondents who indicated that their 
organizations’ payroll functions are at the “Optimized” or “Strategic” stages.
Throughout this report, we will examine the data in a way that identifies correlations between various 
organizational factors and the strategic nature of their payroll functions. Correlation does not prove 
causation, of course, but these correlations may point our attention toward possible best practices and 
strategies.

Finding: Payroll functions typically excel 
at compliance, with nearly nine in 10 
organizations rating their compliance feature 
as good or excellent  
Organizations rely on payroll to stay compliant, and the vast majority of 
respondents say they live up to this requirement. Two-fifths (42%) are 
excellent and another 45% are good at compliance. Only 3% indicate they 
are poor at compliance. This trend has remained true over the 
past two 
years of our research on this topic. 
Close to two-thirds are either good (49%) or excellent (15%) at the 
potentially challenging task of adjusting pay for different geographies. 
Another notable finding is that 41% say they are good at enhancing the 
employee experience, and 21% say they are excellent at it. This likely 
reflects the broader trend in HR to see its mission as providing a good 
employee experience, not just completing transactions.
“Effective compliance-related functionality has become table stakes 
among vendors of software for payroll, ” says Skinner. “The most 
encouraging finding here is that more than two-fifths of respondents see 
the connection between payroll and the employee experience. Payroll is, in 
fact, ground zero for the employee experience. The tricky part is that when 
payroll goes right, employees tend not to notice, whereas when it goes 
wrong, organizations experience an acute threat to employee retention. ”
Differences based on organization size
Larger organizations are more likely than midsized or small ones to say 
their payroll strategy empowers payroll professionals to contribute as 
strategic business partners, offers valuable payroll add-ons, and adjusts 
pay for work in different geographies.

have undergone a more recent strategy

Transformative payroll functions have redesigned their payroll strategy 
more recently compared to the transactional ones. Thirty-seven percent

get there, they tend to stay. Contrast this with payroll departments 
more apt to be strategic, the transformative ones. They understand 
that strategy demands continual adjustments and reassessment of 
direction. They have a goal to achieve at all times, not just a mere 
destination to reach. ” 
While 19% of transactional payroll functions have never been 
redesigned, only 3% of transformative functions haven't undergone a

HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 
Based on our research, please consider these suggestions. 
 ● Strengthen strategic payroll: Focus not only on transactional efficiencies but move to strategic payroll 
initiatives that align with broader business objectives and drive long-term value.
 ● Enhance competitive differentiation: Use payroll strategies creatively to position the organization as a leader 
in employee benefits and experience, setting it apart from competitors.
 ● Adapt to economic changes: Build payroll systems that dynamically respond to economic fluctuations, 
ensuring consistency and trust among employees.
 ● Focus on employee experience: Modernize payroll processes to make them more transparent and employee-
friendly, fostering engagement and satisfaction.
 ● Commit to continuous innovation: Regularly redesign payroll strategies to stay agile and responsive to 
evolving workforce needs and industry trends.

Finding: Paying competitively is the biggest 
challenge to payroll strategy
The top challenge organizations face in their payroll strategy is paying 
competitively in today’s market (47%), followed by staying updated on 
laws (42%) and managing economic trends like inflation and recession 
(38%).
These concerns reflect the pressure to offer competitive compensation 
while ensuring legal compliance and adapting to changing economic 
conditions. In fact, paying competitively has remained the top challenge 
to payroll strategy 
over the last two years, while dealing with economic 
trends has remained in the top 3. 
“The top three goals of payroll strategy mirror the main objectives of 
the best CEOs: attracting and retaining top talent, mitigating risk, and 
maximizing revenue under any condition, ” says Skinner. “This alone 
should alert and inspire any transactional payroll function to embrace a 
transformative attitude. ”
Another key challenge is managing pay equity and transparency (38%), 
alongside maintaining an efficient payroll technology stack (36%). 
This underscores the growing emphasis on equitable compensation 
practices and the need for scalable, integrated payroll systems.
To stay competitive and compliant, organizations must focus on 
regulatory updates, optimizing payroll technology, and addressing pay 
equity.
Differences based on organization size
The top challenge in small and midsize organizations is paying 
competitively in today's market while large organizations are most likely 
to grapple with managing geographical differences (e.g., global payroll).

Finding: Technology integration and cost 
remain top challenges to payroll processes   
The top challenges organizations face with payroll processes include a 
lack of technology integration (43%), the high cost of payroll technology 
systems (41%), and inconsistent or minimal automation (33%). The cost 
of payroll technologies and lack of technology integration have figured 
among the top challenges to payroll processes over the last two years. 
Other top challenges are related to data management and reporting 
(31%) and inflexible payroll systems (31%).
“For all the grandstanding in the marketplace, it turns out that vendors 
may still have a long way to go in terms of delivering on the promise 
of modern software for payroll, ” says Skinner. “It could instead be that 
most respondents’ systems are in need of replacement with something 
more modern. Regardless, for transformative payroll functions to 
increase in their numbers, payroll technology must be up to the task. ”
These challenges suggest that organizations need to invest in 
integrated payroll technology, increase automation, and improve data 
management to streamline their processes and enhance efficiency. 
Addressing the cost of payroll systems and ensuring flexibility will help 
reduce inefficiencies while maintaining focus on compliance and tax-
related issues remains important.
Differences based on organization size 
Small and midsize organizations are most likely to cite the cost 
of payroll technology systems as their top challenge while large 
organizations are most likely to say lack of data management and 
reporting is their top challenge.
