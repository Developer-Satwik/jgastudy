0079: Suggested minimum rates
0080: What are the CIEP’s suggested minimum rates?
0081: These rates are an indication of the fees that a freelance editorial professional, with training in the core skills and some experience, could charge for their work.
0082: They take into consideration the costs that a freelance editorial professional must pay, including:
0083: holidays and sickness absence
0084: National Insurance (or equivalent)
0085: pension provision
0086: continuing professional development (CPD)
0087: office space and utility bills
0088: software and subscriptions
0089: business equipment and supplies.
0090: This means they are not directly comparable with employee remuneration.
0091: We have been publishing suggested minimum rates since 2001. View our archive (scroll down to the bottom of the page to find the button).
0092: What are the limitations of the CIEP’s suggested minimum rates?
0093: The CIEP does not have the authority to recommend or tell people what they should charge or pay. There will be a range of factors that affect an editorial professional’s rates, including:
0094: what needs to be done with the text, with a greater level of intervention attracting a higher fee
0095: how quickly the job needs to be done, with a quick turnaround attracting a higher fee
0096: the specialism of the editor or proofreader, with professionals with a relevant specialism being able to command a higher fee
0097: the experience of the editor or proofreader, with more experienced professionals being able to command a higher fee
0098: the format of the text and therefore how easy or difficult it will be to work with
0099: market forces.
0100: Every job is different, every editorial professional has different skills and knowledge, and ultimately price is a business negotiation between the parties.
0101: Some editorial professionals charge more, sometimes considerably more, than the rates below – always, or in certain conditions. Some charge less – always, or in certain conditions.
