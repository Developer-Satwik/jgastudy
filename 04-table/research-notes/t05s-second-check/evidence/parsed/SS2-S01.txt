From humble beginnings to world-leading research firm, Forrester has grown its influence by guiding companies through era-defining shifts. Here’s a look at some of the pivotal moments along our four-decade journey.
 2026 
September 8 
Navigating The GTM Singularity 
To help B2B leaders adapt to changing buyer behavior and AI-driven disruption, Forrester introduces a dedicated B2B Go-To-Market stream of research, frameworks, and guidance.
 Learn More 
March 12 
Forrester Where You Work 
Forrester AI becomes available in Microsoft Teams, bringing our insights and advice into clients’ everyday workflows. Similar integrations with Microsoft Copilot, Claude, and Slack become available later in the year.
 Request A Demo 
January 28 
Forrester Events Evolve 
To complement our flagship Summits, we introduce shorter, more intimate Forums designed for greater flexibility and deeper engagement. We debut the format with our North America CX events.
 Learn More 
 2025 
September 9 
Fast, Trusted Insights For The Organization 
Forrester announces AI Access, a self-service platform that equips organizations with Forrester’s trusted insights and advice so they can take action fast. AI Access is the first AI-led research service offered by a major research firm.
 Request a demo 
August 4 
Securing The Agentic Enterprise 
Anticipating the growing challenge of managing autonomous agents, we introduce the Agentic AI Enterprise Guardrails for Information Security (AEGIS) framework.
 Explore AEGIS 
June 24 
Redefining The Total Experience 
Forrester unveils its new Total Experience Score for measuring the power of a unified brand and customer experience. Our research has shown that improving brand equity in tandem with CX lifts revenue by up to 3.5x.
 Learn More 
April 1 
Defining A B2B Paradigm Shift 
At B2B Summit North America, we unveil our inaugural research on buying networks — complex webs of relationships encompassing decision makers and influencers within and outside of buyers’ organizations. Subsequent Forrester research explores various facets of these networks.
 Learn More 
 2024 
 “What AI can do may seem magical now, but soon it’s going to seem commonplace. Just as we couldn’t have imagined the impact that ridesharing apps like Uber would have on cities and transportation, we’re not imagining all the ways that AI will change things.” 
 Senior Analyst Rowan Curran speaking to Newsweek 
September 10 
Turn Trusted Data Into Business Value 
To help leaders harness data to fuel better decision-making and build a foundation for scalable AI, we announce our new Forrester Decisions for Data, AI & Analytics service.
 Learn More 
September 4 
Research And Continuous Guidance 
Forrester announces its unique research and continuous guidance model, distinguishing its approach from traditional library research models and consulting firms. The research and continuous guidance model is anchored in clients’ initiatives and desired outcomes, predicated on long-term partnership, and accelerated via access to Izola, now available to all Forrester clients.
 Learn More 
January 16 
Ramp Up Business Results Through Tech 
Forrester introduces its high-performance IT research to help companies drive growth and gain efficiencies by aligning technology capabilities with their unique business needs.
 Learn More 
 2023 
October 18 
Forrester AI Debuts 
Forrester launches Forrester AI, a generative AI tool that allows users to query Forrester’s research and get a clear, synthesized answer via a simple chat interface, to a select group of Forrester clients. Forrester AI was previously named Izola after Izola Forrester, a pioneer female journalist and founder and CEO George Colony’s grandmother.
 Meet Forrester AI 
July 23 
Forresterites Give Back 
To celebrate our 40th anniversary, we kick off a worldwide effort to support organizations focused on food insecurity. Hundreds of employees in North America, Europe, and Asia Pacific sort, pack, and distribute food, and many contributed funds to those organizations.
June 
Generative AI 
CEO George Colony issues a clarion call for companies to explore generative AI, predicting its widespread transformational impact. Forrester began writing on the impact of this type of technology years earlier — for instance, through our research on “TuringBots” that will learn to code enterprise applications.
February 
Driving Customer-Obsessed Growth 
We publish a series of research on building a customer-obsessed growth engine — a perpetual business motion designed to grow revenue, profit, and customer retention. To build this engine, we note, companies must “relentlessly drive internal alignment” and “focus on maximizing customer value.”
 Build Your Growth Engine 
January 18 
Rewarding Customer Obsession 
Forrester announces its inaugural global Customer Obsession Awards to recognize companies and senior executives who center all that they do on customers. The award is given in two categories: Customer-Obsessed Enterprise and Customer-Obsessed Leadership.
 2022 
October 18 
Green Market Revolution 
To help business, marketing, and technology leaders capitalize on the historic business opportunity that sustainability presents, we launch our Green Market Revolution research, which we continue to actively build upon.
 Explore The Hub 
June 7 
Accelerating Digital Excellence 
We announce our Forrester Decisions for Digital Business & Strategy research service to help digital leaders create and deliver businesswide digital strategies to boost customer acquisition and profitability.
 Read More 
May 2–4 
Return To In-Person Events 
B2B Summit North America 2022 brings approximately 2,000 registrants and Forresterites to Austin, Texas. It is our first in-person event since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic.
April 
CX Index Goes To Washington 
The White House begins using Forrester’s Customer Experience Index (CX Index™) to publicly evaluate its customer experience efforts. Forrester is the only provider of a tool that captures perceptions of the quality of experiences citizens have with the federal government. The President’s Management Agenda directly references the federal government’s use of the CX Index as a benchmarking tool and tracks its performance on the index.
January 
Best Place To Work 
For the fifth time in six years, Forrester is honored with a Glassdoor Employees’ Choice Award, recognizing the Best Places to Work in 2022.
 Read More 
 2021 
May 4 
B2B Revenue Waterfall™ 
To help B2B companies accelerate revenue growth, we unveil our B2B Revenue Waterfall™ at B2B Summit North America. The B2B Revenue Waterfall is the latest iteration of the highly influential SiriusDecisions Demand Unit Waterfall®, which helped cement the concept of “buying groups” into the B2B collective consciousness.
 Learn More 
May 3 
Forrester Decisions 
We launch our flagship research product portfolio, Forrester Decisions, bringing together bold vision research, tools and frameworks, and hands-on guidance to help clients solve their most pressing business challenges. To celebrate the milestone, Forrester CEO and Chairman George Colony rings the Nasdaq opening bell virtually.
 Read More 
 2020 
April 
Events Go Virtual 
Shortly into a global pandemic, we pivot to virtual events, beginning with CX Sydney and followed closely by B2B Summit North America and CX North America. We hold a total of nine successful virtual events in 2020, defying expectations of what’s possible.
 1/
 4 
 2019 
January 
“The New Forrester” 
Forrester joins forces with SiriusDecisions in our largest acquisition to date. The acquisition adds a powerful dimension to our offerings, combining forward-looking research with actionable tools and frameworks to operationalize the insights.
 Read More 
 2017 
February 24 
What It Means 
Our flagship podcast, “What It Means,” debuts. The name reflects Forrester’s long tradition of explaining the implications of our research findings for our clients and for society. In our first episode, Vice President James McQuivey discusses the science of emotion and the implications for companies.
 https://go.forrester.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/What-It-Means-Episode-1-James-McQuivey-FINAL.mp3 
 2015 
November 3 
Customer Obsession 
Recognizing that “technology and economic forces have changed the world to such a great extent that an obsession with winning, serving, and retaining customers is the only possible response,” we release our report, “The Operating Model For Customer Obsession.” The report, based on an analysis of over 30 companies at various stages in their customer-obsessed transformations, details the fundamental reset of day-to-day operations needed to drive customer obsession. The report has become the bedrock of our customer-obsession guidance to clients.
 2014 
June 
Mobile Mind Shift 
To help businesses meet customers’ expectations for immediacy in the mobile era, we publish the book “The Mobile Mind Shift.” It is the latest in a series of Forrester books analyzing the impact of technology and empowered consumers on business.
 2011 
August 29 
Going Green 
We move in to our current corporate headquarters, a LEED-Green-certified building at Cambridge Discovery Park. At 190,000 square feet and home to 500 employees, it is a mere two miles, but light-years away, from George Colony’s first basement home office.
June 
The Age Of The Customer 
Our report “Competitive Strategy In The Age Of The Customer” heralds a new era in business. While previous eras were defined by expertise in manufacturing, distribution, or information, “the new power of customers means that a focus on the customer now matters more than any other strategic imperative,” the report notes.
May 
APAC Expansion 
The acquisition of Singapore-based Springboard Research bolsters Forrester’s presence in the Asia Pacific region and advances our commitment to serving tech industry leaders.
 2010 
September 
Zero Trust 
Our first report on the Zero Trust security model launches. Now an industry standard, the Zero Trust concept centers on the belief that trust is a vulnerability and security must be designed with the mindset, “verify and never trust.”
 Learn More 
 2008 
July 
Serving Marketers 
The acquisition of JupiterResearch bolsters our offerings for marketing and strategy professionals. Through the acquisition, Forrester gained “some of the most prominent and prolific voices in the analysis of social media and technology’s effects on consumers,” wrote Forrester alum Cliff Condon.
 2007 
November 
Measuring CX 
Our first Customer Experience Index (today often shortened to CX Index™) is published. For our first index, we asked nearly 5,000 consumers about their interactions with a range of companies, gauging the usefulness, usability, and enjoyment of those experiences. The index methodology has since evolved; today’s methodology is reflected in the graphic above.
 Learn More 
 2003 
March 
Tech Expertise 
Forrester acquires the global technology advisory firm Giga Information Group, complementing Forrester’s emerging tech expertise. Employees who came from the acquisition are still with Forrester today.
 Read More 
 2002 
November 
Predicting Offshoring 
We foresee a large-scale movement of US services industry jobs to other countries as companies look to lower their costs. Our research brief, headlined “3.3 Million US Services Jobs To Go Offshore,” garners significant media attention — so much so that in 2004, report author John McCarthy revises his projections upward and notes that widespread publicity over the cost savings linked to offshoring may have hastened the trend.
 Learn More 
November 
Advising Governments 
CEO George Colony is invited to the White House to help inform how to set up the IT organization for the newly created US Department of Homeland Security.
July 
Catch The Wave 
The first Forrester Wave™ appears in the report, “Grading Apps For Inventory And Order Visibility.” The Wave, which guides buyers in their technology purchase options, is a rigorous vendor selection analysis based on a transparent methodology. 
Spring 
Forecasting The Cloud 
Our reports on organic IT offer an early glimpse into cloud computing. They foretell a future in which IT infrastructure is built on cheap, redundant components that share and manage enterprise computing.
 Learn More 
 2001 
September 
Trying Times 
President and Chief Operating Officer Bill Bluestein dies unexpectedly at age 44, one week before the 9/11 terrorist attacks. A former analyst, Bluestein brought an inquisitive, sophisticated, and rigorous approach to research that set an enduring standard. He instilled the belief in analysts that their role was one of great responsibility and privilege. (Hear him speak in the video below.) Each year since his death, Forrester has presented the Bill Bluestein award to an analyst whose work is particularly provocative, courageous, and inspirational.
May 
Westward Expansion 
Our San Francisco office opens, Forrester’s first US office outside of Cambridge. The location enabled analysts to better serve Silicon Valley and Bay Area internet companies.
January 
Business Insights 
The launch of Business Technographics® enables us to equip our clients with data-driven insights to understand the changing B2B tech buyer.
 Read More 
 2000 
Spring 
Hands-On Guidance 
We launch our consulting practice, the most personalized way clients can apply Forrester’s deep body of research, data-driven insights, and best practices to their business challenges.
April 
The Dot-Com Demise 
Our report “The Demise Of Dot-Com Retailers” foresees the impending dot-com crash. The report accurately predicts that many dot-coms are in weak positions and that many won’t survive the year.
 Read More 
 “Amazon will thrive, but only a handful of dot-coms will survive.” 
 The Demise Of Dot-Com Retailers 
 April 2000 
February 
Secondary Public Offering 
We announce a secondary public offering of over 626,000 shares.
 1998 
September 
Kicking Off CX 
Our first customer experience (CX) report, “Why Most Websites Fail,” is published. The report, which began with the sentence at right, introduced the first version of Forrester’s website review methodology.
 Read The Report 
 Usability goals are business goals. 
April 
Going Global 
Our first major European office opens in Amsterdam. During 1998, we host six Forrester Forums worldwide, reflecting our growing influence outside of the US.
 1997 
Summer 
Consumer Data 
Survey fielding begins for what will be the first Consumer Technographics®. Consumer Technographics is now the longest-running and most comprehensive program examining how consumers consider, buy, and use technology. Only the US Census Bureau surveys more consumer households on technology adoption and attitudes.
May 
Hot Growth 
Businessweek ranks Forrester number 13 on its “Hot Growth” list. An accompanying headline describes us as “sassy, quirky, and rich.”
 1996 
November 
Going Public 
We announce our initial public offering of 2.3 million shares.
 Read More 
 1993 
Future Vision 
Two particularly prescient reports provide a glimpse into the future: “The New Public Internet” predicts that the internet will become a household tool, while “Defining Social Computing” foresees a technology-enabled future in which “people will converse more broadly, electronic ‘communities’ will be created, and text, pictures, and sounds will be woven into conversations.” Soon after, we created a New Media Research group to analyze website operations and new internet-based technologies.
 1989 
Big Names 
Bill Gates and Steve Jobs speak at our annual conference in Boston, signaling Forrester’s growing prominence in the technology space.
 1987 
Bold Calls 
We predict the fall of the mainframe and the rise of PC-based client/server computer systems. Our prescience around client/server computing — which we defined as “desktop computers cooperating with computers known as servers to do a job” — boosted Forrester’s profile and authority as the trend took off in the 1990s.

 1983 
First Clients 
Air Products and Chemicals and IBM sign on as Forrester’s first clients.
July 7 
The Beginning 
George Forrester Colony starts his eponymous company in the basement of his home at 20 Cambridge Terrace in North Cambridge, Massachusetts. He initially considered “Wave Associates” as the name for the new company but, persuaded by a friend, chose to go with his middle name.
