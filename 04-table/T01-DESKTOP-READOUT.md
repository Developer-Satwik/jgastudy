# The Table: first comparable-model readout

Internal desktop review, 29 October 2026. Six selected models have been researched and integrated. No buyer/member contribution, financial forecast or launch recommendation has been completed.

## What the comparison changes

The Table needs a clear exchange before it needs a premium venue. The comparables support three separate design questions: what a research buyer can use, what a funding partner receives, and why a senior practitioner would contribute and return. A dinner can support these exchanges, but its incremental value still needs evidence.

1. **Make the research useful for a named decision.** Broad payroll research and partner-supported reports are already visible publicly. A possible paid distinction is bounded original decision cases, practical evidence requirements and a useful briefing. This is a hypothesis to test against the buyer's actual free and internal alternatives, not an established market gap. CLM-0494, CLM-0495, CLM-0496.
2. **Define rights alongside the output.** ISG distinguishes research access from provider marketing licences. The Payroll Centre and Conference Board also restrict reuse. Reading a report, circulating it internally, excerpting it publicly and reselling it are different permissions. No third-party terms are copied into JGA's contract by this comparison.
3. **Design a reason to return.** Support memberships and research communities offer continuing help, interpretation or peer exchange. The implication is to cost and test a useful continuing output; it is not to promise a payroll helpdesk or a large analyst programme JGA has not staffed.
4. **Choose how commercial participation works.** iVentiv explicitly markets meetings/contact access. That is a different exchange from a founding research partnership. The Table should not promise confidential independent research and default sponsor access to private inputs or identities in the same breath. CLM-0497.
5. **Compare the event with a lower-burden route.** CPO demonstrates a continuing online payroll peer format, with distinct free editorial content. Its forum eligibility/fee is not fully public and provider-side leaders are present. The Table's proposed employer-practitioner mix, confidential contribution and UK gathering therefore require their own definition and evidence.

## What we can and cannot conclude

The observed offers show several publicly offered product architectures in the wider payroll/HR ecosystem. They do not demonstrate that JGA can recruit the intended members, sell licences, secure a partner, earn a margin or fund four cities. The one observed Corporate membership list price is GBP 1,122 per organisation/year, with business terms excluding VAT unless stated otherwise; it includes materially different services. No comparator price has become a JGA input.

Currentness is explicit: HR.com's accessible custom-research help article dates to 2021 and its linked main offer was unavailable; the payroll example and ISG guide are 2025 editions; CPO's forum launch was reported in 2025; iVentiv London's17–18 November 2026 event was forthcoming on review. The existing CIPP catalogue edition-label discrepancy remains. Promotional outcomes, retention figures and testimonial enthusiasm are not adopted as verified economics.
