# The Table design brief to investigate

This records Nick's proposed concept as research input. It is not an approved event booking, confirmed membership list or predetermined recommendation to launch.

## Concept

The Table Global Payroll Leadership Council is intended as an invitation-only senior practitioner community. The proposed annual format is four premium private dining research events, initially considering London, Manchester, Dublin and an undecided Texas city. Each is intended for 12–15 different Payroll Directors, Global Heads of Payroll or equivalent senior leaders from major organisations.

The aspiration is an exceptional professional experience, potentially Michelin-starred or comparable private dining. Suitability also requires sufficient total seats including hosts/moderation, privacy, acoustics, accessibility, dietary provision, travel practicality and proportionate hospitality under members' employer rules. Premium does not automatically mean suitable.

Practitioners would attend free, receive the research free and optionally be recognised as council members. Each would contribute one industry question; an independent research process would structure those alongside a consistent core. Individual responses would remain confidential, while appropriate aggregate findings could be published and licensed.

The first commissioned report gives the concept an initial research asset. The events are a possible mechanism for deeper future insight and community, not a prerequisite for this inaugural report's completion. A different group at each event produces changing cohorts, not a longitudinal panel. The annual arithmetic of 48–60 places is a proposed capacity, not a recruited or representative sample.

## Questions the feasibility work must resolve

1. Does the research itself improve a buyer's real decision enough to merit paying despite free substitutes?
2. What original evidence and practical tools can distinguish the first edition?
3. What continuing benefits would make a community useful beyond a dinner?
4. Does an event add research depth, member engagement or commercial value beyond less expensive remote/written approaches?
5. Can confidential contribution, editorial independence and partner benefits coexist without promising private lead access?
6. Can JGA recruit, host, research and sell within a reasonable workload and funded cost envelope?
7. What must be demonstrated in the UK before international expansion?

## Products to keep distinct

- Research licence: defined report/briefing and specific internal or external reuse rights.
- Founding research partnership: funding, disclosed recognition, defined aggregate outputs and rights; no ability to suppress unwelcome findings.
- Council participation: research contribution, peer value and appropriate recognition, distinct from individual response data.
- Later bespoke intelligence or advisory: potential future work, excluded from the initial delivery unless separately agreed.

Do not describe sponsorship as recurring contracted revenue merely because the programme is annual. Do not promise sponsors unrestricted respondent contacts or attribute all research sales to dinners.

## Pilot decision path

Start with a quote-ready one-city UK pilot specification. Validate audience appeal and a defined partner use case; establish realistic all-in costs, responsible owners and funding timing; then set go/no-go criteria before any non-refundable venue or supplier commitment.

The recommendation may be research-only first, revised event design, a funded pilot, or pause. Threshold values will be derived from the model and agreed risk appetite rather than invented now. No sponsor prospecting, venue enquiry, expenditure or event delivery is included in this planning pass.
