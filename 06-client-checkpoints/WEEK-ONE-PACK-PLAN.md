## Purpose

Give Nick and Mark a concise, concrete set of materials to approve before production is far advanced. Nick can use the preview to get reactions from relevant practitioners and commercial contacts, as requested.

## Contents

1. Research brief and proposed report outline, reader decisions, main population/geography and rationale for the chosen focus.
2. Core practitioner question map and routed guide, with the analysis each question supports.
3. Participant introduction brief: eligibility, desired variation, estimated burden once tested, optional modes and permission expectations.
4. Short sample/prospectus: value proposition, planned sections and an honestly labelled evidence-based sample, with no invented practitioner quotes or findings.
5. Separate buyer/member validation questions for The Table, with the proposed commercial product definitions.
6. Short decisions/input sheet: focus, format/length, contacts, brand assets, data/collection arrangements and confirmed working dates.

## Rules

Keep the pack readable and internally consistent. It is not the full final report or an excuse to require Nick to perform the research. Ask only for decisions and inputs his team actually needs to supply. Use a short cover message after user review; do not auto-send.

The early sample/prospectus may later become the two-page commercial preview, revised to reflect actual findings. Preserve shared versions and feedback so the final package matches what was approved.

## Feedback record

For each response: date, respondent role, pack version, exact concern or request, effect on questions or scope, and decision. Interest is useful input, not a sale. Material additions beyond the contract require explicit agreement rather than silent scope drift.
