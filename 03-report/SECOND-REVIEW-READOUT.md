# Second review of the JGA report design

28 October 2026. Prepared for Satwik. Internal analysis before anything is sent to Nick.

## Main judgment

Keep the agreed report, the eight-chapter structure and the 14 core questions. The research direction remains useful, but the second review uncovered two material gaps in the draft: needs that never become proposals, and whether an approved change actually produces its intended benefit. Close these through routing and selective probes, rather than add another survey or more chapters.

The strongest product is a report explaining which needs remain unresolved, why different organisations make different decisions, and what employers and suppliers can do about the specific barrier. It should not become a generic argument that payroll needs more technology or investment. Both substantial report parts and the From Ambition to Investment conclusion remain essential.

The review used the accepted correspondence, agreement, R01 source/claim records, the draft checkpoint and fresh primary sources. Four parallel lenses covered history, economics, methods and JGA's own positioning/policy context. Twenty-seven candidate source records were reconciled to the canonical system, adding 26 distinct routes after de-duplication. These include reports, guidance, individual cases, commercial product descriptions and JGA material. They are not 27 independent surveys, and no new practitioner evidence was collected.

## The historical story we can support

Use a short timeline of recurring questions and changed conditions, not a numerical trend curve.

- **2020 and 2022:** CIPP originals document JGA support and Nick's contributions. They already discuss automation, education and strategic payroll. These are dated survey findings and attributed commentary, not proof subsequent predictions occurred. [2020 original](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/903316/), printed pp13–14; [2022 original](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/1017671823/), pp9–10.
- **2024:** CIPP distinguishes technology labels and shows that no change can coexist with satisfaction. Question wording and bases differ from earlier studies. Do not compare an expectation of future automation with a later current-use percentage as if they measured the same thing. [Technology discussion](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/706203142/4/), [software question](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/706203142/7/).
- **2025 and 2026:** The inspected recent sources add operational handoffs, buying evidence, business cases and delivery conditions. They support more concrete questions; they do not establish that barriers have risen or fallen across the industry. [PayrollOrg 2025 original](https://info.payroll.org/pdfs/global/Global-Payroll-Week-2025-survey-results.pdf), method p3 and discussion pp12–14.
- **2027 and 2028:** The inspected HMRC interim guidance describes phased benefits payrolling. Treat this as a dated planning signal, rechecked before publication. It does not predict employer readiness, supplier revenue or required system replacement. [Current phasing chapter](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/draft-guidance-and-legislation-to-aid-preparation-for-reporting-benefits-in-kind-in-real-time/the-phased-introduction-of-mandatory-payrolling-for-benefits-in-kind).

The valuable historical question is: which expectations became useful operational change, which remained ambitions, and what explains the difference? The sources do not justify an industry-wide claim that payroll has failed to progress.

## The two gaps to fix before sending

### Needs that never become proposals

A contributor may have a significant unmet need but no investment decision in the recall window. The present route preserves their priorities but can miss why the need was never put forward. Add one optional probe after the no-case answer: has an improvement mentioned been raised internally, and what happened or prevented it being raised?

Only a never-raised need is a pre-proposal observation. If this probe uncovers a qualifying recent case, confirm it and resume questions 7–13. Older proposals or decisions remain separately labelled context; preserve uncertainty if timing or involvement is unclear. Do not code a need as rejected, abandoned or unfunded unless the account supports that description. Not evaluating the market is not evidence that no supplier can meet the need.

Classify an explanation only when supported: not known to be available; available but unsuitable; considered unaffordable or value unproven; insufficient readiness, skills, data or ownership; not evaluated; no material shortfall. Allow overlapping causes and retain uncertainty. A participant's belief is not a market audit.

### Benefits after approval

The draft asks whether delivery matched the agreement but does not explicitly establish what success meant. Add selective probes for intended outcome, a baseline/measure where known, who benefits and who tracks the result. For released capacity, ask where it went. Operational go-live, improved control and spending reductions are different outcomes.

The [Government Efficiency Framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-government-efficiency-framework/the-government-efficiency-framework--2) provides useful distinctions, adapted proportionately. It is public-sector guidance, not proof of payroll savings or a private-employer reporting obligation. The [Green Book 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026) similarly supports separating value, affordability and delivery feasibility. We should not impose its full process or invent a score.

## Other refinements worth making

1. **Recent milestones in older programmes.** A substantive approval, renewal, re-scope or go-live decision inside 24 months can qualify even if the programme started earlier. Record original start, selected milestone and dates separately. An older case with no recent milestone remains contextual.
2. **Investment beyond software.** Include people, training, retained management work and support in existing case-selection probes. This stays within transformation research; it does not turn into a recruitment report.
3. **Actual service boundaries.** For outsourced/mixed delivery, identify work retained by the employer, information supplied to the provider and responsibilities across handoffs. Outsourced is not a complete description of the operating model.
4. **Task-level technology.** When AI or automation arises, clarify the actual workflow, pilot versus live use, exception review and observed result. Avoid making terminology knowledge a respondent test.
5. **Preferred versus constrained renewal.** Ask whether staying was selected on merit, constrained by transition or contract timing, or both. Do not presume lock-in or universal dissatisfaction.
6. **Trigger and conditions.** Distinguish a mandatory deadline, lifecycle decision and discretionary improvement after the unaided answer. Ask what condition would move a priority forward or make it less important.
7. **Supplier response.** Derive a practical response from the diagnosed barrier, such as clearer evidence, transition support, service scope or ownership. A missing feature is only one possibility. Proposed remedies remain interpretations to test, not promises that a supplier action will secure approval.

These are a menu of clarifiers, not a requirement to read every probe. Prioritise the pre-proposal route, intended outcome/baseline and observed result. Test timing before making a participant-duration promise. Preserve the proposed core and version later material changes.

## More useful analytical outputs within the agreed scope

| Output | What it helps the reader do | Evidence discipline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs and barriers map | Distinguish what is wanted from the actual reason it remains unresolved. | Separate perceived market gaps, internal conditions and untested availability. No automatic league table. |
| Decision-case summaries | Understand context, trigger, intended value, authority, alternatives, constraints, decision, delivery and result. | Use permissioned cases; keep measured evidence separate from recollection and attribution. |
| Payroll Investment Decision Framework | Identify what evidence or action is missing before the next decision. | Build it from supported patterns and contrary cases. It is a reusable tool, not a prediction model. |
| Conditional outlook | Revisit a decision when an important condition changes. | Show the dated signal, uncertainty, possible implication and next check; no invented probabilities. |

For a future-facing table, the format can be simple: “If this condition changes, reconsider this decision, and check this evidence.” For example, a reporting requirement may change data-flow readiness needs; a contract renewal window may change transition options; approved automation may release capacity without reducing spend. These are possible decision conditions, can overlap and are not forecasts.

No extra chapters, new survey, formal foresight workshops or additional interview quota are needed. Avoid decorative graphs that suggest an adoption trend or market share unsupported by comparable data. Charts can show sound published measures with their actual bases; original qualitative structure is often better shown as a decision pathway or labelled case matrix.

## JGA alignment and research independence

The strongest alignment with JGA is its access to senior payroll experience and existing discussion of leadership, skills and change. The current report can turn those broad themes into evidence readers can use when making decisions. It should remain open to evidence that a process fix, adequate incumbent or no immediate purchase is appropriate.

The 20 October 2026 [PayrollOrg announcement](https://payroll.org/news-resources/news/news-detail/2026/10/20/payrollorg-partners-with-jga-ventures-to-offer-ai-program-for-payroll-leaders) is relevant commercial context. It is not evidence that the programme works or that the report should recommend it. Describe JGA commissioning and preserve editorial independence from recruitment, training and partner preferences.

JGA's network is the agreed introduction route, but it can select for particular relationships and experiences. Seek a few meaningful contrasts in operating model, decision outcome and organisational context. Record actual participation and disagreement; six to ten people cannot fill a credible matrix of countries, sectors and company sizes. Ask how the participant knows what they describe, rather than treating role verification as verification of every claim.

## The Table should pass a separate value test

The financial-model specification already separates cash, staff time, bundled rights, funding timing and adverse scenarios. Keep it. The additional question is whether the event adds enough research, member or buyer value to justify its incremental cost and work.

Research usefulness, purchase demand, council appeal and hospitality appeal are different. Potential buyers must identify a concrete decision, what they already use, required evidence and rights, approval/budget ownership and a real next step. Two positive buyer conversations cannot establish a conversion rate or proven price.

If a public/free version and a paid version coexist, define exactly what is being licensed. Internal use, external reuse, custom briefing, partnership recognition and access to confidential identities are not interchangeable. Do not manufacture scarcity around a PDF available to everyone or add bespoke work to the agreed licence product without assessing delivery cost. Final packaging and pricing still belong to the commercial assessment.

Council-member interest counts toward the practitioner target only when an eligible, permissioned report contribution is actually supplied. The original scope remains 6–10 distinct practitioners, up to four Table conversations and six comparable models. No additional collection is proposed here.

## What is established and what remains to test

Established for design: the themes are already well covered; JGA has relevant published history; the draft separates approval and delivery sensibly; primary guidance supports more careful benefit and scenario treatment; the new routing gap is visible in the instrument itself.

Interpretations worth testing: classifying different gap mechanisms will make the framework more useful; concrete cases may add commercial value beyond free surveys; event involvement may or may not improve the research product.

Still requiring actual work: the practitioner priorities and cases; credible result measures; variation in the cohort; buyer use and willingness to pay; viable licence rights/prices; event economics and funding; the final framework. More desk research cannot substitute for those observations or make a market hypothesis certain.

Previously recorded CIPP base issues, Moorepay conflicts, Finity wording differences and HR.com chart/prose differences remain held. New material has not silently resolved them. General guidance and one public programme cannot become typical private-employer benchmarks.

## Recommendation before sharing

Hold the existing version 0.1 pack as a review draft. Apply the proposed refinements to the internal guide and a new version 0.2 only after this review is assessed; preserve version 0.1. Keep the client-facing pack compact and explain the sharper focus plainly. No need to send Nick the internal challenge logs or all source candidates.
