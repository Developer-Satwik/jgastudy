## Research questions

1. What actual task changes through process/configuration, integration, rule-based automation, analytical/AI support, software replacement or a delivery-model change?

3. What is advertised, planned, piloted, live, independently observed and measured?
4. What do successful, mixed and adverse cases reveal without implying market prevalence?
5. Which adequate controls or arrangements should be preserved, and when is no replacement a reasonable option?

## Findings and implications

Integration must be evaluated at the actual handoff, including field/identity meaning, source ownership, timing, access, partial acceptance and recovery. Technical states do not demonstrate protected outcomes. Current HMRC transition/correction guidance supplies a concrete external dependency.

Delivery requires a component-level map of execution, authority and retained work. Processing payroll and making payments can have different owners. Software support, managed execution and employee-case resolution need their actual scope established.

Automation/AI analysis distinguishes task mechanism, availability, actual use and benefit assessment. Configurable controls do not become generative AI; a supplier availability statement does not become proven adoption or savings. Proposed evidence tests include meaningful intervention and continuing operational support.

Selected employer cases distinguish continuity/cutover, useful information, support, adoption and assessed benefits. Positive assurance is retained alongside mixed outcomes. Date, sample and scope limit each account; two public employers cannot supply a market success rate or comparative vendor ranking.

For payroll leaders, compare viable responses to the diagnosed need and preserve essential controls. For suppliers, demonstrate relevant handoffs, states, duties and exception closure. For JGA, keep development, specialist capacity, process, service and technology routes open to the evidence rather than presume a sale or a hire.

The second challenge makes continuing readiness, whole-work distribution, actual country/entity/service context, engagement/output ownership and recovery/dependence explicit. Individual capacity, project delivery and ongoing payroll execution differ; delegated capability can have benefits as well as dependencies. Prior constructed prompts must not lead the participant's unaided priorities.
