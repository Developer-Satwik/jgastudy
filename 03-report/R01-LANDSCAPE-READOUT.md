# Existing payroll research and the opportunity for JGA

28 October 2026 | Internal research design readout | R01

## Recommendation

Keep Nick's two-part report, but make its distinctive contribution the connection between a payroll need and a real organisational investment decision. Use established research to explain the wider context, then use JGA's original practitioner contributions to investigate the evidence, people, constraints and trade-offs that changed the outcome.

The reviewed landscape does not support claiming that payroll frustrations, AI, strategic value, investment barriers or buyer behaviour are unexplored. Those subjects already have substantial coverage. JGA can still create a useful product by offering sharper senior-practitioner case analysis and a practical decision framework. That is a focused value hypothesis, not a proven unique market position or proof buyers will pay.

## The contribution to investigate

We should reconstruct a recent real decision rather than ask only what someone wants in future.

1. What triggered attention, and what consequence made it matter?
2. Which outcome was desired, and what was already working adequately?
3. Who sponsored the change, controlled budget, approved it or could block it?
4. Which evidence was requested, and which evidence actually affected the decision?
5. What alternatives were considered, including renewal, process improvement and no change?
6. What cost, implementation, data, integration or continuity condition became non-negotiable?
7. What was approved, deferred, rejected, abandoned, implemented or retained?
8. What differed between the initial ambition and the funded or delivered result?

The final question set belongs to the next design stage. These are evidence requirements, not claims that interviews have occurred or final questionnaire wording has been approved.

## Four promising value hypotheses

**Evidence that changes the decision.** Develop comparable case summaries of what persuaded an approver or stopped a case. Useful to practitioners preparing investment proposals and suppliers supporting an evaluation. Test whether participants can describe a real evidence request and its effect rather than a general preference.

**The gap between desired, funded and delivered.** Keep these stages separate instead of combining them into an adoption statistic. Test whether a desired capability was unfunded, approved but delayed, implemented differently or consciously deprioritised.

**Why staying may be rational.** Include successful implementation and sensible renewal, not only frustration. Test whether data readiness, transition risk, internal capacity or a sufficient incumbent offer made no change the better decision.

**A reusable Decision Framework.** Convert supported case patterns into a practical evidence checklist and decision pathway. The framework will be analytical, not a validated predictive score. Its usefulness must come from concrete questions and actions, not from calling it an index.

## Consequences for the intended readership

The strongest working primary audience remains senior employer-side payroll decision-makers. UK relevance with international responsibility is a proposal for Nick to review. Provider and vendor perspectives can inform the commercial interpretation but should not silently become employer evidence.

Our commercial hypothesis is that readers may value insight about a decision they need to influence, while generic statistics alone may struggle against existing free material. The Table's buyer conversations must test the specific product, evidence depth, rights and use case. Research novelty, reader usefulness and willingness to pay are three different questions.

With 6–10 purposively introduced practitioner contributors, seek contrasting outcomes and operating contexts. Do not promise representative sector or country rankings. The value is explanatory depth and practical application, supported by a clear method.

## Source issues that change our handling

- CIPP's viewed original reports identify the edition differently from some web labels. Their separate annual survey response count must not be borrowed as the sample size of the report series.
- Moorepay's switching and funding displays contain unresolved differences. We retain the evidence and withhold the affected figures from headline use.
- Finity uses different wording around delays across sections; HR.com's missing-feature prose and chart disagree on one figure. Those are recorded as conflicts, not silently corrected.
- Preference, confidence and intention do not establish actual error incidence, approved spend or realised adoption. Positive provider/operating experiences also occur in the inspected material.

These are internal evidence controls. The client publication should contain the sound, relevant conclusions and the context needed to interpret them, without unnecessary working logs.
