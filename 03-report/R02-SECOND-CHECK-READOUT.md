# R02 second challenge: what holds, what changes and why it matters

28 October 2026. Internal research synthesis. Covers historical, market, competitor, employer, employee, institutional, resilience and evidence-quality perspectives. No participant data or client approval is implied. Nothing has been sent to Nick.

## Conclusion

The nine-area needs map holds, with important refinements. It should explain the specific task, required outcome, gap, consequence and responsible boundary in an employer's context. The deeper evidence makes that analysis more useful than a general list of desired technology features or a claim that payroll suppliers do not meet the market's needs.

The most valuable additions are a wider ownership map, a clearer correction lifecycle, more precise capacity distinctions and explicit conditions for safe continuity. Employer size alone cannot explain the differences. Actual priorities and commercial materiality still need the original practitioner cases.

## Historical context that earns its place

The 2017 HMRC RTI programme review provides a positive overall modernisation account while describing difficult exceptions. CIPP's seven selected cases published in 2025 illustrate particular resolution mechanisms, including one reported software fix followed by manual correction of affected records. These are different evidence types, not a repeated national sample. [HMRC review](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/real-time-information-programme-post-implementation-review), [CIPP release and response](https://www.cipp.org.uk/resources/policy-report/systemic-issues-in-hmrc-rti-data-collection.html).

The report can therefore use a short dated context panel: longstanding ambitions; contemporary operating evidence; scheduled readiness conditions. It should not display a numerical improvement curve. Registered 2027/2028 benefits-in-kind preparation is a conditional planning context, to be rechecked at publication, rather than observed employer readiness or a spending forecast. Existing CIPP historical evidence and JGA's prior involvement remain attributed context.

The correction lifecycle is the most useful historical refinement:

1. Prevent new occurrences where the cause can be changed.
2. Correct previously affected records or balances.
3. Resolve the employee, employer-account or reporting consequence and confirm closure.

Progress at one stage does not establish completion of the others. This is a proposed analytical distinction supported by the selected mechanisms, not a validated classification or an extra collection exercise.

## Market evidence: availability is only the beginning

The [market comparison](research-notes/r02-second-check/market/MARKET-CHALLENGE.md) contrasts HMRC tools/recognition, Xero's software and pricing boundaries, ADP's managed UK and multicountry descriptions, and the already reviewed Fourth service definition. These illustrations test the gap explanation; they are not a complete supplier census.

Several broadly named capabilities are already advertised. The report should establish whether the employer can obtain the required outcome under its actual arrangement. Relevant explanations include unsuitable fit, excluded scope, affordability/value, data/readiness, skills, ownership, delivered service, unknown availability and an adequate present arrangement. They can overlap.

Keep a software licence/add-on, transaction charges, managed-service fees and retained internal work separate. A cheap/free licence does not establish zero operating cost. An enterprise service declaration does not establish affordability, country fit, entitlement or delivery for the case. HMRC reporting recognition is not a comparative quality endorsement.

Record context sparingly: pay frequency/variable work, entities/countries, execution model, retained duties and actual package. Broad headcount is useful context but cannot stand in for those conditions. The micro-employer example is a market boundary check; the proposed original cohort remains UK-relevant senior employer-side practitioners, subject to Nick's review.

## What the six recruitment comparators add

The [current primary comparison](research-notes/r02-second-check/competitors/COMPETITOR-CHALLENGE.md) covers JGA, Portfolio Payroll, Frazer Jones, Oakleaf Partnership, Middlemore, Hays and Robert Half. It separates recruitment, consultancy and payroll execution, and records current source access limits.

For Part One, distinguish four capacity contexts where the case supports them:

- Ongoing operational coverage, including ordinary replacement or growth.
- Temporary continuity cover while someone is absent or a permanent appointment is made.
- Specialist change work, with identifiable tasks, deliverables and retained operations.
- Accountable leadership and oversight, including authority over internal processes or providers.

Frazer Jones's published organisational-change case and Oakleaf's dated interim payroll/pensions vacancy provide concrete scope examples. They do not establish typical outcomes, market shortage or a completed hire. The remaining firms' current service declarations establish inspectable routes, not relative quality. [Frazer Jones case](https://www.frazerjones.com/case-studies/delivering-payroll-and-hr-operations-talent-during-organisational-change/), [Oakleaf request](https://www.oakleafpartnership.com/jobs/interim-payroll-and-pensions-manager-pr-016244).

The report's independence matters to JGA's credibility. A finding may support development, clearer authority, a process change, revised service scope, temporary expertise or no immediate change. It must not be written to make recruitment inevitable. Where temporary support is relevant, establish what knowledge and ownership must remain or pass back afterwards.
