# R05: evidence for a fundable and deliverable payroll proposal

29 October 2026. Internal desktop synthesis. Original practitioner integration and final publication review remain open. No employer forecast, price quotation, product test or original contribution has been produced.

## Main conclusion

A credible proposal connects a real need and feasible alternatives to relevant costs, benefits, funding and delivery evidence. It should explain what has to happen for the intended result, which assumptions could alter the choice, and who owns the next decision and benefit. A headline saving or positive return is insufficient on its own.

The practical output is a proportionate [business-case evidence checklist](R05-BUSINESS-CASE-CHECKLIST.md) and [structured map](R05-evidence-map.json). These build on R02 needs, R03's eight combinable response paths and R04's specific decision rights. They are analytical aids, not a validated investment score, compulsory employer process or nine extra interview questions.

## Three views that must stay distinct

| View | Question it answers | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-life resources/cost | What does this option consume across the relevant service, transition, operation and exit period? | Existing staff time and constrained expertise can matter without a new invoice. State what is included and whether any cost is allocated or discounted. |
| Future incremental cash appraisal | Which future cash payments/receipts change because of this choice, against a credible alternative? | Already-spent amounts and unchanged allocations are not automatically new cash outflows. Do not erase historical cost from accountability or ignore future cancellation/exit payments. |
| Funding and affordability | What money/resources are needed, when, and within whose actual remit? | An attractive forecast return does not establish available cash, protected delivery time or permission to commit it. |

CLM-0295–0302. Government social-appraisal definitions and educational cash-appraisal principles retain their own scopes. Social discount rates, transfer treatment, public thresholds and classroom conventions are not imported as private-employer policy. Tax/financing treatment must fit the actual perspective; no client tax or cost-of-capital advice is supplied.

### A realistic comparison, not a frozen world

The baseline may include expected renewal, maintenance, staffing or preparation work. Retaining or improving a supported incumbent can remain a strong option. Conversely, if an actual applicable requirement rules out unchanged operation, describing it as a comparison does not make it a feasible recommendation. Establish the requirement and compare credible ways to meet the necessary result. Necessity alone does not select an expensive replacement. CLM-0295–0297, CLM-0302.

## Benefits need a mechanism and a status

Distinguish a proposed feature, forecast effect, approved benefit plan, enabled capability, reported result and evidenced spending effect. These are different claims, not a maturity score or an inevitable progression.

- **Cash release:** an actual or forecast change in expenditure/receipts with its stated basis and timing.
- **Capacity/productivity:** useful time or output can improve without the budget falling. Valuing time at salary does not independently establish cash release.
- **Cost avoidance:** a credible future cost path and the action preventing it, not the same as an observed cut in current spending.
- **Quality, control, continuity or employee outcomes:** meaningful effects may remain in their own units or qualitative evidence. Do not invent a fine probability, reputational value or revenue effect to improve a ratio.

Use an appropriate baseline, owner, dependencies, measurement period and gross/net distinction. Avoid adding overlapping time, FTE and output claims as if independent. Planned savings must account for relevant implementation, recurring review and exception work. CLM-0298–0302.

Public guidance supports these distinctions; it is not evidence of an achieved payroll saving. A participant's account may establish what was forecast or observed without supplying confidential budgets. Unknown or undisclosed amounts remain unknown.

### Return measures answer different questions

Simple payback describes recovery timing; it can omit later cash flows and does not itself discount. NPV assesses forecast discounted incremental cash under a stated rate and assumptions. A reported ROI or accounting return needs its actual numerator, denominator, horizon and cash/profit basis before interpretation. No universal hurdle rate, payback period, cash-conversion factor or approval formula is imposed. CLM-0300–0302.

The AQA source's inconsistent worked example is not reused. Only the bounded conceptual distinctions are retained; no classroom amount, arithmetic or threshold becomes a client result.

The commercial checks illustrate cost structures rather than rank suppliers:

| --- | --- | --- |

| BrightPay billing and pricing interface | Employer/employee record counts and import/duplication can affect usage; an empty calculator needs inputs. | An empty zero output is not a free offer. Differing dated billing-period descriptions do not support an exact current invoice forecast. CLM-0304–0305. |
| Moorepay pricing page | Scope/enquiry and selected implementation/account-credit qualifications matter. | A usable rate card, full managed-service scope, verified savings, enforceability or automatic cash reimbursement. CLM-0306. |

Define the actual product/service component, country/entity, quantity, included work and chargeable extras where relevant. Include internal retained work, transition overlap, support and renewal/exit needs. This does not assert that every supplier charges every item. A public list structure is not a negotiated quote or an observed result. No price, saving percentage, market average or bill was adopted. CLM-0303–0308.

The live Sourcing Playbook supplies scoped whole-life, transition and exit design context. Its current capture is kept separate from older versions; it is not a private-company mandate or a source of approval mechanics here. CLM-0307.

## Deliverability is part of the proposition

The case needs enough evidence for the particular commitment or release, not an assurance label detached from scope. Proposed tests, available training and named teams differ from executed results, usable skills and protected time.

Relevant checks include scope/data, migration/integration, normal and exception cases, operating continuity, realistic change/BAU capacity, practical learning, support/handover and dependencies. If parallel comparison is part of the actual plan, establish its coverage, differences and acceptance. No fixed number of runs, payroll-error tolerance, recovery target or compulsory migration gate is prescribed. CLM-0309–0318.

The inspected public evidence preserves contrasting states:

- **Synergy:** planned sequencing, capability and contingencies are a prospective management case. They are not completed rehearsals or payroll-only benefits. CLM-0312.
- **NAO shared services:** risk information needs owner, mitigation and interaction context. The audit expressly did not replicate or assure the published business-case figures. Its March 2026 publication cannot be treated as a later assessment of the April-published Synergy summary. CLM-0313–0314.
- **Scottish Government:** the audit reports controlled-cutover and sampled-pay positives alongside practical training/support issues and benefits not yet demonstrable at the audited stage. These are dated scoped observations, not universal accuracy or October 2026 outcomes. CLM-0315–0316.
- **Scottish Parliamentary Corporate Body:** its report preserves a positive internal-audit account for a separate HR/payroll implementation. The underlying test programme was not inspected; no zero-error, savings or comparative-product conclusion follows. CLM-0317.

Known exceptions can coexist with a reasoned release decision. Record the result, outstanding condition, owner, relevant authority and next action. A risk colour alone does not establish tolerability or effective mitigation. Capacity can be addressed in several ways; no automatic hiring recommendation follows.

## Match evidence to the claim

Plan how important benefits can be assessed while baselines and relevant data are available. Tracking an outcome does not by itself isolate why it changed. Evidence of a useful pilot may be relevant without establishing the same result at scale or in another context. The evaluation method and burden should fit the decision; our qualitative study is not a causal impact evaluation. CLM-0319–0320.

Where AI is actually part of an option, examine the task, permitted actions, context, output quality and review/correction work. A demonstration with selected inputs does not establish live performance or a cash saving. NIST's guidance supports context-specific assessment and limits to generalisation, not product certification or a payroll threshold. Its current framework-update notice remains recorded. CLM-0321–0322.

For the practitioner case, separate evidence requested, supplied, accepted and reportedly influential. Preserve the contributor's direct knowledge and missingness. The checklist assists existing Q008–Q013; it does not expand the fourteen-question core or demand private test packs and forecasts.

## Implications

**For practitioners:** identify the actual choice and next decision, reconcile resource/cash/funding views, assign benefit and delivery owners, and concentrate on assumptions that could change the decision. A small scoped improvement need not carry a major-programme bureaucracy.

**For suppliers:** make service/cost boundaries, realisation dependencies, implementation evidence and continuing responsibilities clear. A promotion, promise or case testimonial is not the customer's business case.

**For JGA:** the report can connect operating needs and leadership/capacity with actual evidence requirements while preserving alternative responses. It should not imply that every unmet need needs a hire, that non-cash benefit is worthless, or that a larger forecast ratio guarantees approval.
