# R03 second challenge: the operating arrangement behind the option

29 October 2026. Internal synthesis. Historical/lifecycle, segmented market, specific competitor, whole-work, engagement, dependency and recovery checks have been completed. No new practitioner evidence, product trial, procurement decision or external contact is implied.

## Conclusion

The eight response paths remain useful, with one substantive wording correction and five cross-cutting refinements. P08 must allow enduring capability/leadership as well as temporary expertise. The broader map should ask who does the work, what service is actually being compared, what an engagement owns, and how the arrangement remains usable through updates, disruption and handback.

This makes R03 more practical without expanding it into employment advice, a cyber audit, a vendor ranking or a full investment model. The current [option map](R03-OPTIONS-AND-TRADEOFFS.md) and its structured JSON apply the changes. The first-pass texts are preserved privately.

## Historical and lifecycle research

Earlier CIPP/JGA material shows that automation, skills, employee access and strategic information are familiar themes. It does not establish whether the earlier ambitions were achieved, how much work was displaced or whether a new platform is warranted. Positive current-software wording remains a counterweight to an automatic replacement story.

The useful new condition is continuing readiness. HMRC's annual-task chapter describes updating software for current rates/thresholds; SAP's public HCM lesson describes release preparation, tests, administrative tasks and adoption/support. These are different scopes. A supplier-specific release cadence or preview rule must not become a universal payroll requirement. [HMRC chapter](https://www.gov.uk/payroll-annual-reporting/update-software), [SAP lesson](https://learning.sap.com/courses/cloud-onboarding-for-sap-successfactors-hcm/understanding-sap-successfactors-hcm-product-release-cycles).

For the case, identify an applicable change, the person/party responsible, the relevant check, communication and exception route. Keeping a supported incumbent can be an active, evidenced choice. A successful prior test cannot demonstrate every future configuration or interface state. This does not show that updates cause failures or that old systems are automatically unsuitable.

## Market and whole-work research

Xero's UK self-service description assigns information/submissions and manager approvals across roles. That supports examining the full work journey. An employee or manager doing a task is not automatically an inefficient transfer: easier access, better timeliness or useful status may be valuable. Conversely, a reduction in payroll-team handling cannot by itself establish lower total effort. Ask who benefits, who performs each step and what actually changed. [Xero Me](https://www.xero.com/uk/xero-me/).

CloudPay's service description supplies a partner-coordination example. Deel's help distinguishes employment-provider, direct-employer payroll and HR-record relationships. These are publisher descriptions, not independently evaluated delivery or jurisdictional advice. They warn against comparing unlike products under a shared global-platform label. No new employment-model response path is added, and no country count is used as evidence of fit for a particular buyer.

The proposed cohort remains UK-relevant senior employer-side practitioners, subject to Nick's review. Smaller-employer and multinational illustrations test boundaries; they do not change the population or create a segmented survey. ## Dependency, recovery and positive managed-service evidence

The [root challenge](research-notes/r03-second-check/root/DEPENDENCY-AND-RECOVERY-CHALLENGE.md) adds GDS and NCSC guidance. It distinguishes delegated work from retained configuration/access, export from usable handback, and backup from recoverable service. These are design conditions to investigate, not an assertion that any inspected payroll service lacks them. No sensitive configuration or employee records are requested.

The counterargument matters: managed services can supply useful security/operating capability at scale, and technical dependence can have worthwhile benefits. Assess the actual trade-off rather than prescribe in-house operation or zero dependence. Public-sector spend-control obligations are not private-employer mandates. No recovery deadline or cost advantage is inferred. [NCSC shared responsibility](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cloud/understanding-cloud-services/cloud-security-shared-responsibility-model), [GDS dependence guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/managing-technical-lock-in-in-the-cloud).
