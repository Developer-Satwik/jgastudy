# What payroll operating needs mean for the report

28 October 2026. R02 desktop assessment completed for internal synthesis and updated following the user-requested second challenge. Original practitioner integration and final publication checks remain open. Nothing has been sent to Nick.

## Main conclusion

The most useful needs analysis follows an operating outcome: what must happen, where the process falls short, who experiences the consequence, who controls the relevant condition and what improvement would count. The evidence supports that approach across data handoffs, correction, service resolution, records/control, capacity and reporting.

This gives Part One concrete substance without assuming that all suppliers are failing or that every problem needs new software. The [needs and consequences matrix](R02-NEEDS-CONSEQUENCES-MATRIX.md) maps nine areas to evidence and the result to investigate. It is an analytical structure, not a market ranking.

## Refinements after the second challenge

The [second-check readout](R02-SECOND-CHECK-READOUT.md) tests this synthesis through historical, market, recruitment-competitor, employer, employee, institutional, payment and resilience lenses. The nine areas remain, with four refinements:

- Ownership can extend to banks and public institutions. Locate who can detect, authorise, amend and confirm the actual outcome.
- Preventing recurrence, clearing old affected records and closing the consequence are separate stages.
- Capacity includes ongoing operations, temporary cover, specialist change and accountable leadership/retained oversight, where relevant. An extra person is one possible response.
- Apply context, entitlement, safe authorisation, appropriate access, continuity and recoverability across the map. Four protected outcomes do not exhaust every need.

The earlier 352-answer AI study generated contrasts and source leads. Current primary checks support the market and comparator context; model recommendations do not establish real buyer demand or comparative service quality. No core interview question or client review file was changed.

## What the deeper research established

### The problem can begin before payroll processes it

Public employer evidence documents incomplete or late information leading to additional payments, checking and recovery tasks. The right question is therefore not only whether the final system has a feature. It is whether the information is complete, authorised, timely and owned at the relevant handoff.

Comhairle nan Eilean Siar's May 2025 internal audit identifies interdepartmental information-transfer problems while also giving Reasonable Assurance and praising maintained payroll service after a cyberattack. It shows why additional work and weaknesses can coexist with successful continuity. It does not establish how common this is elsewhere. [Original audit](https://www.cne-siar.gov.uk/sites/default/files/imce/Committees/Audit%20and%20Scrutiny%20Committee/2025/June/O-15A-Final-Report-Payroll-and-Expenses.pdf), executive summary, opinion and findings M2–M6.

### Service quality needs more than one clock

Fourth's public definition distinguishes response or a progress update from full resolution, and allocates customer duties around input and approval. This is a concrete scope example, not evidence of achieved performance. It supports analysing acknowledgement, investigation, resolution and employee outcome separately. [Definition](https://uk.fourth.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/Payroll-Bureau-Service-Definition-February-2026.pdf), PDF pp14–18.

An excluded task, unclear expectation and failure against an agreed service are different explanations. A dated Phase3 definition also describes assurance and reconciliation support, so the report cannot simply claim those capabilities are absent from the market. The current case still needs its actual scope and result.

### Capacity includes work that a headcount ratio misses

The reviewed CIPP and Deloitte originals distinguish processing from queries, validation, controls, projects, analysis and technical support. CIPP acknowledges complexity limits in its staffing presentation. Deloitte's small multinational peer group offers useful item definitions, but no ordinary UK staffing norm.

For the original cases, establish the work, dependency and consequence. A team keeping pay running through extra hours may still lack capacity for improvement or control. That is a condition to investigate, not an inference from headcount alone. No automatic recruitment, training or outsourcing recommendation follows.

### Correct payment is only one protected outcome

HMRC describes how reporting/identifier mistakes can require manual correction and cause duplicate or wrong employment records. DWP explicitly warns that incorrect or late earnings information can affect the Universal Credit a worker expects. This creates a consequence beyond the bank payment, without requiring us to collect personal benefit information. [HMRC corrections](https://www.gov.uk/payroll-errors/correcting-your-fps-or-eps), [DWP employer guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/universal-credit-information-for-employers).

Acas separately recognises communication, correction and possible financial difficulty when pay is late or wrong. That supports employee service as an operating need; it does not quantify incidence, attrition or a standard monetary loss. [Acas guidance](https://www.acas.org.uk/if-your-wages-are-not-paid/handling-unpaid-wages).

### Correction transactions are not automatically business losses

Oldham's October 2025 management report records correction/recovery activity and incomplete historical classification. Launched approval and reconciliation work is distinct from planned future improvement. [Original report](https://committees.oldham.gov.uk/documents/s155079/Payroll%20Audit%20Progress.pdf), sections 3–5.

Across the cases, keep remedial payment principal, gross overpayments, recoveries, outstanding sums, write-offs and administrative resource cost separate. The research did not establish a transferable payroll-only correction cost. That is preferable to attaching an impressive but unsupported ROI number to a proposed solution.

## What can be recommended at this stage

For payroll leaders: define the outcome and locate the owner, evidence and handoff before making a resource or technology argument. Preserve controls and arrangements that work. Distinguish recurring avoidable work from a temporary disruption.

For suppliers: clarify retained responsibilities, support entitlement, escalation and the evidence of closure. A service-scope, readiness or ownership problem calls for a different response from a genuinely missing capability. These are conditional implications, not proof of an unsatisfied market or a guaranteed sale.

For JGA: use explanatory cases to connect need, consequence, response and observed result. The value is the detail that helps a reader decide what to do, alongside sound published context. Avoid a universal failure story, staffing norm or vendor league table.

## Evidence strength and remaining boundaries

The desk stage combines surveys, official guidance, commercial scope documents and two public employer cases. These are different evidence classes. Public-sector case availability is a selection constraint; targets are not achieved performance; survey answers are not audited results. Earlier positive employee-experience evidence remains part of the picture.

Two additional Deloitte numerical presentations are held because displayed counts/bases or combined/subgroup figures do not reconcile transparently. We have not corrected them or inferred an omitted category. Existing Moorepay, Finity and HR.com holds also remain. No held statistic is needed for the synthesis above.

The original 6–10 practitioner contributions are still needed to identify the actual priorities, consequences, variation and decision histories in JGA's accessible cohort. They will not become a representative market estimate. The final chapter also needs exact claim wording, permissions and numerical context checked where used.
