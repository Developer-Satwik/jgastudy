# R02 Operational needs and service gaps

28 October 2026. Initial scoped evidence assessment for the project team. This is an internal working memo, not a final report chapter or practitioner finding. No participant collection or external contact occurred.

## Working conclusion

The useful unit for Part One is an unresolved operational outcome in a specific setting, rather than a general wish for better technology. Locate the problem, who is affected, what is already being done, what evidence supports the account and which actor can change the relevant condition. This links needs to the later investment analysis without assuming every need has become a proposal or every solution requires a purchase.

The reviewed originals support investigating information handoffs, employee access and explanation, measurement, capacity and service boundaries. They do not establish a single ranking of unmet needs among UK senior payroll leaders. Different studies ask different populations about different things.

## Four implications for the research

### Find the handoff before diagnosing the product

PayrollOrg's 2025 mixed-role, mainly US-located survey reports source-data and timing problems. Its accuracy question asks respondents to rank a supplied list of causes; this is attribution, not independent causal verification. Its performance chart also includes a no-tracking response. The achieved cohort is 585, but selected item-valid bases and aggregation detail are not shown on the inspected pages. [Original](https://info.payroll.org/pdfs/global/Global-Payroll-Week-2025-survey-results.pdf), printed pp3, 12, 37 and 40.

For JGA, the resulting question is practical: where does the issue enter the workflow, who supplies or approves the input, and what would need to change? A connected interface can move poor inputs more quickly. Whether that occurs in a particular case must come from the case evidence.

### Separate employee experience from an error rate

CIPP's in-house report identifies pay-information and late-data query themes. The item asks for the most frequent query; it does not measure the proportion of employees affected, total query volume or payroll error incidence. Its challenges page is a word cloud, with no verified frequency ranking or disclosed base. The hosted title says 2027 while the original body identifies 2026; preserve that distinction. [Queries](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/472716226/11/), [challenges](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/472716226/12/).

This supports investigating avoidable work around explanation, access and corrections. It does not prove that a chatbot or new portal is the best response. The improvement may involve clearer information, timely inputs, better ownership or an adequate existing process.

### Preserve evidence that arrangements work

SD Worx's appendix reports UK employee agreement of 75.7% for payroll accuracy/reliability and 79.0% for payslip clarity. The nominal UK employee subgroup is 998; item-valid bases and weighting are unstated. These are self-reported agreement, not audited accuracy. Non-agreement cannot be recoded as experienced errors or confusion. The company and employee groups are distinct, not matched case pairs. [Original](https://www.sdworx.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Corporate_HRPayroll_Pulse2026_Report.pdf), PDF pp4, 32 and 36.

The implication is to ask what should be preserved and to seek the actual consequence behind a proposed improvement. A design that only seeks failure would lose relevant variation and risk recommending unnecessary change.

### Treat capacity and control as part of the need

Part One should allow a contributor to describe insufficient time, capabilities, ownership or evidence, not just missing functionality. Do not infer that a training intervention or additional hire is required from a broad concern. Ask how the condition affects a concrete workflow and what support has already been tried. This is an analytical design recommendation, not an estimate of staffing shortages.

## A usable needs map to populate later

This is a coding structure for the actual research, not a table of measured market findings. A case can carry more than one mechanism.

| Mechanism | What to establish | Practical consequence to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability or service not known to be available | What is needed, how options were checked and whether availability is actually known | A genuine gap may warrant product or service development; an untested belief does not prove a market opportunity |
| Fit or service-scope mismatch | Which requirement fails and which part is provided or retained internally | Configuration, service scope or a different option may matter more than a new feature |
| Affordability or unclear value | Which cost or benefit is disputed and whose budget or work is affected | A need can be valid while the proposed response is not affordable or persuasive |
| Data or workflow readiness | Where information starts, who owns it, when it arrives and what checks exist | Resolve the handoff or preparation condition before attributing failure to the final system |
| Capacity, skills or ownership | What work is not being done, by whom, and how this affects the desired outcome | A technology-only recommendation may leave the delivery constraint unchanged |
| Not evaluated or not proposed | Whether the need reached internal consideration or a relevant market review | Keep never-raised needs separate; a newly recalled eligible case returns to the case route |
| Adequate arrangement | What works, what it costs to preserve and whether a material change is worthwhile | Renewal, incremental improvement or no immediate change can be justified |

The map should lead to conditional responses for employers and suppliers. It should not automatically label any category as a high-value sales opportunity.

## What to capture for a concrete need

Within the proposed core and selective probes, aim to establish:

1. The task or event where the issue occurs, its approximate recurrence and the organisational context.
2. Who experiences the consequence: payroll colleagues, employees, managers, HR, finance or a provider.
3. The current workaround and any useful controls or capabilities already in place.
4. A baseline or indicator where one exists, and whether it is a record, an estimate or an impression.
5. The owner of the relevant input, handoff or decision, including any boundary between employer and supplier.
6. The desired improvement and what would count as worthwhile progress.

Do not turn this into an extra six-question survey for everyone. Follow the most relevant need and use existing question routes. Exact budgets, employee records and confidential documents are unnecessary. Released capacity, better service, reduced risk and spending reductions remain separate possible outcomes.

## Evidence strength and safe use

| Evidence | Safe current use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CIPP query item and challenge word cloud | UK in-house context and selective interview prompts | Treating a most-frequent-query selection as query volume or error prevalence; ranking word-cloud terms |
| PayrollOrg ranked accuracy-cause list | Explore perceived data, timing and provider mechanisms in global settings | Causal conclusions; UK prevalence; silently assuming every achieved respondent answered each item |
| PayrollOrg performance chart | Support asking how the organisation assesses results | Equating absence of a stated tracking method with ineffective payroll; treating chart options as a closed partition |
| SD Worx employee agreement | Balanced contextual evidence and clarity/accuracy as distinct dimensions | Computing an error rate from the remainder; claiming causal retention or cash ROI; matching employer and employee responses |

Survey responses are measured answers to questions, not necessarily observed operational performance. Professional-body commentary and vendor interpretation are distinct again. A final chart should preserve the relevant population, field dates, wording and actual base; it must not combine these sources into one payroll failure index.
