# R03 second challenge: market context, work transfer and the unit of service

29 October 2026. Internal desktop challenge. Existing originals and three selected new supplier routes. No procurement ranking, product trial, market sizing, new participant evidence or commercial validation.

## Judgment

The eight response paths hold. The map does not prescribe replacement, AI, outsourcing or a hire. It becomes more useful with two refinements: examine whose work changes, including employees/managers, and define the actual country/entity/worker/service unit before comparing offers. An employer's size or a provider's brand is insufficient context.

No new path is required merely because a platform bundles several services. The eight paths describe payroll responses; changing the legal employment relationship is a boundary to identify, not silently classify as a different payroll-processing arrangement or add to the report's advisory scope.

## The missing unit is the complete work journey

Self-service can be a positive operating improvement: easier access, fewer requests, clearer status and less re-entry may matter. It can also relocate entry, checking, approval or help-seeking. Neither a saving nor a burden should be assumed from the feature. In the actual case, identify the originator, approver, operator, beneficiary and support route. Distinguish one-time onboarding/training from recurring work.

Where a participant reports time released, ask what happened to the task elsewhere and whether the intended outcome improved. A central payroll-team reduction can coexist with additional manager work; that does not automatically make the change bad. Quality, timeliness and employee usefulness matter alongside effort. This is an analytical implication to test, not evidence that any inspected product creates extra burden.

## A supplier is not the unit of comparison

Compare the actual product/module, package, country/entity, worker relationship, task boundary, access and date. A supplier can supply multiple combinations. Global payroll, payroll payments, employee self-service, HR administration, staffing and EOR employment are not interchangeable because they appear on the same platform.

The CloudPay declaration supports examining oversight and coordination, not a claim that all global providers use the same network model. The Deel explanation is a publisher's account of its worker types, not a general legal definition, jurisdictional advice or proof of its compliance. Neither is independently evaluated delivery evidence.

## Specific recommendations for the integrated R03 map

1. Keep all eight paths, their combinable status and adequate/no-immediate-replacement cases.
2. In P02–P07, add a selective check of who supplies information, who approves and whose work becomes easier or harder, including employees/managers where relevant. Record uncertainty rather than impose a cost estimate.
3. Add country/entity/worker/service unit to context. Confirm whether the product acts as software, processor, payment executor, coordinator, employment provider or a combination in the case.
4. For shared or managed arrangements, identify the owner of partner coordination and the escalation/acceptance route across parties, not just the named account contact.
5. Keep feature availability, entitled access, configured workflow and observed result separate. Preserve the Cintra UI difference and beta limits; new pages do not clear them.
6. Test both directions: a smaller process improvement may be inadequate, and a broader platform may impose a poor fit. Do not make the least extensive option automatically cheapest or sufficient.

These fit existing selective probes and analysis under Q002/Q004/Q012/Q013. No compulsory questions, new cohort, employment-advisory workstream or R05 financial model is introduced.
