# R03 second challenge: dependency, recovery and ongoing operation

29 October 2026. Internal desktop assessment. Four deliberately selected public primary guidance pages; no system, recovery or migration test was performed. This is an option-design lens, not a cyber audit, legal opinion or procurement approval.

## Judgment

The eight paths remain useful. They need a cross-cutting lifecycle test: can the employer and relevant partners continue, update, recover and eventually replace the relevant work? Acquisition of capability and recoverability are separate. A file that can be downloaded is not by itself evidence of a usable service elsewhere.

The existing map mentions recovery, updates and handback, but does not yet consistently distinguish data access, configuration/knowledge, authority and a working recovery route. Add those conditions across relevant paths rather than create a ninth product option or prescribe a new security programme.

## Primary checks and balanced implications

The [GDS integration/adaptation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/integrate-and-adapt-technology) addresses service management, skills, configuration management and continuing integration tests. It supports treating integration as an operating capability. The public-sector spend-control requirements are not private-employer mandates, and its benefit list is not a measured payroll result.

[NCSC's shared-responsibility guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cloud/understanding-cloud-services/cloud-security-shared-responsibility-model) distinguishes delegated provider work from customer configuration and choice of data. It also describes benefits of managed services. This is counterevidence to a one-sided argument that dependence on a supplier is intrinsically worse. The actual service and employer needs still require assessment; the guidance does not certify any payroll product.

[NCSC's SaaS guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cloud/using-cloud-services-securely/using-saas-securely) addresses protected backups, access recovery, logs and reviews after updates. That supplies conditions to ask about where continuity or access matters. It does not prove a backup was restored, promise a recovery time or justify collecting sensitive payroll/security records.

[GDS's lock-in guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/managing-technical-lock-in-in-the-cloud) explicitly balances dependence with useful managed capabilities. It supports understanding the dependence accepted and maintaining practical skills and options. It is not evidence that a particular payroll contract is difficult to leave, a mandate for zero dependence or a payroll switching-cost estimate. Its September 2026 update changes an agency reference; that date is not evidence of a new technology trend.

## Four distinctions for the integrated map

| Distinction | Useful case-specific evidence or question | What must not be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delegated operation versus retained configuration/access | Who can use, configure, monitor and restore the relevant component under the actual arrangement? | A provider does everything, or an employer must build every control itself. |
| Export versus usable handback | Required records, meanings/IDs, relevant history, configuration/knowledge and an authorised receiving process where applicable. | A generic export promise guarantees a complete, reconciled migration. |
| Backup versus recoverable service | Evidence of the relevant recovery route, access, dependencies and essential tasks, appropriate to the claim. | Possession of a file establishes continuity, or any tested timetable is universal. |
| Dependency versus avoidable constraint | What useful function is gained, what dependence is accepted, who can maintain it and what alternatives remain workable? | All technical dependence is harmful, or a managed benefit erases every future constraint. |

These are analytical extensions, not additional measured market findings. Different options may warrant different detail. Avoid turning a qualitative conversation into a technical questionnaire or requiring confidential evidence participants cannot share.

## R03 changes proposed

1. Add ongoing operation/update, recovery and handback to the option map's cross-cutting conditions.
2. Record what expertise and permissions must remain with the employer or other accountable operator after go-live or specialist departure.
3. Keep restored operation, recovered records and employee consequence closure separate.
4. Preserve the positive managed-service argument and judge the actual trade-off. Neither in-house operation nor a supplier label establishes the best response.
5. Leave quantified costs, approval authority and reasons for switching/deferral to R04–R06. This pass identifies technical/operating feasibility conditions only.
