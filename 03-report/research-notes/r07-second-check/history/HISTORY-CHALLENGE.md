# R07 second challenge: priority history and sequencing

29 October2026. Internal targeted historical review. Three canonical sources are reused, not three new datasets or original practitioners. Four bounded claim/design candidates. No percentages, investment trend, forecast or financial input.

## What this adds

R07 correctly separates stated importance, resources and delivery. History adds a useful selective condition: establish whether the work is newly selected, continuing, re-scoped or re-timed, and what the next milestone actually depends on. A repeated theme is not proof of failed delivery; a later phase is not proof of lower importance or no funding. H7S-C04.

This refines the existing priority record without turning it into a project-history questionnaire. A contributor need only explain the relevant change or continuing condition they directly know. If history is unknown, preserve that rather than reconstruct it from an old forecast or corporate profile.

## Repeated themes do not make a matched investment history

[CIPP2020](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/903316/) places automation and changing skills in a pandemic-era discussion, with an item about expected transactional development. Its JGA foreword is attributed commentary. [CIPP2024](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/706203142/) revisits that expectation alongside current AI/RPA measures. Different tasks, definitions and respondents do not identify the same employers’ progress or a funded delivery shortfall. H7S-C01.

The2024 reaction-timeframe questions concern perceived pressure and reported team issues. They do not identify which funded priorities were reassessed or whether resources moved between projects. H7S-C02. No figures are transferred, no cross-edition arithmetic is performed, and publisher progress commentary is not adopted as an independently established trend.

Both reports cover mixed payroll-professional perspectives. Item-valid bases, matched respondents and comparable UK employer investment measures are not established for this application. CIPP2022 was screened but adds no necessary independent support to this narrow comparison.

## Planned sequence is different from importance

The existing [LFB2025 report](https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/media/jrqngpy0/lfc-25-098-replacement-hr-and-payroll-system-novemberr-2025-update-signed.pdf), PDF5–6, makes phase2 dependent on phase1 and places it after go-live to manage workload alongside ordinary activity. Phase2 contains particular HR modules within one combined programme. This is a declared dependency and sequence, not a payroll-only priority ranking, actual later completion or evidence of no funding. H7S-C03.

This matters when an apparent “lower priority” is actually waiting for another piece of work, a resource window or a defined milestone. That is a possible interpretation to establish in a case, not a universal excuse for delay. The plan’s2026 dates remain prospective in that record; a passed date supplies no later operational result. No new later-outcome search finding is claimed here.

## Application to the unchanged R07 design

- Preserve the original priority wording and current task/outcome, then selectively establish its origin or material revision.
- Distinguish an importance judgment from known delivery order, dependency, timing or shared capacity.
- Record the contributor’s actual reason for a changed milestone; do not infer displaced resources from perceived pressure alone.
- Do not treat continuing work as a new approved investment each time it appears in a plan, or presume continuity means no progress.
- Keep last evidenced state, original/updated target and actual later event distinct. No extrapolation from an older plan.

These are analyst checks under Q005 and the existing recent-case routes. They add no compulsory question, status ladder, new cohort or numeric score. A priority may legitimately remain operating work or adequate retention, and earlier critical work can coexist with later valuable improvement. Whether that is true in any original case still needs eligible, permissioned contributor evidence.
