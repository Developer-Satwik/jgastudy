# Historical and recent evidence challenge

28 October 2026. Internal challenge of R01 and the WP02 version 0.1 draft. Public desktop research only; no messages, forms, purchases or participant collection. This memo is not a client-approved finding or final publication text.

## Conclusion

The decision-case design remains useful, but a historical lens makes it sharper. It should investigate why long-discussed ambitions become different organisational choices, not imply a newly discovered payroll transformation problem. Add targeted probes and a concise historical context element within the existing outline. Do not add a separate forecasting study or a larger survey.

## Six findings that matter

### 1 JGA has a documented history in this research conversation

The [CIPP 2020 original](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/903316/) names JGA as a sponsor and contains a JGA foreword by Nick Day, printed pages 13–14. The [CIPP 2022 original](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/1017671823/) also names JGA and identifies Nick as Managing director of JGA Recruitment Group in its education foreword, printed pages 9–10. This is evidence of support and a named contribution, not ownership of those reports or endorsement of this project.

Implication: the new report can examine what has changed since earlier expectations. Internally, ask Nick whether there are useful lessons or materials from that prior involvement. Do not make that an access dependency, reproduce the earlier reports or imply our work is the first research JGA has commissioned/supported. The main valuable extension is examining actual decisions and consequences.

### 2 The historical story is continuity and uneven progress

Earlier CIPP material already discusses integration, strategic influence, automation and skills. The 2024 report also records 53% choosing the response that they were happy with their current software, on printed page 7. That item combines satisfaction with a no-change category and has no clear item base in the inspected text. It supports balanced questioning, not an estimate of satisfaction among all UK employers.

Implication: preserve renewal, deliberate no change and successful current processes as legitimate outcomes. A planned historical graphic should show recurring questions and dated evidence, rather than a numerical progress line from incomparable editions. Describe context and question changes before claiming a trend.

### 3 Technology labels can create a false adoption narrative

The 2020 report distinguishes anticipated future transactional automation from present use; Nick's foreword gives the anticipation item a base of 441. The [2024 original](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/706203142/) separately reports AI use at 6%, RPA use at 17%, and 48% unable to distinguish the two, printed page 4. Item bases and a precise achieved total are not established in the inspected 2024 text. These are not matched respondents, equal measures or an adoption forecast.

Implication: keep unaided questions first. If AI or automation arises, ask what actual task is performed, whether it is a demonstration/pilot/live workflow, who reviews exceptions and what change was observed. Do not ask respondents to classify a product correctly as a precondition to contributing. Existing macro surveys can provide context; the report's original contribution is workflow and decision detail.

### 4 A payroll purchase may not fix the boundary where the problem originates

The [PayrollOrg 2025 original](https://info.payroll.org/pdfs/global/Global-Payroll-Week-2025-survey-results.pdf), printed pages 12–14, identifies inbound/outbound data and local compliance as challenges and poor inputs, time data and inputs after cut-off as reported accuracy causes. The study describes 585 respondents, mainly US-located, with fieldwork 3 February–3 April 2025, printed page 3. Its attributions do not independently prove causal mechanisms for JGA's population.

Implication: add an optional case probe about where the problem begins, who owns that handoff and whose work must change. A framework that distinguishes provider capability from internal data and process responsibility would be more useful than a shopping checklist. Do not present payroll leaders as responsible for all upstream conditions they cannot control.

### 5 Investment should include capability and organisational change

Both historical JGA forewords connect technology with analytical, advisory, leadership and education needs. They include forward-looking commentary and cannot establish that promised benefits were subsequently realised.

Implication: explicitly allow investment in training, staffing, management time and process controls within Q006 case-selection probes. Capture ongoing support and capability needs alongside implementation effort in Q010/Q013. The current wording says payroll investment broadly, but software/provider examples could otherwise dominate recall. This is a small guide refinement within scope, not a recruitment or skills report.

### 6 The 2027 horizon needs dated readiness scenarios

[HMRC interim guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/draft-guidance-and-legislation-to-aid-preparation-for-reporting-benefits-in-kind-in-real-time/the-default-operation-of-mandatory-payrolling), updated 23 September 2026, sets the first mandatory phase from 6 April 2027 for cars, fuel, vans and employer medical benefits. [Agent Update 145](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agent-update-issue-145/issue-145-of-agent-update), 16 July 2026, states most other benefits follow from April 2028. The guidance is interim; confirm current primary material again at publication. These are planned regulatory milestones, not forecasts of actual employer readiness or spend.

Implication: distinguish a legal deadline, contract/system lifecycle and discretionary improvement as triggers. Ask what minimum adaptation is unavoidable and what additional transformation remains a choice. Use conditional 2027 scenarios with observable signposts and practical actions, not fabricated adoption probabilities. Avoid a blanket statement that all benefits must be payrolled in April 2027.

## Specific changes recommended to WP02

Keep the 14 core questions and eight-chapter structure. Add selective probes:

- Q004: Where does the shortfall originate, and what can the payroll team or provider directly change?
- Q006: Include people/capability investment as well as technology, providers and process change. Keep the same 24-month recent-case rule.
- Q008: Was the trigger a required deadline, lifecycle issue, opportunity or discretionary improvement? Ask after the unaided answer.
- Q010/Q013: Identify the baseline and desired outcome where known, ongoing staffing/training/support and responsibilities across handoffs. Do not require exact confidential budgets.
- If AI arises: ask for a task and actual stage of use. Treat the terminology as a prompt for clarification, not an adoption score.

Within chapter 1 or the introduction, consider a short dated context panel on recurring ambitions and changed conditions. Within chapter 3, distinguish readiness milestones from conditional future scenarios. Do not add new chapters or an elaborate pseudo-forecast.

## What this challenge does not establish

It does not show market-wide change over time, prove the new report is unique, validate commercial demand, identify causal effects of technology, or show what JGA's practitioner cohort will say. Recent figures from ADP, PayrollOrg and CIPP should not be joined into one trend: populations, years, questions and technology definitions differ. Previous source conflicts remain held. No previously quarantined statistic was resolved by this work.
