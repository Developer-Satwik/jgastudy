# R02 second check: market segments and service boundaries

28 October 2026. Internal desktop challenge. No participant data, procurement recommendation, market sizing or achieved service evaluation. Six deliberately selected live primary routes, plus the already inspected Fourth scope example, test whether R02 is too generic or incorrectly assumes market absence.

## Judgment

The nine operating needs survive the market challenge. The strongest refinement is to tag the context of each case before diagnosing a gap. Employer headcount is one descriptor; entity/country coverage, pay frequency, variable inputs, internal skills, retained responsibilities and actual entitlement can change the work. This is an analytical interpretation to test, not a survey finding.

Published offers contradict a blanket claim that payroll users cannot obtain automation, employee access, managed expertise, controls or consolidated reporting. Their existence does not establish fit, affordability, successful implementation or actual service quality. The question becomes: what outcome is inadequately served for this employer, under the current arrangement, and why?

A supplier name is not a consistent unit of service: package, contract, jurisdiction and date matter.

## Challenge to the nine operating needs

| Existing area | Market challenge result | Refinement for case analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timely complete authorised inputs | Retained inputs appear explicitly even in the managed example. | Identify who originates, validates, approves and submits each relevant input. A system integration is not evidence those duties are performed. |
| Validation/payment approval | The managed example still requires employer sign-off; software offers calculations and payments. | Distinguish calculation/checking, authority to release and the actual payment action; verify the case arrangement. |
| Correction/recovery capacity | Product pages establish capabilities, not handling of a particular exception. | Ask whether correction is included, who owns related records, and what was actually resolved. Do not assume absence from a page means exclusion. |
| Reporting identity/payment information | HMRC recognition is a defined reporting boundary. | Keep reporting suitability and employee outcome distinct; verify needed functions and case evidence. |
| Employee explanation/resolution | Employee tools and online support already appear in advertised offers. | Separate access to information, support for the employer and direct employee-case resolution. Check entitlement and observed closure. |
| Records/reconciliation | Managed checks and global reporting are available as advertised capabilities. | Establish retrieval/access and actual reconciliation owner rather than counting a dashboard as a control. |
| Sustainable skills/coverage | Software, managed specialists and global expertise represent different boundaries. | Record internal work and specialist dependencies remaining under the actual arrangement; avoid universal headcount or cost benchmarks. |
| Employer/provider boundary | Strongly confirmed by explicit managed inputs and approval. | Record package, date, country/entity and documented responsibility alongside the observed experience. |
| Decision-useful reporting | Global supplier offers consolidated data/reporting; smaller bundle offers accounting reports. | Identify the user, decision, data scope and latency. A feature list cannot show whether a particular decision is supported. |

## The useful market distinction

When the required result is not obtained, retain multiple plausible explanations:

1. Capability exists but is not included or affordable under the current package.
2. Capability is included but lacks usable data, permissions, configuration, skills or a workable handoff.
3. Execution/service fails against an agreed requirement.
4. The need is unusual, unresolved or not known to be available after actual evaluation.
5. The present arrangement is adequate, or deferring change is reasonable given other constraints.

Do not label one explanation from a promotional page or a participant's first impression alone. None of the inspected pages measures prevalence of these explanations, willingness to pay, business losses or the relative merits of in-house and managed delivery.

## Proposed refinements, within the existing interview design

Use existing context questions and selective probes. Do not expand the fourteen core questions or turn a small qualitative cohort into a segmented survey.

- Context tags where relevant: employee/entity/country footprint; pay frequency and variable/exception work; internal/shared/provider execution; retained input, approval and query duties.
- Need evidence: intended result, what the current package/process should supply, the actual gap and consequence, workaround and owner.
- Economics: licence or service entitlement, extra-charge work and internal effort are distinct. Ask only for known or permissioned evidence; preserve estimates as estimates.
- Multicountry cases: ask which parts must be locally correct, which centrally visible, and who controls exceptions. Include international remits as context within the proposed UK-relevant cohort rather than changing the population silently.
- Functioning arrangement: what is adequate, what must be preserved and why renewal/no purchase is reasonable.

## Consequences for the report and next module

R02 should explain the operating need before attaching a solution label. A bounded market comparison can show that broadly named capabilities are already supplied in different forms while the unresolved issue may sit in access, fit or execution. This makes the report more useful to employers and more credible to informed suppliers.

The actual choice among technology, delivery models and integration approaches belongs to R03. That stage can evaluate dependencies and full costs after the case need is defined. This check supplies questions and constraints, not a preferred provider or a conclusion that the market is failing.
