# R07: what a stated priority actually establishes

29 October 2026. Internal desktop evidence. Five existing canonical routes re-inspected; no new source route or independent dataset added. Eight bounded claim/design/hold records. No original employer, budget document, forecast or product trial.

## Main conclusion

Published payroll priorities are useful inputs to the research, but their units and question frames matter. A desired feature, a strategic-status label, reported investment, a plan, a respondent's personal training intention and an approved organisational project are different observations. They should not become a single ranked list of funded priorities.

The practical refinement is to record each priority's exact meaning and timeframe, then clarify approval, money/resources, delivery and result separately where known. This fits the unchanged Q005: up to two unaided organisational priorities over the next 12–24 months, with selective status/funding clarification. It does not add a compulsory question or a numerical maturity ladder. PRI7-C08.

## The composite-status trap

Azets' B1 is stronger than a mere statement of future interest, but it still combines several ideas into one response category. It does not identify which action was reviewed, approved or paid for, nor its amount or timing. Splitting the composite percentage into separate funded, approved and strategically important groups would invent information the item does not supply.

E1 is also not a clean spending question. Its scope includes how payroll is managed as well as investment. The presence of employee experience, efficiency, skills, regulatory complexity, technology or expansion in the response set does not mean a funded project exists for each selected factor. Several factors may apply to one organisation. Neither their coexistence nor their reported ordering identifies causal importance for a specific decision.

The original method describes a May 2026 survey of 205 senior/C-suite/payroll decision-makers across six markets, with 52 UK respondents and a large-employer profile. Four separate qualitative interviews supply commentary; they do not enlarge the survey denominator to 209. Overall country samples are available, but the inspected item-valid bases, recruitment mechanism, routing, weights and UK-specific item results are unestablished. These remain source-scoped reported observations, not an all-UK employer estimate. PRI7-C01–C02.

## Why different lists can disagree without being contradictory

SD Worx's broad urgent-HR-challenges item and its payroll-specific priority list use different reference frames. A payroll improvement can be important within payroll while not displacing employee wellbeing, recruitment or another issue in an organisation-wide list. Publisher recommendations form a third category. They are not an additional measured priority list.

The thematic report's narrative compliance figure and its payroll-priority figure therefore must not automatically be flagged as an arithmetic conflict: the broad HR question and narrower payroll list differ. The source supports careful framing, not a reconciled common ranking or a claim that compliance became more/less important. PRI7-C04.

The full report's separate AI agreement statements likewise do not show the same organisations moving step by step from exploration to investment, deployment and outcome. A respondent might explore one task while using another. Without linked records and a common definition, subtracting marginal percentages would create a fictitious investment funnel. The statements concern workplace/HR AI, not necessarily the specific payroll task. PRI7-C03.

## A new small numeric conflict, kept narrow

Fresh inspection of Moorepay's original published feature chart V2-01 found branding/customisation labelled 12%, while the current annual prose says 13%. PRI7-C07 records a targeted `hold_numeric_reuse` candidate. Do not average the figures or pick a preferred version. The discrepancy does not establish which publication is correct, that every other value is wrong, or that all feature responses are funded priorities.

No feature percentage is used in this strand. Existing CLM-0051 and CLM-0052 switching/investment quarantines remain unchanged, as do the earlier Deloitte numerical holds. The feature-desire question still has a useful qualitative meaning without adopting its percentages. Moorepay describes mixed payroll, finance and HR roles across in-house, outsourced and bureau organisations; that is not a confirmed senior employer-side investment-decision sample. PRI7-C05/07.

## Personal development and organisational investment

CIPP's employer support and personal qualification measures provide a useful counterweight to a software-only frame. A training priority can involve sponsorship, fees, time, cover, completed learning and application in different combinations. A personal qualification plan need not be employer funded; an employer offer need not establish a paid invoice, completion or effective operational capability.

R07 should retain both the person's own desired improvement and what they expect their organisation to prioritise. Ask whose priority it is and what they directly know, without demanding confidential amounts or assuming lack of budget means no useful action. Existing paid time, smaller process work and retention of a functioning arrangement remain possible. This is an analytical application of the item distinctions, not a measured finding that particular support delivers a given result. PRI7-C06/08.

## Implications for the report

For practitioners, clarify the concrete outcome and current commitment behind a broad theme such as automation, integration or upskilling. Distinguish expected importance from known organisational action, and identify the conditions that could change it.

For suppliers, a desired feature can open an evidence conversation but does not identify an active deal, price acceptance or approved scope. Do not label every preference as unmet demand or sell a technology solution before understanding the actual task and retained responsibilities.

For JGA, the value is a clear connection between priority, context and action. Publish themes as source-scoped context or actual qualitative cases, not an unsupported league table of funded markets. Preserve personal versus organisational priorities and optional unaided/prompted distinctions. No new question, client-file edit, hiring recommendation or forecast is created.
