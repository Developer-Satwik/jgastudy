# R07 contextual differences: mechanism is not a group ranking

29 October 2026. Internal desktop evidence. Two new official HTML routes, two canonical reuses and five bounded claims/design applications. No survey subgroup estimate, legal determination, original practitioner observation or employer forecast.

## Main contribution

Employer scale, sector and geography can help describe a case, but their labels do not establish which payroll improvement is most important, approved or funded. The useful context is the actual population and task within the contributor's remit, the working arrangements and relevant jurisdiction, retained delivery responsibilities and the decision's current stage. Keep these dimensions separate rather than produce a complexity score.

The selected sources establish conditional mechanisms and boundaries. They do not demonstrate that one group invests more, has worse payroll or prefers a particular supplier. That distinction is the main R07 contribution of this strand.

## Use context selectively under the unchanged questions

Existing Q002 already asks how payroll is delivered, broad scale, relevant countries, remit, retained work and handoffs. Q005 asks for up to two future priorities and separates their status from funding. This is sufficient for selective follow-up; the table below is an analyst aid, not six additional compulsory questions.

| Context dimension | What is worth establishing if material | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population and unit | The payroll population actually in the contributor's remit; whether a stated size covers one payroll, entity, country or group. | Headquarters, total global workforce, team size and employees processed are not interchangeable units. Do not manufacture an employer-size threshold. |
| Working and pay arrangements | Relevant contracted pattern, variability, pay-period structure and the actual information/calculation work that follows. | Sector or job title alone does not classify workers, prove a calculation rule or establish the priority's cost. |
| Jurisdiction and remit | Which places and schemes matter to this work, and the limits of the contributor's knowledge. | A UK headquarters or the number of countries does not establish a single common payroll regime or a comparative investment rank. |
| Delivery boundary | Which tasks are internal, provided or retained, and which handoffs the described priority affects. | In-house, outsourced and mixed are incomplete descriptions of responsibility. No delivery preference or retained-work workload is inferred from the label. |
| Decision and change context | Current service, proposed change and relevant organisational or implementation stage, using the R06 distinctions. | A pilot, approved plan, live process and observed benefit can have different dates and scope. Do not call a sector more advanced because different measures were compared. |
| Relevant timing | Applicable period and known deadline or operating calendar, only where it affected the case. | One school-related timing account is not a general sector rule or a reason to presume every delayed project was prudent. |

CTX7-C05 makes this explicit as a researcher application. Do not treat every dimension as applicable, force precise confidential workforce figures or fill an unknown context from a company's public profile. Preserve the contributor's remit and direct knowledge, including not known or prefer not to disclose.

## What comparisons are defensible

A case can explain how a particular arrangement made a priority relevant, with the contributor's account and supporting evidence. A small qualitative study can also describe contrasts between its actual cases, while preserving their scope and contrary cases. These are explanatory case contrasts, not a representative sector or size estimate.

For a published quantitative group comparison, first establish the actual item, response definition, period, relevant group membership, valid bases and the study's recruitment/weighting/method. An overall sample or country count does not supply each item's subgroup base. Different questions about importance, intention, funding and actual implementation are not the same outcome. The current source set does not support a numerical SME-versus-large, sector or country ranking of funded payroll priorities.

A plausible mechanism remains a hypothesis until the case establishes its relevance. For example, the official guidance shows that working arrangements can affect payroll work. It does not show which employers prioritised that work, whether a proposal was raised or funded, or how much it cost. The analysis should not jump from an applicable rule to a sale, a technology replacement or a hire.

## Sparse and missing groups

No original participants have yet been collected. The proposed UK-relevant population is not approved, and international public context does not expand it. Absence of a small-employer, sector, geography or delivery group from the eventual sample would not demonstrate absence of need or investment in that group.

Do not split six to ten contributors into apparent market segments and publish percentages that imply a sampling basis we do not have. Where actual cases offer a useful contrast, describe the context and evidence in prose or a labelled case table. Where information is sparse or withheld, record that limitation rather than impute an average, stereotype or publicly available workforce total. Avoid unnecessarily detailed combinations that could identify a contributor.

## Reader implications

For practitioners, define the relevant payroll work and decision before borrowing a market priority. For suppliers, establish the actual operating and jurisdictional scope rather than assume a sector/size label determines the offer. For JGA, preserve contextual explanations and different legitimate responses without turning a mechanism into a general recruitment or spending recommendation.
