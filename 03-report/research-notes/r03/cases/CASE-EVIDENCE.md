# R03 implementation cases: continuity, usable service and realised results

29 October 2026. Internal desktop assessment. Two deliberately selected Scottish public employers, not a representative implementation sample or a comparison of product quality. Three primary source candidates cover an external audit, an employer annual report and a programme self-review. Same-publication section pages are aliases, not additional independent studies.

## Judgment

The cases support separating availability, controlled transition, usable operating outputs, stable support and demonstrated benefit. A platform can meet an essential continuity goal while other outcomes remain incomplete. Conversely, positive implementation assurance must stay in the evidence rather than be displaced by high-profile problem cases.

These cases inform option prerequisites and result tests in R03. They do not establish a preferred technology, delivery model, market failure rate, comparative vendor quality, typical costs or ROI. R04/R05 will examine organisational authority and the full investment case separately.

## Case 1: Scottish Government shared platform

Audit Scotland's October 2025 annual audit reports a reasonably approved go-live and well-executed cutover. Sampled staff-pay testing found no issues. It also describes reporting/reconciliation difficulties, support pressure and benefits not yet delivered or demonstrable at that stage. These outcomes coexist. The financial-statement audit's scope is not a census of employee-pay correctness. CASE-C01 to CASE-C03; original printed pp42-56, especially pp47,50,55.

The Scottish Government's closure report, published 23 March 2026, mixes retrospective initial-operation accounts, management's assessment and reported progress by publication, and future plans. Section4 describes a more stable position alongside remaining issues and work, rather than merely repeating the early implementation experience. Appendix1 contains early directorate reflections; section8 also discusses a later benefits checkpoint. These timeframes must remain distinct. It is management self-review, not independent confirmation of every benefit or of the position in October2026. CASE-C04 and CASE-C05; sections4,8 and Appendix1 HR.

Originals: [external audit](https://audit.scot/uploads/2025-10/aar_2425_scottish_government.pdf), [post-implementation self-review](https://www.gov.scot/publications/shared-services-programme-phase-1-programme-closure-report/pages/5/), [benefits section](https://www.gov.scot/publications/shared-services-programme-phase-1-programme-closure-report/pages/9/), [HR reflections](https://www.gov.scot/publications/shared-services-programme-phase-1-programme-closure-report/pages/11/).

### What this changes in the R03 map

- Identify the essential task being protected or enabled. Replacing an unsupported/unsuitable foundation and reducing routine processing effort are different objectives.
- Test the whole relevant flow. A successful transfer and calculation do not prove usable reconciliation, reporting, query handling or controlled access.
- Describe known limitations, owners, workarounds and support capacity. A plan need not claim every defect is absent, but must establish what makes continuation acceptable in its own context.
- Separate transition work and backlog from stable recurring work. Temporary surge support, training and exception ownership are part of the operating arrangement to investigate.
- Keep benefits conditional on task readiness, adoption, data and the required modules being available. A product feature or go-live date is not a measured business result.

No programme-wide budget, monthly operating cost, support-ticket count, test percentage or user-body ratio is proposed as a payroll benchmark. HR/payroll tickets include more than pay errors, and the programme spans HR, finance and purchasing. Expected later cost reduction is not achieved savings. The later publication does not justify retroactively treating earlier benefits as realised, but its later management-reported progress must not be suppressed by treating the entire document as early-month reflections.

## Case 2: Scottish Parliamentary Corporate Body

The employer's 2024-25 annual report records replacement with i-Trent HR/payroll and an October 2024 go-live. It reports that a June 2025 internal audit of the new system found no significant issues, following planning, design and testing. This is a useful positive counterexample, not proof of zero payroll errors or quantified savings. The full underlying internal-audit test programme was not inspected. CASE-C06; printed p37/PDF p38.

The same report discusses a separate Oracle finance replacement. Do not conflate it with the Parliament's HR/payroll solution, or treat the organisation as the same implementation as the Scottish Government's HR/payroll programme. The report is approved/signed 25 September 2025; the exact public upload date is unestablished. A search result's document title is misleading, but the retained cover identifies 2024-25.

Original: [SPCB annual report](https://www.businessintheparliament.scot/-/media/files/spcb/annual-reports/spcb-annual-report-and-accounts-2024-25.pdf).

## Evidence tests for an option, derived from the cases

| Test | Evidence worth seeking in an actual case | Limit to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can the task work? | Relevant end-to-end, exception and reconciliation checks; actual required interfaces/outputs. | A feature demonstration is not a live operating result. |
| Can transition protect essential work? | Migration/cutover responsibilities, input/approval arrangements, workable contingency and continuity evidence. | An accepted workaround or missing test criterion is case-specific, not permission to reuse another employer's tolerance. |
| Can people operate it? | Role readiness, usable guidance, time to train and a defined support/exception owner. | Training availability is not completed learning or successful adoption. |
| Can the required outcome be verified? | Pay/records/reporting/employee consequence checks appropriate to the claim and period. | Audit assurance is bounded by scope, timing and sample. |
| Can it be sustained? | A stable operating period, recurring controls, update effects, unresolved work and actual resource use. | Early support effort is neither automatically a permanent burden nor automatically temporary. |
| Has a benefit occurred? | Baseline, intended change, observed result, user/owner and assessment date, where available. | Go-live, risk reduction, released capacity and reduced spending are distinct claims. |

The table proposes analytical checks, not new data, validated go/no-go thresholds or additional mandatory interview questions. Existing Q002-Q005 and Q012-Q013 can supply relevant context. A sensible current arrangement or smaller process/configuration change remains a legitimate comparison.

## Selection and remaining gaps

Public reporting makes these examples inspectable but selects for public-sector governance and shared-platform conditions. Two organisations cannot estimate the rate of successful or unsuccessful change or prove that larger employers need integrated suites. More case discovery would not cure the selection issue. The original permissioned practitioner contributions should establish private-employer context, actual trade-offs, observed results and adequate incumbent arrangements.

Edinburgh's 2026 payroll-controls original was discovered but returned403/inaccessible on direct attempts. It is discovery-only and excluded from the analysis; no search-snippet finding was adopted. No contact or access bypass occurred.
