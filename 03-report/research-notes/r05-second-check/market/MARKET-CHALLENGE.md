## What changes after looking across different service models

R05's three financial views remain useful. The extra market evidence sharpens **what quantity, work and cash flow each view is about**. A licence, payroll, payslip, employee record and payment are not interchangeable units. A decision to stop using a service is also different from the date its payment obligation ends.

## Strongest new analytical refinement: fees, wage principal and funding timing

A payroll payment service can require approved instructions and funds to be available in a specific account before disbursement. That creates a potentially important operating and liquidity condition. It does not independently create new wages, an employer credit facility or a new transformation expense equal to the full payroll.

For a real case, distinguish:

1. Fees and genuinely additional transition/operating payments.
2. The employer's existing payroll principal and any change in the amount actually owed.
3. Any change to when/where funds must be available, including verification and access/approval dependencies.

Do not count unchanged wage principal once as ordinary business expenditure and again as an incremental technology investment. Conversely, do not hide a materially changed funding-timing requirement merely because the underlying wages were already due. No interest rate, float period, credit availability or cash model has been assumed. This is an optional application of the existing three-view framework, not a fourth financial model or another mandatory interview question. R5M-C08.

## Change work and continuing work belong together

The retained Payfit text supplies a concrete illustration of active client cooperation and checks, as well as service allocation and contractual lifecycle conditions. A business case can understate retained work if it relies only on a service label. It can also overstate it by assuming that every customer performs the same tasks. The actual plan/contract and operating split are decisive.

Where AI is actually involved, this version's human-verification requirement is relevant to net effort. The distinction between administrator and employee controls also shows why a generic assumption that “AI can just be turned off” is unsafe. This is specific declared behaviour and contractual wording, not a tested capability, wider market rule or a recommendation to deploy it. R5M-C04.
