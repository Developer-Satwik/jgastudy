# R06 second challenge: changing alternatives and the observation boundary

29 October 2026. Internal desktop challenge. Two primary-route candidates plus a companion summary/authorisation concern one London Fire Brigade programme. These are not two independent employers or original JGA practitioners. No monetary totals, risk estimates, forecast outcome, supplier trial or external contact.

## What changes

The first-pass distinction between option, disposition, delivery and benefit remains sound. Add two selective checks:

1. Establish which alternatives were actually feasible at the particular event, and whether the obstacle would persist under another supplier or system.
2. Record the last evidenced event and the point at which observation ends. A projected date that has passed is not an observed result, continued delay or abandonment.

These refine the existing nine analysis rows. They do not create a new status, question, compulsory governance sequence or survival-statistics model.

## Application to the existing R06 analysis

**Feasible alternatives can change over time.** For a material event, preserve the actual option set, remaining support/contract window, relevant receiving capability and what maintaining service during transition entails. Identify whether a condition belongs to one proposal or would also affect alternatives. Do not claim the option set necessarily worsens with time or that every employer is locked in. H6S-C04.

**The last observed state is not an ultimate outcome.** Record the latest substantively evidenced event, its date and source, plus a later event only if supported. The decision can be known while later delivery is unknown. Do not turn a missed or passed forecast date into continued delay or failure without evidence. An interview’s current account can add later information; unaided knowledge and existing Q006–Q013 routes remain primary. H6S-C05.

A new approval, reset, procurement route or release decision in an older programme can be a substantive recent milestone under the unchanged instrument. The original programme start remains separate. None of these public records is entered into the six to ten original practitioner target.

## How this changes the response interpretation

A response should address the condition at the current decision, not promise that switching vendors will erase it. Possible actions include resolving an upstream condition, preserving support while work continues, re-scoping a phase or considering a genuinely feasible alternative. Those are analytical possibilities. Only the reported reset measures are described as adopted here; their successful effect remains unestablished.

For JGA, this makes the case narrative more useful than a list of reasons to stay or switch: it shows which choices existed then, what constrained them, what continued and where the evidence ends. It remains neutral about whether a hire, service change, process improvement or no new investment is the appropriate response.
