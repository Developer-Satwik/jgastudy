# R06 second challenge: lifecycle feasibility, access and handback

29 October 2026. Internal desktop research. Three new primary routes, one canonical reuse and seven bounded claim/design records. These are supplier declarations and explicit analytical applications, not observed employer decisions, a product trial or a representative market sample.

## What changes

R06 already separates the actual option, dated decision, delivery and benefit. The market check adds two selective questions for the analyst: **does the supposed retained option still support the required operation, and how will both current payroll and historical evidence remain usable through the change?** A continuing supplier relationship is not necessarily a continuing product. Data kept somewhere is not automatically data the employer can still retrieve, interpret or use in the receiving arrangement.

These clarifications fit the existing nine rows and core Q006–Q013. They do not add required interview questions, prescribe a migration, prove lock-in or establish that a provider change is superior.

## The stronger R06 distinction

Keep three requirements separate where material:

1. **Live operation:** a supported and applicable arrangement for the payroll, submissions, approvals and payment tasks actually required.
2. **Historical evidence:** access to required prior records, reports, documents and audit trail, in a form the relevant people can retrieve and interpret.
3. **Receiving setup and handback:** the actual data, configuration, permissions, retained responsibilities and verification needed for the next arrangement.

A declared export supports the existence of an export route, not a conclusion that all settings/history will import, reconcile or remain available. Equally, the receiving service does not necessarily need every historical record loaded into its live database: requirements can legitimately be met by separate secure, usable archives. Actual obligations, format and permissions must be established. M6S-C06.

These declarations also challenge a static “do nothing” comparator. A specific product's retirement can make unchanged supported operation unavailable. It does not select the supplier's promoted replacement or prove which choice a real employer made. Record the applicability and known notice date rather than infer a universal deadline or assume every employer must move at tax year-end.

## Separate the relevant clocks

The service's support/processing eligibility, notice and remaining commitment, old-system access/export, receiving-service readiness and first live run can have different dates and owners. They need not be forced into one deadline. An employer may cease production use yet retain notice obligations, or keep a read-only reference tool after changing processing. A client may also need to revoke old filing authority separately from activating the new arrangement. M6S-C05–C07.

This is a conditional analysis aid, not a tested transition checklist or remedy. The appropriate response can be a supported same-supplier migration, another provider, staged work or secure archival arrangements; none is recommended in advance of the actual case. The supplier declaration does not establish the employer's reason, rationality, demand, budget or resulting benefit.

## Version, access and inference controls

- The BrightPay legacy 2025/26 documentation contains older feature/CIS/access language. It was inspected only as a version crosscheck; those details are not merged into the current hub's claims. Existing BrightPay billing-period and Cintra universal-UI holds remain unchanged.
- Sage's UK public cancellation page was inspected through the original web reader; a direct raw request returned HTTP 403. The retained selected notes are paraphrases, not original HTML. A separate UK knowledgebase article was only a crosscheck. South African, Irish and US access rules are not imported into UK claims.
- The Payfit reuse preserves the original 27 October 2026 label versus the earlier reader-label discrepancy. Actual subscribed/executed terms remain uninspected. No notice/retrieval period, fee or liability estimate is adopted.
- A supplier's declared future support/access status is not evidence that any particular employer migrated, delayed or experienced a failure. No tested UI, contact, account, purchase, private contract or actual data export occurred.

## Implications

For practitioners, make the feasible option set and continuing evidence access explicit before classifying staying or changing. For suppliers, explain the actual handback, retained work and lifecycle conditions rather than promise an effortless move. For JGA, preserve the difference between capability needs and a recruitment recommendation: these conditions can be addressed in more than one way.

Original employer cases must later establish what was known, relevant, adopted and effective. No existing case disposition or questionnaire wording is changed here. The Table financial model remains separate and no inputs are populated.
