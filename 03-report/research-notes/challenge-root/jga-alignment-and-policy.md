# JGA alignment and policy challenge

28 October 2026. Internal second review. Public-source inspection and interpretation only; no new practitioner or buyer evidence.

## Scope test against Nick's actual brief

The accepted 27–28 October correspondence and agreement require two substantial report parts, a connecting conclusion and an independent commercial feasibility assessment. A report concerned only with approval mechanics would under-serve Part One. The decision-case emphasis is useful, but needs must be captured even if nobody has yet evaluated a product or submitted a proposal. Supplier implications need to follow from the diagnosed gap, not default to building more software. Original analysis, a reusable framework and a finished publication remain the deliverables, rather than an interview transcript compilation.

The earlier JGA AI visibility benchmark belongs to another project and is not evidence of payroll buyer behaviour. No earlier LLM answer should become a practitioner observation or payroll-market statistic here.

## JGA's own existing work changes the novelty test

JGA's July 2024-path AI ebook already covers technology, governance, skills, readiness and adoption. Its contents and introductory scope were inspected; its numerical examples have not been independently verified. Do not recycle those numbers as fresh research.

JGA's 4 March 2026 article describes strategy, data, skills and employee experience through its podcast editorial lens. It is evidence of JGA's current positioning, not a representative survey. The public podcast page describes an established expert-conversation resource. Published material can inform topic selection with attribution; it does not automatically count toward the new permissioned practitioner cohort.

PayrollOrg's 20 October 2026 primary announcement describes Nick/JGA Ventures' AI learning programme, including readiness, governance and business cases. This creates a useful boundary: the report should supply independent decision evidence and diagnosed barriers, while avoiding a predetermined conclusion that buyers need Nick's training. No programme purchase, access, efficacy or uptake has been evaluated.

The historical team independently found JGA sponsorship and Nick forewords in CIPP 2020 and 2022 originals. Root confirmed the sponsor statements in the originals. This supports a lineage/continuity lens; sponsorship is not authorship, ownership of data, or permission to resell the old reports. A small question for JGA, if useful, is what prior research users found valuable or missing; it must not replace independent assessment.

## A dated 2027 trigger is more useful than an invented adoption forecast

HMRC's interim manual, updated 23 September 2026, describes phased benefits-in-kind payrolling. The inspected phasing chapter distinguishes an April 2027 first phase and most remaining benefits in April 2028, with exclusions. It remains interim guidance and must be rechecked before final publication. It is a planning signal, not observed implementation or evidence that all employers need a replacement platform.

The preparation chapter specifically addresses timely data flows among benefits providers, HR, finance and payroll; software readiness; training; employee communication; and transition cash flow. That is a concrete counterexample to treating every unmet payroll outcome as a software feature gap. Its detailed rules are not being republished as tax advice.

The Employment Rights implementation collection was updated 22 October 2026. We inspected the collection and linked timeline as a freshness signal. It reinforces the need to distinguish in-force requirements, scheduled changes, consultation-dependent measures and speculation. Do not turn a timetable into a prevalence estimate or add a legal-compliance chapter outside the brief.

## Design implications

1. Keep the eight-chapter structure and both report parts. Make pre-proposal needs and the type of shortfall explicit within existing questions and analysis.
2. Ask what changed over the last few years only as a selective retrospective probe. Historic thematic repetition does not prove no progress; unlike surveys cannot form a numerical trendline.
3. Distinguish regulatory adaptation, replacement/lifecycle decisions and discretionary improvement. These conditions can overlap.
4. Record data ownership, employee outcomes and implementation responsibilities alongside product capability. Provider, employer and third-party boundaries matter.
5. For 2027–2028, use dated signals and conditional implications. No invented probabilities, adoption rates, sponsor revenues or participant claims.
6. Describe and manage JGA commissioning and commercial interests. Keep findings independent of recruitment, training or partner-sales preferences.
