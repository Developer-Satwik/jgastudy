# R04 decision-pathway cases

29 October 2026. Internal research notes for parent review. No client communication or account action. This strand examines who could make which decision, and the evidence used at that point. It does not estimate prevalence, value or supplier performance.

## What these cases establish

A payroll-related change contains several decisions with different owners. The strongest record here separates a functional migration signoff, a recommendation to go live, conditional backing and final go-live approval. The contrasts show an incumbent-support contract awarded under an officer delegation, and a later phase of an existing implementation receiving explicit additional funding. None establishes a universal CFO veto or a standard private-employer buying journey.

Three employer cases are selected. All are public bodies chosen because original decision/audit evidence was inspectable. They are mechanism illustrations, not a sample of UK employers. The Scottish audit and closure are two perspectives on one programme. The Argyll report and minutes concern one decision, not independent confirmations of market prevalence.

The record identifies Jo Brown, Assistant Chief Executive, using the HR executive-function delegation. It states that senior Finance, Legal and HR stakeholders reviewed the procurement business case and the Assistant Chief Executive signed it off, then records an award to Softcat, subcontracting to MHR, following framework further competition. The contract retains support/maintenance for incumbent iTrent through 31 March 2029. The stated rationale favoured continuity without substantial migration cost/disruption.

The useful interview distinction is between influence, delegated authority, conditional approval and actual execution. A single person may hold several roles, but record each role and its evidence separately. Public procurement routes, committee structures and expenditure rules cannot be imposed on private employers.

## What original practitioner input must establish

Within the existing R04 question, reconstruct one actual recent case rather than ask only who normally owns payroll:

1. What decision was being sought: new system, component, support renewal, process/service change or a further implementation phase? Was doing no more an acceptable option?
2. Who raised and shaped the proposal, and what evidence or operational problem caused escalation?
3. Who owned the budget, who authorised spending, who approved the supplier/route, and who could sign? What delegation or limit applied to this case?
4. Which operational, finance, HR, technology, security, legal or procurement inputs were recommendations, mandatory conditions or formal approvals? Can the participant describe an actual condition rather than infer a veto?
5. What was approved conditionally, what remained to close, who judged the residual risk and who finally authorised use? Did the actual sequence differ from the initial plan?
6. Who accepted handover and retained responsibility afterwards? What evidence would show the intended result separately from approval, purchase or launch?

Do not add a new questionnaire or presume participants have documents. Capture approximate dates and uncertainty when necessary. Subsequent R05 can value outcomes; subsequent R06 can examine barriers. No value estimates, universal risk thresholds or procurement recommendations have been added here.
