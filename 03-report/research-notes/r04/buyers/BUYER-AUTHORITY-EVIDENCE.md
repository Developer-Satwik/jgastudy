# R04 buyer and authority evidence

Internal research note | 29 October 2026 | Prepared for integration, not client release

## What the evidence changes

R04 should reconstruct a particular decision, rather than attach purchasing power to a job title. Three bounded cases supply contrasting examples: a finance-transformation team making a case to its CFO, a payroll manager excluded from the original provider selection but involved in remediation, and a payroll manager leading a time-and-attendance transition. These are supplier or adviser accounts of actual projects, not independent observation, a representative sample, or a prevalence estimate.

The important distinction is between identifying a problem, shaping requirements, securing permission to proceed, approving money, selecting a supplier, signing a contract, authorising go-live and accepting ongoing payroll risk. A source can establish one without establishing the others.

## Existing research recheck

### G2 / Paycom buying-logic study: context, not an authority map

[Original study](https://research-hub.g2.com/the-new-buying-logic-for-hr-and-payroll) and [publisher methodology](https://research-hub.g2.com/methodology), canonical SRC-0060 and SRC-0061.

The study describes 211 interviews with software-experienced professionals across roles, industries and employer sizes. Its displayed role mix includes HR, IT and Finance/Payroll; only 8% is labelled Finance/Payroll. Geography and fieldwork dates are not established. Its topics concern operating practices and evaluation considerations, not a disclosed case-by-case approval chain. Statements by people holding finance or HR titles do not show that they controlled a purchase.

G2 describes AI voice interviews with real participants, transcript coding and human analyst review. Its generic method says screening can include relevant experience or authority; that does not show every participant in this particular study had purchasing authority. Raw respondent-level records are not published for independent reconstruction. Paycom commissioned the study; G2 states editorial independence. Treat findings as reported perceptions/practices, and publisher implications as interpretation. Do not equate a preferred control with a formal veto or financial delegation.

Locators: study numbered capture lines 123-162, 191-228, 307-333, 369; methods sections 3.2, 4, 5.2 and 6. These routes are reused, not new independent sources. No new authority percentages are accepted.

## Three bounded decision cases

### BUY-S01: Purplebricks, UK, finance-led case for change

[ADP original case](https://uk.adp.com/resources/what-others-say/testimonials/p/purplebricks.aspx), section “Change of ownership leads to payroll rethink”, web-reader lines 59-63.

Tim Moyce, identified as Head of Corporate FR&A and Finance Transformation, describes a team reviewing suppliers after Strike acquired Purplebricks in May 2023. The team prepared a change case and presented it to the CFO, who agreed change was needed. Existing use of ADP at Strike and prior personal experience supported acceptance. This supports a reported finance-team case-building role and CFO agreement at that stage. It does not establish sole initiation by Tim, budget release, a competitive evaluation, final supplier approval, contract signature, or IT/security/procurement authority. Publication and implementation dates are not disclosed on the inspected page.

Evidence class: named employer testimony selected and published by the winning supplier. Local raw fetch was HTTP 403; the readable original-page web extraction is retained separately. No outcome or headcount claim is required for R04.

### BUY-S02: UK construction company, payroll excluded from selection

[CIPP original case](https://www.cipp.org.uk/best-practice/consultancy/case-studies/case-study-payroll-operations-post-migration.html), “Scene Setting”, “The Action” and “The Results”; numbered capture lines 8-20.

CIPP reports that the Payroll Manager was not involved in selecting a new service provider during a wider finance-system upgrade. Subsequent accuracy/control concerns led senior management to seek independent review. CIPP worked with the Payroll Manager and produced client/provider findings; the CEO then approved additional payroll resources. This distinguishes exclusion from the original selection, operational participation in remediation and an explicit later resource approval. It does not identify the original selector, original budget owner, signatory, procurement or technical approver. The employer is anonymous, its ownership and event date are not disclosed, and CIPP supplied the review. Do not attribute all later improvements to exclusion or treat the sequence as causal proof.

Evidence class: adviser-reported anonymous actual case with an unattributed manager testimonial; not employer-verifiable or representative.

### BUY-S03: Decjuba, non-UK comparison, payroll-led transition

[OAHI original case](https://www.oahi.io/case-studies/decjuba), dated 22 December 2025; “Background” and “The Solution”; numbered capture lines 8-25.

The supplier describes Payroll Manager Claire Nagle leading a time-and-attendance transition, her team's requirements, and a tender after which Decjuba selected OAHI. The account connects selection to her prior platform experience. This supports a reported operational leadership and evaluation contribution. The page does not name the formal final approver, budget holder, contracting party, security/procurement review or delegation threshold. A tender does not prove procurement owned the decision. This is an Australia-context retail case referencing the General Retail Industry Award, not evidence about UK market practice or a core payroll engine replacement. Event dates and the full tender evaluation are undisclosed.

Evidence class: supplier-published named case; selected positive account, not an independently observed buying process.

## Concrete R04 pathway fields

The following is a researcher-designed collection structure, not a discovered universal buying committee. Add an optional case worksheet or interviewer probes around the existing core questions; do not silently expand or replace the 14 agreed questions.

1. Decision unit: one dated change or considered change; country/entity, employer context, decision type, current status, scope and originating problem. Separate original purchase from remedial resourcing, change requests and renewals.
2. Participant involvement: what the contributor personally did, which stages they observed, and whether the account is first-hand, reported by someone else or inferred. Record title separately from authority.
3. Actions and actors: who raised the problem; developed requirements; compared options; built/recommended the case; sponsored it; controlled funding; approved selection; signed; set technical/legal/security conditions; authorised go-live; and owned operational acceptance. Several people can share an action, one person can combine actions, and some actions may not apply.
4. Mechanism of influence: specific input or evidence supplied, challenge raised, condition imposed, option changed, or decision delayed. Record what changed as a result when known. Attendance, consultation and job title alone do not establish influence.
5. Decision rights: formal delegated approval, advisory recommendation, mandatory concurrence, operational acceptance, or claimed informal influence. Ask the basis, for example direct involvement or a described approval process. Do not request confidential contracts, thresholds or personal names when role-level descriptions suffice.
6. Sequence and dependencies: whose input came before/after whose, where an option was sent back, and whether a condition was satisfied, waived, escalated or remains unresolved. Capture parallel paths and cases with no formal committee.
7. Boundaries and missingness: unknown, not disclosed, not asked, not applicable, not involved, and disputed are different values. Record authority changes by country, project scale or stage only when the account supports them. Leave untested dimensions blank with a reason.
8. Evidence and permission: source locator/date; anonymisation; whether the contributor agrees the case summary; quotation permission; uncertainty and contrary accounts. Do not count supplier/publication descriptions as new JGA participant contributions.

Recommended minimum useful case: a specific decision, contributor's direct role, at least one decision action linked to an actor, and a clear account of what is unknown. A complete committee map is not an eligibility requirement. Historical decisions can explain sequence, while a planned decision records intended rights only; neither substitutes for the other.

## Reporting guardrails and value

- Present a small number of decision pathways with their context and exceptions. Do not publish “the payroll buyer” or a universal hierarchy.
- Report people who execute payroll, system users and managers as distinct from people with selection, funding, signature or risk-acceptance rights. They may overlap; do not presume separation either.
- Report “consulted but excluded”, “recommended but not funded”, “approved additional resources” and “selected supplier” as different outcomes. This can expose gaps between operating responsibility and access to decisions without claiming a market-wide mismatch.
- Where new JGA accounts disagree, preserve both versions and the basis of knowledge. Do not resolve disagreement by seniority.
- Market studies can help design neutral probes; actual JGA decision cases must establish the report's original account. No former AI benchmark count, vendor ranking, economic return estimate or provider preference is buyer-authority evidence.
