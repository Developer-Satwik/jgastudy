# R04: decision rights and pathways

Research cut-off: 29 October 2026. Internal desktop work, not a client finding or an approved interview instrument.

The useful unit is a decision at a particular stage, not a person's title. A payroll leader can frame the requirement, select an option, control a delegated budget, recommend a supplier, sign a contract or accept an operational handover. Those are different actions. One person can hold several of them; several people can share or sequence them. The current evidence does not establish which arrangement is most common among private UK employers.

These are definitions and bounded workflow examples. They support disciplined reading of practitioner cases. They do not supply a prevalence estimate, a universal CFO veto, a mandatory payroll procurement committee, or proof that a particular role always has the final say.

## Proposed role map for analysing real cases

The following is an analytical vocabulary, not a required organisation chart. Leave absent, combined, disputed and unknown roles visible.

| Function to identify | Action to establish from the case | What must not be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initiator / requirement contributor | Raised the need; supplied operating requirements; proposed the options. | Being consulted means owning budget or approval. |
| Sponsor / accountable change owner | Advocated the change; carried the justification and outcomes; secured attention or resolved escalations. | Sponsor means contract signatory or unlimited authority. |
| Business / service owner | Owned the service need, acceptance criteria or ongoing service; could accept or reject a proposed service outcome within an actual remit. | Payroll, HR, finance or IT title identifies the owner automatically. |
| Budget holder / funding allocator | Could allocate existing funds, approve extra funds or reprioritise resources within a stated limit. Record recurring and implementation funding separately if they differed. | An allocated budget means a contract was signed or a go-live was accepted. |
| Proposal / investment approver | Authorised this proposal, stage or commitment, perhaps subject to conditions. Identify a person or body by function and its limit. | An early approval remains valid after material scope/risk changes. |
| Commercial / procurement lead | Ran market engagement, evaluation, negotiation or contract process; established any actual delegated decision or signature right. | Advising on procurement means owning the business need or having the final vote. |
| Authorised contract signatory | Committed the organisation to the agreement under its scheme of delegation. | A signature proves that person originated, selected or personally funded the change. |
| Assurance / specialist adviser | Assessed risk, controls, security, privacy, legality, architecture or readiness; issued advice, findings or recommendations. Establish any additional power to impose a condition or block separately. | A negative review is itself a formal veto or a reviewer can approve spend. |
| Delivery / implementation lead | Managed configuration, migration, integration, testing, readiness or transition. | Implementing a decision means having selected or approved it. |
| Operational authority | Accepted a handover, payroll result or payment release within an actual operational remit. | Routine payroll approval means authority to buy or replace the payroll service. |
| Escalation / exception authority | Resolved an exceeded limit, disputed condition or exception. Identify the trigger and resulting decision. | Seniority alone establishes escalation power. |

Code rights separately from influence: recommend; evaluate; allocate funds; approve a stage/commitment; sign; set a binding condition; formally stop; advise/challenge; implement; accept operations; authorise payment. Several rights may apply to one function. A participant's understanding is a useful account, not documentary verification of a delegation.

## Follow the route rather than impose a fixed funnel

Use the selected real case's chronology: need raised, options assessed, funding decision, commercial commitment, readiness/handover and operating review. Stages may be absent, combined, repeated or parallel. A renewal or a small change is not automatically a new major programme. A major programme can contain a qualifying recent renewal, re-scope or go-live decision even if the programme began earlier.

Three route hypotheses are worth testing, not reporting as established private-employer patterns:

1. A routine renewal might remain within existing delegated scope, but a material price, risk, service, data or contractual change could trigger a further decision. Record what actually changed and who judged the threshold, without assuming every renewal has the same path.
2. A small improvement might use a service owner's existing resources without a separate investment approval. Preserve the guide's valid state, no formal approval required, while establishing what authority allowed it and what constraints remained.
3. A multi-function replacement or programme might have several approvals: funding, commercial commitment and readiness acceptance. A conditional approval might require work before proceeding, rather than constituting either a rejection or unrestricted permission.

The government standards demonstrate proportionate and delegated governance, but their numerical thresholds and mandatory gates do not transfer automatically to private cases. Current GovS 008 also separates commercial assurance and expert advice from the designated approval mechanism; an assurance recommendation should therefore be recorded as an input unless actual decision power is established.

## What original practitioner cases must establish

Apply this under existing Q001, Q006-Q009 and Q013, with targeted clarification only. Do not expand the agreed 14-question core.

- Anchor one directly experienced decision or substantive recent milestone within the 24-month window. Record programme start separately from the selected event.
- Identify the action, approximate date, function and decision remit. Functions rather than names are enough; no confidential delegation letter or exact budget is required.
- Establish whether the function's authority is known first-hand, based on internal communication or the participant's interpretation. Preserve not known, not asked and prefer not to disclose separately.
- Distinguish formal authority from influence and practical dependency. If someone could stop progress, establish what they could stop, by what route, and whether that power was exercised or only believed available.
- For conditions, record who set them, who could clear them, whether they were resolved and what decision followed. A concern, unmet prerequisite, recommendation and binding condition are different codes.
- Record the outcome at that date independently of implementation: approved, pending, deferred, declined, abandoned or no formal approval required. Preserve any later change rather than overwrite the earlier stage.
- Seek counterexamples to a senior-finance-centred explanation: delegated decisions, owner-led renewals, payroll staff excluded from initial selection, cross-functional choices and retained arrangements chosen on merit. Do not recruit or code only major failures.

Suggested case-row fields: case ID; sector/jurisdiction; change type; selected milestone and date; function; action/right; authority basis and scope; threshold/trigger stated or unknown; advice versus binding condition; escalation; observed decision; subsequent implementation state; participant knowledge status; source/locator and attribution permission. These are analytical fields, not new mandatory participant questions.

## Integration and limits

Re-use existing R03 service-boundary evidence and the public/supplier case strands rather than count them as new employer contributions. A published company case can show a CFO's involvement without proving that all CFOs control such decisions. A reported payroll manager's exclusion can show that one selection route omitted payroll input without proving typical exclusion. Scottish Government recommendation, conditional backing and formal go-live events must retain their distinct dates and statuses.

The proposed 6-10 original employer-side contributions will provide qualitative variation, not representative percentages or saturation. R04 can establish who could do what in each recorded case and where the route changed. R05 will later examine evidence and full business-case economics; this note does not calculate ROI or give legal/procurement advice.

No client documents, canonical registers, consent wording or live outreach were changed in this strand. No original practitioner cases were collected.
