# R06: what published buyer evidence can and cannot explain

29 October 2026. Internal desktop research. One new original-report route, four canonical reuses and seven bounded claim/design records. No original practitioner, price forecast, supplier score or new questionnaire.

## Main finding

Published payroll research provides useful contrasts between functioning arrangements, dissatisfaction, consideration, anticipated triggers, tendering and reported implementation. It usually does not reconstruct a dated employer decision from initial need through authority, evidence, selected option and actual result. Use it to improve what we notice in a case, not to populate the explanation before hearing that case.

The strongest new check is the wording of Azets' E2. The June 2026 report asks a prospective change-trigger question. A later article's retrospective-sounding description must not turn that into evidence of why completed switches happened. This is a difference in inference, not an accusation that the underlying survey is wrong. BUY6-C05.

## What the new Azets report actually measures

The 34-page June 2026 publication describes a May 2026 survey of 205 C-suite executives, senior leaders and payroll decision-makers, conducted by Reputation Leaders for Azets. Method page 32 supplies overall country counts: UK 52; Ireland 30; Denmark 31; Norway 31; Finland 31; Sweden 30. It describes a substantial large-employer profile: 66% employ more than 500 people and 75% report turnover above €50 million. These are sample-description figures, not JGA payroll-market findings or within-country item bases.

The same page identifies four in-depth qualitative interviews with two Azets executives and two external opinion leaders to supply commentary. They are distinct from the survey base and are not this commission's original participants. The report calls itself independent research commissioned by Azets; that description does not provide external validation, a probability-sampling design or proof of representativeness. Recruitment mechanism, weights, item-specific valid counts/routing, country-specific item results and microdata were not established. The large-employer pooled sample is not automatically transferable to all UK employers.

PDF and printed page 13 contain E2: prospective conditions that could prompt provider or setup change. The page separately states that respondents had reviewed their arrangements in the last three years; that statement does not say which review ended in renewal, change, rejection or no decision. The report's opportunity-oriented response categories are useful counterweights to a failure-only interview, but not observed switching triggers.

Pages 29 and 30 ask about importance when evaluating providers and the extra value that might justify a premium. These are stated values/conditions, not tested price elasticity, a funded demand estimate or an actual transaction. Do not treat the report's provider-evaluation population as necessarily all 205 without item-routing evidence. A respondent can rate several things highly; a closed importance scale is not a causal decomposition of purchase decisions.

The original branded report was publicly indexed at an NTB distribution URL and captured without submitting a form. The Azets landing and later article are retained as companion publications from the same study, not independent corroborating datasets. Their displayed June/August dates are publication dates for those pages. ## Two mistakes the existing data helps us avoid

### Staying does not automatically mean a barrier

CIPP's combined satisfaction/no-change answer is counterevidence to universal dissatisfaction. It is not enough to label every retained arrangement as a successful renewal, but it reinforces the need to ask whether staying was preferred on merit, constrained, or both. Existing Q012 already allows this. Retaining the provider can also coexist with a funded process, control or capability improvement; no new platform is not equivalent to no investment.

### Experienced ease does not invalidate anticipated effort

Moorepay's ease item refers to switching experience, whereas the separate reasons concern people who considered but did not change. These need not be the same population or circumstances. The former does not prove that every prospective move would be easy, nor that an employer who retained its arrangement misjudged the decision. Do not subtract the two measures, combine them into a conversion rate or infer a causal role for cloud software or competition from their coexistence.

Fresh original charts V2-02, V2-03 and V2-05 were visually inspected. The annual prose still differs from the direction in the non-switch chart and from some barrier values in the chart/guide. Existing CLM-0051 and CLM-0052 therefore remain held. Neither averaging nor selecting the favourable publication would solve the unknown version/base problem. Other earlier Deloitte numerical holds are untouched.

## Application under the unchanged core

- Q006–Q007: establish the actual selected case, object and dated decision. A survey statement about intention or review cannot fill those fields.
- Q008: allow both problem-led and opportunity-led triggers. Do not read the survey's closed categories before the unaided account.
- Q012: retain adequate incumbent, process/capability improvement, alternative service and other feasible paths. Satisfaction, constraints and transition trade-offs may coexist.
- Q013: distinguish anticipated condition, actually encountered constraint, reported influence, proposed remedy and observed effect. A supplier's suggested solution is not an observed cause of progress.

These are interpretation rules, not additional compulsory questions. Unknown, not asked, not applicable and non-disclosure remain valid. No original case has been coded and no route through the practitioner guide has changed.

## Implications

For practitioners, a useful record identifies what was actually decided and why in that context. For suppliers, evidence and transition support must match the real condition; generic promises of an easy switch are insufficient. For JGA, the report can explain differences between unmet need and action without portraying every unchanged arrangement as a sales failure or every hypothetical interest as a funded opportunity.

The published research does not supply a defensible general rate of dissatisfaction-without-action, payroll proposals that fail to decide, or rejection caused by a particular barrier. Actual permissioned cases must later establish the explanation, including successful retention and action that did not involve a provider change.
