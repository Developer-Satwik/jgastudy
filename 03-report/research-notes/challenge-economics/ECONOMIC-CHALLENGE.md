# Economic and commercial challenge of the JGA research design

28 October 2026. Independent internal review. No client document changed, source promoted to publication approval, participant contacted or commercial commitment made.

## Conclusion

Keep the current two-part report and 14 core questions. The economic lens reveals a useful omission in the optional probes: we ask what triggered a proposal and whether delivery matched what was agreed, but do not explicitly establish the intended outcome, baseline or who would capture the benefit. A small wording change can make the eventual framework much more useful without turning the project into a cost survey.

The practical opportunity is not merely to say that payroll deserves investment. It is to distinguish a worthwhile improvement from a proposal that is affordable now, feasible to implement, and capable of producing an observable benefit. Those conditions can diverge. The Table must pass the same test: a report may be useful while an event is not worth its incremental cost, and a commercially attractive programme can still have an unmanageable cash requirement.

## 1 The CFO lens separates capacity from money

The Government Efficiency Framework, updated 24 November 2025, explicitly distinguishes direct spending reductions from monetisable benefits at unchanged spending. Its example is an IT system enabling staff to handle more queries without reducing the budget. It also requires baseline, counterfactual and actual spending evidence for a cash-saving claim. See EC01, section 4.1–4.4.

Inference for payroll: hours released may be valuable capacity for controls, queries or growth rather than cash returned to the business. We should not convert every hour saved into a claim of payroll cost reduction. Nor should we count time saved, productivity and headcount savings three times if they describe the same mechanism.

Refine Q008 with: “What improvement was the proposal meant to deliver, and how would you know it had worked?” Refine Q010 with a selective probe about the baseline and expected measure. Refine Q013 with: “Where did any time or capacity released actually go?” These are prompts, not requests for confidential budgets or a demand that every benefit be monetised.

## 2 Value, affordability and delivery readiness are different tests

The Green Book 2026 identifies value for money, affordability, supplier capability and achievability as separate critical success factors. It includes continuing current arrangements and a minimum viable intervention in option appraisal, and explicitly addresses uncertainty and sensitivity. See EC02, table 4 and chapters 5–6. This is public-sector guidance, not evidence that private employers use the same formal method.

Our interpretation: a participant saying “budget” may mean insufficient cash now, an unconvincing benefit, a different department bearing the cost, or lack of implementation capacity. Code the explanation rather than collapse these into a single funding barrier. Delaying an economically useful project is not necessarily irrational.

Use existing Q009/Q010/Q013 selectively: who bore the cost; who expected the benefit; whether the issue was total value, current affordability or delivery capacity. Retain broad bands or qualitative descriptions if the participant prefers. Do not add an ROI questionnaire.

## 3 Approval does not establish delivery or benefit realisation

The DWP Synergy summary business case was published 8 April 2026 after Treasury approval on 8 December 2025. It presents separate investment/running costs, alternatives, modeled cash/non-cash benefits, sensitivity and implementation plans. These are approved appraisals, not realised payroll savings. The unit is a large shared HR, finance, procurement and payroll programme, not payroll alone. See EC03, executive summary and sections 2.2–2.3 and 5.

Separately, the NAO's 6 March 2026 audit of the wider government shared-services strategy records improved governance and procurement progress alongside unresolved ownership, onboarding and interoperability risks. See EC04, printed summary paragraphs 7–9 and 22–23. This is not a follow-up evaluation of the completed Synergy programme and must not be narrated that way.

Together they support the structural distinction already in our guide: an approved proposal, a chosen option, implemented functionality and observed outcomes are different records. Add a benefit owner and measurement point to the case summary. Where there is no measured result yet, retain “not yet assessed” rather than implying success or failure.

## 4 Staying can be rational while also constrained

The Sourcing Playbook's established 2023 practice guidance discusses whole-life cost, early exit planning, data handover, transition resources and simultaneous management of the incumbent and replacement. It explains how inadequate procurement data can produce supplier risk pricing. See EC05, delivery-model assessment and chapter 13. Some legislative passages are old; do not use this edition as current procurement-law advice.

Inference: “kept incumbent” is not a sufficient explanation. Distinguish positive service fit, a consciously preferred minimum intervention, switching costs, incomplete data, contract timing and a transition that cannot safely be staffed. These are different reasons with different implications. Supplier refusal, unavailable exit data or contractual restrictions must be reported as the participant's experience unless verified independently.

Add selective Q012/Q013 prompts about whether staying was preferred on merit, constrained by the transition, or both; and whether contract/renewal timing affected the feasible alternatives. Do not label all incumbent retention as lock-in, and do not assume that a new platform solves process or data problems.

## 5 Outsourcing changes delivery but does not erase retained work

HMRC's current employer guidance states that an employer remains responsible for PAYE tasks even when a provider is paid to perform them, and that the level of provider support varies. See EC06, “Paying a payroll provider.” This is a specific UK PAYE statement, not a claim about every legal arrangement in every country.

Implication: compare the actual service boundary, not just “in-house versus outsourced.” What is retained internally, what has to be supplied to the provider, and what support remains necessary? Q002 already asks delivery context. Add a brief service-boundary probe where outsourced or mixed delivery is relevant. This helps explain a “provider shortfall” that may actually be an unclear scope, handoff or ownership issue.

## 6 Existing paid research makes differentiation more demanding

The Hackett Group's 8 July 2025 release markets a full 82-page payroll provider assessment and a complimentary registration-based summary. Its public account says 18 providers were assessed across 26 criteria using briefings and customer interviews. The full report and respondent bases were not inspected; do not repeat the headline cost or automation claims as general payroll benchmarks. See EC07.

APQC's payroll benchmark collection, updated 11 August 2026, advertises measures of cost, cycle time, process efficiency and staff productivity. Its public page is product evidence; numerical benchmark tables, sample sizes and actual paid terms were not inspected. See EC08.

Implication: generic operational benchmarks and provider comparison are established alternatives. JGA's useful contribution can be a decision case that makes evidence actionable, not another provider league table or a promise to supply the market's definitive benchmark. For The Table's buyer conversations, ask what specific decision this proposed depth would improve relative to the research or internal evidence the buyer already uses. The existence of paid research elsewhere does not validate JGA's price, demand or licensing rights.

## Changes worth making now

1. Add intended outcome and success measure to Q008; baseline/whole-life cost/benefit owner to Q010 as selective probes; observable benefit and remaining work to Q013. Preserve unaided answers and the 14-question count.
2. Clarify outsourced service boundaries in Q002. Keep the probe short and role-based.
3. Add contract timing and preferred versus constrained renewal to Q012/Q013. Avoid duplicating the existing migration/data/capacity probe.
4. In the report outline, make chapter 5 explicitly cover what “value” means and who captures it. Chapter 6 should distinguish operational go-live from realised improvement. No new chapter is needed.
5. Use a one-page decision-case card later: context; desired outcome/baseline; alternatives; decision authority; conditions/costs; decision and delivery state; result/measurement/owner. It is an analytical template to populate, not a current finding or new contractual product.

## Historical and future interpretation

The sources are not a time series. The 2023 sourcing guidance shows that transition and exit concerns predate current AI positioning; the 2025 efficiency definitions sharpen benefit treatment; the 2026 business case and audit show those tests still matter in active programmes. We cannot infer that barriers rose or fell from this sequence.

For 2027, prefer conditional scenario notes to manufactured projections. Examples for later evidence testing: a cash-constrained employer may favour a small process improvement; a legacy deadline may make a transition unavoidable despite moderate short-term return; a proven automation capability may release capacity only if data, controls and staffing allow it. These are analyst scenarios, not forecasts or measured prevalence. Ask what observable condition would change the decision rather than assign an unsupported probability.

## The Table model review

The existing financial specification already separates cash contribution, fully loaded result and funding requirement, avoids double-counting bundled licences, includes staff time and stress-tests receipts. Keep it. The main additional check is whether the incremental event improves an identifiable research or buyer outcome enough to justify the extra work and exposure. Research-only interest, council participation, hospitality appeal and sponsorship demand must remain separate evidence.

The current six buyer questions are adequate. In analysis, retain four levels: no use identified; concrete use identified; defined proposal requested; actual permissioned commercial commitment. Do not convert a positive conversation into validated revenue. Four exploratory conversations cannot establish a market conversion rate.
