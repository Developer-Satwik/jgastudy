# R02 Service boundaries and retained employer work

28 October 2026. Internal evidence assessment. No contact, form submission, account use, purchase or publication occurred. This is not legal advice, a supplier evaluation or evidence that the inspected services deliver their advertised results.

## Working conclusion

An outsourced payroll arrangement should be described as a set of responsibilities and service conditions, not a binary label. A reported service gap may be a missing capability, a package exclusion, a handoff problem, an expectation mismatch or non-performance against an agreed obligation. The response should depend on which explanation is supported.

The evidence now supports a practical extension to the R02 needs matrix: record who supplies the information, who validates it, who approves payment, who handles the employee, what service was actually included and how an exception is escalated. Establish these facts before attributing the consequence to the provider or recommending replacement. Actual employer cases remain necessary.

## The service gaps that matter to investigate

This matrix is analytical interpretation for later case investigation, not measured prevalence or proof that the mechanism occurred.

| Potential need | Boundary to establish | Consequence to examine | Evidence that would support the account |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Timely accurate inputs | Manager/HR/employee submission, employer validation, provider acceptance and the agreed cutoff | Missed amendments, additional checking, reruns or delayed correction | Input and acceptance timestamps, exception log, agreed calendar, actual case chronology |
| Clear review and approval | Who checks the preview, authorises the payment and owns changes after sign-off | An incorrect or incomplete output may pass through an unclear approval chain | Approval record, validation steps and which discrepancy was identified when |
| Employee query ownership | Employer-facing support versus direct employee contact, query types and included channels | Repeated handoffs, slower explanation or time diverted inside the employer | Query routing, repeat contacts, elapsed response and resolution time; the account of an actual case |
| Correction and exception service | Source of error, required action, scope/cost and escalation route | Additional administration, off-cycle work and unresolved employee consequences | Incident chronology, responsibility agreed, correction completed and who did the retained work |
| Useful reporting and control | Production of reports versus reconciliation, explanation and management information | Reports exist without someone using them to identify exceptions or support decisions | Defined report users, review routine, reconciliations and actions taken on exceptions |
| Continuity of service | Deputy coverage, access permissions, alternative contacts and recovery arrangements | A single absent approver or inaccessible account may interrupt a critical handoff | Authorised-role coverage and actual contingency exercise or incident evidence |
| Data access and visibility | What the employer can see/export, audit information and responsibility for retaining records | Delays answering queries, checking outputs or demonstrating what happened | Actual permissions, usable export and audit trail; do not rely only on a brochure statement |

The important distinction for support is the difference between acknowledgement or progress update, full resolution and the employee actually receiving the correct outcome. A response-time target alone does not establish the latter two. The Fourth definition provides an explicit example of that distinction; it does not establish how often delays occur.

## Avoiding an unfair provider-failure narrative

An explicitly excluded service is not automatically a failure to deliver, although a buyer may experience the exclusion as an unmet need. A missing capability may instead be available in another package or as a specialist service. An employer's delayed input also does not establish that all subsequent impact is its fault. The actual agreement, circumstances and actions need to be understood.

The Phase3 assurance tier is useful contrary evidence: reconciliation, controls and support can already be offered without replacing the entire in-house function. Whether a particular organisation needs that support, can afford it or obtains value is still unknown. The CIPP source also describes different service inclusions, so we should not claim all providers offer identical packages.

Published operational and accuracy targets should be kept separate from audited outcomes. The G-Cloud page does not demonstrate achievement of its targets. A listed export capability does not prove a particular customer can use its data successfully. An agreement's contingency clause is not evidence that a recovery test has succeeded.

## How this makes Part One more useful

For an employer, the needs analysis can identify a missing owner or unclear support entitlement before proposing a purchase. For a supplier, it can show whether the unmet need concerns scope, communication, proof of controls or execution. For JGA, it creates concrete explanatory cases and conditional recommendations rather than a generic statement that employers want a better service.

Within the existing guide, use Q002 for the actual delivery boundary, Q003–Q004 for the task and consequence, and Q013 for conditions and observed change. These are selective probes; no new numbered questions are required. A future case summary should distinguish facts supplied, participant attribution and researcher interpretation.

## Metrics worth asking about where already available

- On-time input, acceptance, approval and final payment stages, kept distinct.
- First response, progress update, resolution and employee outcome timestamps.
- Rework or additional processing events and the stated cause, without assuming causation from a label.
- Reports produced, reconciliations completed and exceptions acted on, as separate activities.
- Employer and provider hours where known, including the use of any released capacity.

Do not require confidential employee records, exact budgets or a new measurement exercise. Missing measurement is a gap in evidence, not proof of a poor service. Do not translate the categories above into a cost multiplier, churn estimate or market opportunity size.
