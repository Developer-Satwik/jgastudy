# R02 Capacity reporting and control needs

28 October 2026. Scoped desktop deepening. Internal evidence assessment, not a practitioner finding or final report chapter. No canonical files or client documents were changed.

## Judgment

The capacity question is which work must be completed, with which skills and dependencies, to deliver the required outcome. A simple employee-to-payroll-staff ratio cannot answer that by itself. The inspected sources support four operational needs: explaining pay and resolving queries; validating incoming information; maintaining controls and procedures; and providing technical/reporting capability across payroll and other teams. They do not establish one national staffing requirement or a causal estimate of the money lost to inadequate capacity.

## What the originals add

### Workload ratios need a scope and complexity check

CIPP's in-house report prints a median of 400 employees per pay professional, while acknowledging that its staffing presentation does not account for complexity. The report-specific base and comparable prior-year composition are not established. Do not use its year-on-year commentary to assert that individual teams absorbed a measured workload increase. [Original p4](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/472716226/4/).

The same report distinguishes monitoring timeliness, pre-payment errors, worker-impacting errors and query resolution. Those choices are not observed incident rates. [Original p10](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/472716226/10/).

### Roles show different work rather than the time each task consumes

CIPP's professionals report separates operational tasks from senior oversight. Query handling appears across operational roles; management and director descriptions include checking, analysis, projects and policy work. Its presentation does not quantify hours or demonstrate that a proposed intervention would release them. Role responsibility also does not establish final investment authority. [Original p7](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/471897251/7/).

### A small peer benchmark clarifies measurement boundaries

Deloitte covers 15 multinational mega-enterprises, with item-specific samples. Its staffing framework includes specialist and technical support beyond processing. The upstream-validation item has 13 responses. The inquiry service-target item has 12: six choose a 72-hour target, three no defined target, two longer than 72 hours and one 24 hours. These are target categories, not achieved resolution times or typical UK performance. [Original PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/docs/services/consulting/2024/us-2025-payroll-benchmarking-survey.pdf), pp3,8,12,24–26.

I visually checked pp 12 and 24–26. Avoid quantitative reuse of p25's query distribution: displayed counts sum to 12 despite a footnote base of 13. P12's combined support percentage also does not reconcile transparently with its subgroup figures. Neither was silently corrected.

### Desire for capacity and visibility is not realised improvement

ADP's official summary describes skills, global reporting, compliance oversight and employee-service priorities in its 2026 research. The source concerns 1,816 senior leaders across 20 countries, not an ordinary UK-employer sample. Exploration of fewer-person delivery is an intention, not demonstrated headcount reduction or efficiency. [Official summary](https://uk.adp.com/resources/adp-articles-and-insights/articles/g/global-payroll-survey.aspx).

The original PDF opened initially, but subsequent deeper requests timed out. No additional exact statistic is attributed to inaccessible pages. The summary is an additional route to the same dataset, not independent corroboration.

## Need to consequence to desired outcome

This is a mechanism map for the report and later cases. Consequences in the middle column are things to establish in context, not measured prevalence or guaranteed effects.

| Need | Consequence to establish | Desired outcome and useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity to explain pay and handle exceptions | Repeated work, unresolved issues or disruption to planned controls | Clearer answers and appropriate resolution; distinguish demand, target and actual completion |
| Ownership and validation of incoming information | Rework, compressed checking time or an unresolved handoff | Inputs arrive fit for use; establish responsibility, cut-off adherence and exception records |
| Preventive and corrective control information | Errors detected only after payment, or inability to learn from near misses | Differentiate prevention, worker impact and correction; examine the actual measure rather than a generic accuracy label |
| Sustainable operating coverage | Routine delivery or change work depends on an unavailable skill or person | Identify critical work, cover and specialist dependencies; do not infer a required hire from a ratio |
| Appropriate technical support | Change, testing or reporting work has no clear owner or is omitted from resource planning | Explicit retained work and cross-team support; include effort outside payroll in the case scope |
| Usable reporting and visibility | Leaders cannot answer a stated operational or business question on time | Establish the decision, data required, timeliness and source; a new dashboard is only one possible response |

## Implications for three readers

For payroll leaders, define work and outcomes before making a resource argument. Separate routine processing from queries, controls, change and specialist support. Treat capacity released at unchanged spending as a valid outcome without automatically calling it a cash saving.

For suppliers, make retained responsibilities and support assumptions explicit. An apparent feature need may concern configuration, explanation, support or an upstream owner. Whether a proposed response is worthwhile requires case evidence.

For JGA, the valuable contribution is the explanation of the constraint and its consequence. Public benchmarks provide context; the original cases should show what was happening, who could change it and how improvement would be recognised. Do not prescribe a universal staffing ratio or turn a skill preference into a vacancy forecast.

## What R02 can conclude now

The desk evidence is adequate to include capacity, queries, input validation, controls and reporting in the needs map. It also supports asking for the basis of a workload or performance claim. It does not rank these needs for JGA's proposed cohort or establish their financial magnitude.

No new broad survey is needed to close this desktop subtopic. The remaining evidence belongs to the permissioned practitioner cases: actual task and recurrence, workaround, source of the measure, consequence, responsible actor and desired outcome. Keep successful/adequate arrangements and non-quantified answers. The current selective probes can collect that detail without adding a separate questionnaire.
