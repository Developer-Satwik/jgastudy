# A second challenge of the JGA research design

28 October 2026. Independent internal review for the project team. No client approval, participant testing, fieldwork or external contact occurred. Recommendations below are analytical design judgments, not practitioner findings.

## Overall judgment

Keep the two-part report and the 14-question core. The current design is materially stronger than a generic technology-needs survey: it separates future intentions from recent cases, includes renewal and successful arrangements, and separates approval from delivery. Another layer of review does not make its market hypothesis certain. The original contribution still depends on the quality and diversity of actual contributions and the usefulness of the resulting framework.

The main improvement is to investigate what happens before a formal proposal exists. A second is to locate change across people, process, data, governance and supplier/technology capability, rather than implicitly making unmet need a missing-software problem. Both can be achieved through selective probes and coding, without adding core questions or interviews.

## 1 The absent proposal is a missing mechanism

Current Q006 selects a real recent decision, then routes participants with no such case to Q014. That is defensible for the decision-case base but can leave Part One disconnected from Part Two. A need may never become a proposal because it is not articulated, has no owner, is considered too small, is believed unaffordable, or cannot compete for attention. These possibilities are hypotheses to investigate, not findings about payroll employers.

Add an optional probe under Q004, or when no recent case exists: “Has this improvement been considered internally? If it has not been put forward, what has prevented that?” If it was raised, ask what happened next. Do not force a hypothetical business case or relabel a need as an abandoned investment.

Retain these as pre-proposal observations, with their own base. The lack of a recent decision is not missing data for every question and is not evidence that payroll is strategically ignored.

## 2 A capability gap can be a fit gap or an execution gap

Q004 already distinguishes unavailable from available-but-unsuitable solutions. Preserve that, then code the contributor's explanation more specifically when supported:

- A required capability is not known to be available.
- Available options do not fit the organisation's requirements.
- An option is considered unaffordable or its value is not established.
- Implementation capacity, skills, data or ownership is missing.
- Options have not been evaluated, so availability is not known.
- There is no material shortfall or the current arrangement is adequate.

Allow multiple codes; never infer the category from job title or our own product preference. “Not known to be available” is more defensible than “the market does not offer it.” Ask what information supports a perceived gap. This helps distinguish a market opportunity for suppliers from an internal preparation problem for employers.

Add to Q013 selective probes on training, user adoption and upstream data ownership. These are currently partly covered by skills/data/capacity, so do not create extra questions. A platform can be live while the workforce still uses manual workarounds; a delivery-status label alone cannot establish benefit realisation.

## 3 The affected employee is relevant even in employer-side research

The primary population can remain senior employer-side professionals. Expanding to an employee survey would be scope creep and would weaken the small cohort's focus. Instead ask Q003's consequence probe to cover, if relevant, who is affected: payroll colleagues, line managers, employees, finance or customers. Keep an operational metric, an employee consequence and the participant's interpretation separate.

Acas's current guidance explicitly connects payroll delay/error with communication, prompt correction and potential financial difficulty or bank charges. That is a useful authoritative consequence lens, not a prevalence measure or a basis for quantifying retention ROI. Original public employee research already in R01 can complement leadership accounts without pretending those populations are the same.

## 4 Seniority is useful access rather than automatic truth

A senior leader may understand authority and budgets but receive operational evidence through others. A junior operational specialist may understand workarounds but not the approval chain. Keep the agreed senior cohort, but code actual involvement from Q001 and ask whether an account is first-hand, an internal report, or the person's interpretation. Do not treat title alone as verification of the whole story.

JGA introductions are a practical and contracted access route. They may also produce a relationship-selected cohort or socially desirable accounts. Record the recruitment route, seek contrasting experience where feasible, and preserve dissent or no-problem responses. Do not claim that seeking variation has removed selection bias. Avoid a many-cell quota plan that 6–10 people cannot credibly fill. Prioritise a few meaningful contrasts: operating model, decision outcome and organisational context.

HM Treasury's current case-study guidance supports purposive contextual investigation using multiple evidence sources, while warning about generalisation. Its interview guidance notes differential willingness to participate. Accordingly, the final method should describe who actually contributed and relevant gaps. An internal framework can be useful without a representative sample; it cannot predict approval probability or establish a market-wide ranking.

## 5 Recall windows need a milestone rule

The 24-month window is a practical recall boundary, not an empirically validated optimum. Keep it as the default. However, a transformation may start earlier and have a meaningful approval, re-scope, renewal, delay or go-live decision within the window.

In the guide, treat a recent substantive milestone in an older programme as eligible if the participant was directly involved in it. Record programme start, selected milestone and approximate dates separately. The analytical unit is that milestone or decision, not the whole multi-year programme compressed into one status. Keep an older decision with no recent milestone as separately labelled context.

If a routine renewal is the most recent case, do not silently replace it with a more dramatic story. A relevant supplementary contrast may be captured briefly, labelled as a second observation, without double counting the contributor or making every interview twice as long.

## 6 Future value comes from conditional decisions rather than invented forecasts

Q005 appropriately asks about the next 12–24 months. It records priorities and commitment stages, not a forecast of actual expenditure. Add one optional follow-up: “What would have to change for this priority to move forward, or to become less important?” Record conditions and uncertainty in the contributor's terms.

The GO-Science 2024 toolkit distinguishes scenario thinking from forecasting, calls for affected perspectives and treats scenarios as possible futures. Its formal scenario exercise usually looks much further ahead than JGA's near-term planning window. Therefore adapt the underlying caution, not the whole workshop method: the 2027 report can include a compact outlook with observable developments, uncertain conditions, possible decision implications and signs to watch. Do not market this as a formal foresight study, assign unsupported probabilities, or extrapolate incompatible historical percentages into a trend curve.

Useful future-facing output within scope: a small “If this changes, reconsider that decision” table, grounded in checked policy/market developments and clearly labelled assumptions. Do not create four glossy future worlds simply because a 2x2 chart looks impressive.

## 7 Preserve genuine disagreement

CIPP's original November 2024 roundtable records differences between participants' interpretation of skills and the accompanying survey view. This does not identify the correct view or establish a new 2026 trend. It demonstrates why a consensus story can erase useful differences.

MHR's live primary research landing page is also a close overlap with the operational-to-strategic territory: its stated cohort is 250 payroll managers and senior leaders in the UK and Republic of Ireland; its proposition includes continuing manual work despite investment, employee impact and skills. The full method and instrument were not inspected. Use this as an additional overlap challenge and source lead, not a reliable headline-error benchmark.

Keep contradictory accounts in the evidence matrix. The final report may reasonably explain that different operating conditions lead to different appropriate choices. A framework that shows when a recommendation changes is more useful than an unqualified “everyone should automate” conclusion.

## 8 The Table must not manufacture support for the report

Current buyer/member guides already separate unprompted needs, the research offer and the event concept. Keep this separation. Use the following further controls in analysis:

- Practitioner input supports payroll findings. Buyer input supports an assessment of a defined commercial use; it does not validate the practitioner finding.
- A council prospect counts toward 6–10 only after an eligible, permissioned, usable report contribution. Membership interest alone does not count.
- Avoid selecting report conclusions because a prospective sponsor finds them commercially appealing.
- Record objections and what would make a buyer decline, not only requested features.
- Two positive buyer conversations cannot establish a market-clearing price, and a dinner preference cannot establish research demand.

No extra validation round is recommended under the current fee. Use the four permitted conversations well and state what decisions they do and do not support.

## Conditions that would weaken the central contribution

1. Most contributors cannot describe first-hand recent decisions or meaningful milestones.
2. The cohort comes from near-identical operating models and produces no useful variation.
3. Accounts remain general opinions, with no concrete evidence requests, chronology or conditions.
4. Outcomes are attributed to a business case without checking alternative explanations such as contractual necessity, leadership change or a mandatory deadline.
5. The framework merely restates a generic procurement checklist already available free.
6. Readers cannot point to an actual decision the finished report helps them make; buyers prefer existing free studies or consulting access.

These are checks to run, not reasons to abandon the design now. If they occur, reduce the strength of the relevant claim, improve the evidence within scope, or agree a design adjustment rather than compensate with confident language.

## Recommended changes before Nick sees the pack

Necessary design clarifications: add the pre-proposal route; specify gap categories as participant-attributed; clarify recent milestones in older programmes; retain first-hand versus interpreted evidence; state no-case and overlapping-contributor bases correctly.

Selective value additions: affected-person consequence, adoption/training/data ownership, a future-condition probe and a conditional outlook table. These fit existing topics and do not require more core questions, a larger sample or a separate futures study.

Do not change: the two report parts, From Ambition to Investment conclusion, target 6–10 practitioners, up-to-four Table conversations, six business-model comparisons, portable deliverables, or the separation of study design from actual findings.
