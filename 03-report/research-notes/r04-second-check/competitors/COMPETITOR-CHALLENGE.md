# R04 second challenge: a leadership brief is not a decision mandate

29 October 2026. Internal desktop challenge. All six earlier recruitment comparators assessed, with JGA as context. Two new primary routes, bounded reinspection of existing routes and the saved prompt bridge. No prompt reruns, participants, applications, accounts or external contact.

## What changes

R04's twelve-function map holds. The competitor and earlier-study evidence sharpen three selective checks without adding a function or changing the fourteen core questions:

1. Separate a **requested role/remit**, an **agreed or described delegation**, and a **right actually exercised**. A job advertisement can establish the first, while leaving the other two unknown. Record the relevant status rather than silently turn a recruitment brief into an organisational authority map.
2. Where an interim, vacancy, de-merger or succession matters, establish the effective period, changes to remit and handover of authority. A replacement appointment or reporting line does not by itself transfer every outgoing power. This is an analyst question for the actual case, not a claim that a particular employer had a delegation failure.
3. Keep the **object of acceptance** explicit. Approval of hours for payment, acceptance of a project deliverable, supplier selection and authority to commit funds are different acts. A person can hold several, but a record establishing one does not establish the rest.

No additional mandatory questions, confidential documents, personal names or exact authority thresholds are required. Apply these only under the existing role/case/authority/condition questions where relevant.

| S01 First global payroll leader | Who defines a new role, and which decisions belong to that role versus other functions? | The question already assumes a new UK-based global leader; S01-D explicitly starts from a CFO request. Neither establishes that this structure is necessary or that a CFO controls every relevant decision. |
| S05 Post-acquisition integration | Which authority relates to group standards, inherited providers, entities and continuing operations? | S05-D specifies a group CFO and one permanent director. Those researcher choices are not observed buyer hierarchy, a mandate to replace every local arrangement or proof that one person owns all rights. |
| S10 Interim cover and succession | What authority exists during the interim/vacancy, for how long, and what is handed back or changed? | The scenarios specify six-month director cover and permanent succession; S10-D names a CFO and talent team. A temporary senior role is not proof of a complete transfer of powers. |

This is a useful disconfirming lesson from the previous study: repeated finance-centred language can reflect how **we wrote the prompts**. It cannot corroborate finance dominance in the market. Obtain the practitioner's unaided need and actual route first. Use these constructed contrasts only to challenge analyst coverage; do not read them as mandatory interview scenarios or ask respondents to validate them.

The named firms and old cited routes remain discovery leads requiring original inspection. Generated claims about people, affiliations, fees, availability and capability remain unadopted. The earlier recommendation scorecard does not supply evidence for R04.

## Why this makes the report more useful

For a practitioner, the useful question is what a proposed or existing leader is actually authorised to do, not whether the title sounds strategic. For a supplier or adviser, identify the decision and acceptance scope before treating a supportive contact as the buyer. For JGA, a role brief can be made more precise about mandate and handover, while the research still tests whether capacity or a hire is needed at all.

Keep this as an overlay on the existing decision object, date, function, knowledge-basis and condition fields. Do not add a new mandatory hiring chapter, payroll-buyer archetype, confidence score or approval hierarchy. R05 will assess the evidence and economics that specific decision-makers need; this note does not price a role or value a benefit.
