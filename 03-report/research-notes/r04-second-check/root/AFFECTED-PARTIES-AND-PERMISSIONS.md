# R04 second check: affected parties and permissions

29 October 2026. Internal targeted check. This is a proportionate case-analysis lens, not an employment-law review, privacy audit or instruction for a particular employer.

## What the first map could miss

R04 separates internal functions well, but a case can have relevant parties outside its internal purchasing group. A corporate approval does not establish that every related action is authorised. First identify what the change actually affects, rather than impose a consultation or regulatory route on every software purchase.

Two original guidance contexts add a useful distinction:

- Acas addresses genuine changes to employment terms, including whether such a change is needed and the applicable direct/representative consultation and agreement route. That is a different object from buying software. Relevant collective arrangements affect who can represent affected employees. Do not infer either universal employee veto over system selection or that managerial approval settles every employment-term change. SC4-ROOT-C01.
- ICO's monitoring guidance separately discusses worker input, specialist advice, conditional regulatory consultation and the basis for processing. Seeking views, agreeing contract terms and data-protection consent are different concepts. The page is explicitly under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act; exact legal statements require a final-use refresh. Do not turn it into current employer-specific advice. SC4-ROOT-C02–C03.

No threshold, sanctions, deadlines, legal strategy or complete compliance checklist is extracted. Monitoring or contract changes are not assumed to occur merely because payroll software changes. These sources supply conditional reading questions, not evidence of their prevalence in the target population or actual rights in any interviewed employer.

## Proposed selective refinement

Add an **affected-party and external-permission** condition to the existing analytical record:

1. Does the specific proposal affect employment terms, monitoring/data use or another relevant commitment? What is actually changing?
2. Which affected-party input, representation, agreement or external condition was identified in this actual case, by whom and on what stated basis?
3. Who owned addressing it, what could they decide, what was resolved and what remains unknown?

Use existing Q008–Q013 only where relevant. This is not a new mandatory question set, legal eligibility test or demand for private legal advice/documents. Record participant understanding as understanding. Where a condition's applicability is unestablished, preserve that status rather than label it a veto or treat it as satisfied.

The distinction also protects positive simple cases: a scoped configuration change need not be inflated into a major transformation merely because some other case had wider conditions. Conversely, a budget or platform decision should not silently absorb a separate change to terms or data practices.
