## Analytical implication for JGA

This source family is useful background, but repeating operational indicators would provide limited differentiation. The stronger research hypothesis is to reconstruct a specific investment decision: how a measured problem became a priority, who controlled the decision, what evidence changed it, and why the outcome was approval, delay, renewal or abandonment.

That is an opportunity to investigate, not proof of an unserved market. Other source families may already answer parts of it. A professional's responsibilities do not automatically identify the budget owner. A KPI is not evidence that changing it secured funding. A provider charging method is not evidence that buyers will pay for JGA's research.

## Questions to carry forward

1. Which operational problem in a recent real case had a consequence serious enough to seek budget?
2. What evidence was sufficient for the approver, and what remained unconvincing?
3. Who sponsored the request, controlled the money, approved it or could veto it?
4. Why was doing nothing or renewing a sensible alternative, if it was?
5. What provider implementation or service evidence changed the decision?

These are analytical prompts, not a final interview guide or a claim that CIPP failed to investigate them elsewhere.
