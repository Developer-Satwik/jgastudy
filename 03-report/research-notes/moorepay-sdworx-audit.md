# Moorepay and SD Worx landscape audit

28 October 2026. R01 source review only. No interviews, forms, purchases or client contact. Canonical registers are unchanged; the companion JSON is for parent reconciliation.

## Moorepay original annual report

[Original public HTML report](https://www.moorepay.co.uk/annual-payroll-survey-report-25-26/) and embedded charts inspected. The voluntary online sample is mixed payroll/HR/Finance, not specifically senior investment approvers. It covers capability requests, delivery models, provider tenure and switching. Funding/approval details remain shallow. Exact fieldwork dates, item bases, full routing and weighting are unestablished. Supplier funding and promotional interpretation matter.

Its automation/reporting requests are useful context. Switching experience supplies counterevidence to an assumption that every transition is difficult. Selected percentages need qualification; several text/chart conflicts require quarantine.

## Resolve the switching ambiguity

The original switching-consideration graphic supports the direction used in the [switching guide](https://www.moorepay.co.uk/the-ultimate-guide-to-changing-your-payroll-provider/), while annual narrative reverses it. The JSON preserves both representations. This resolves which display says what, not the underlying questionnaire or denominator. Do not select whichever wording suits JGA's thesis.

Funding-barrier figures also conflict across narrative, graph and guide. Their version/base cannot be reconciled here. These are not defensible evidence that funding is a minor obstacle. The guide is a commercial explanation, not a new independent dataset.

## SD Worx UK release

[UK publication](https://www.sdworx.co.uk/en-gb/press/2026-04-21-uk-employers-accelerate-move-full-payroll-outsourcing) provides a UK HR subgroup and exact fieldwork dates. Current arrangements and preferred future arrangements must remain different measures. Integration is a stated priority, not proof of an approved project. The release alone does not supply respondent-level approval histories or the full question routing.

## SD Worx original reports

The official [corporate landing page](https://www.sdworx.com/en-en/resources/payroll-reward/hr-payroll-pulse-2026) links publicly to a 38-page [full report](https://www.sdworx.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Corporate_HRPayroll_Pulse2026_Report.pdf) and 12-page [payroll thematic report](https://www.sdworx.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Corporate_HRPayroll_Pulse2026_Payroll.pdf). Both were retained. Method/sample pages and relevant tables were text-inspected; thematic pages 2 and 7 and full-report page 32 were visually checked.

The full appendix separates national samples and employee/employer measures. The companion report identifies an anonymous Bilendi online panel analysed by SD Worx. Weighting and item-level valid counts remain unclear. Broader HR respondents are not equivalent to specialist Payroll Directors.

The material already connects payroll priorities with reliability, control, connectivity and insight; JGA cannot claim this broad connection is absent. Benefits and positive operational experience should temper a universal-failure narrative. Do not equate failure to agree with experiencing payroll errors. The reports and UK release are one study family.

## What remains valuable for JGA to investigate

The strongest next contribution is a concrete recent investment decision: initial need, evidence requested, who funded/approved/vetoed it, implementation conditions, alternative options and actual outcome. Ask about approval, retention and successful implementation as well as delay or abandonment.

This is an analytical differentiation hypothesis, not proof no other research addresses it. The reviewed sources are useful contextual evidence, but they do not substitute for those accounts or establish representative demand for JGA's future product.
