# R05 economics: evidence before a return claim

29 October 2026. Internal desktop strand. Three new primary guidance/educational routes and reused Green Book SRC-0005. No payroll ROI benchmark, employer forecast, financial workbook, original participant or investment recommendation is produced.

## Main judgment

A credible case needs a defined decision, counterfactual and time horizon before a headline saving or return. Keep three views separate: whole-life resources/cost of the option; future incremental cash consequences; and the funding/budget profile needed to deliver it. A measure can be useful without answering all three questions.

Time released, capacity available, a budget reduction and an avoided future cost are different claims. They may be valuable, but their conversion into cash depends on an actual change to expenditure or income. Implementation and recurring work, delayed benefit, ongoing support and exit/handback can change the result. R04's decision object and effective remit identify who needs the evidence and who can act on it.

## A practical economics evidence checklist

The following is researcher-designed case analysis, not a mandatory questionnaire, financial model or assumed employer process. Ask only relevant clarification under existing Q010–Q013. Do not require confidential forecasts, full budgets, contract documents or precise salary data.

| Evidence question | Useful record in an actual case | Error it prevents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What choice is being assessed? | Decision object, scope, minimum necessary outcome and feasible alternatives from R03, including supported retention or smaller improvement. | Treating a preferred product as the only possible response. |
| Compared with what? | A dated baseline and credible expected path without this option; distinguish current performance from future BAU and do-minimum. | Attributing every subsequent improvement to the purchase or assuming BAU is zero cost/no change. |
| Whose cost and benefit? | Relevant employer/group/entity, employees/managers, provider or other affected party; intended beneficiary and resource owner. | A central-team saving concealing transferred work or a supplier fee treated as no employer cost because it is a social transfer. |
| What horizon and basis? | Common comparison period; development, transition, operating, maintenance and exit effects; price base, currency and cash dates where relevant. | Comparing a first-year promotion with recurring cost or mixing nominal and real forecasts. |
| What actually changes in cash? | Incremental payments/receipts avoided or caused by the decision, their timing and basis; distinguish unavoidable historic/committed amounts. | Counting depreciation, an unchanged allocation or already-spent fees again as a new cash requirement. |
| What resource is used? | Existing staff time, constrained expertise, training, temporary cover and recurring review/operating work, with an alternative-use description where relevant. | Declaring existing resource free simply because no additional invoice is raised. |
| What kind of benefit? | Cash release; capacity/productivity; cost avoidance against an evidenced path; quantifiable non-monetary result; qualitative improvement. Record potential/forecast versus observed status. | Multiplying saved minutes by salary and presenting the product as an actual budget reduction. |
| What enables realisation? | Required adoption/process change, person/function able to act, baseline/target, assessment period and measurement plan; overlapping claims identified. | Counting the same time reduction, FTE reduction and output improvement as three additive benefits. |
| What remains uncertain? | Material assumptions and supporting basis, reasonable ranges/scenarios, known limitations and the variable that could change the choice. | A precise point forecast or a generic optimism adjustment standing in for evidence. |
| What cannot sensibly be monetised? | Relevant control, accuracy, continuity or employee-experience outcome; evidence, conditions and trade-offs in its own units. | Inventing a fine probability, reputational value or sales conversion to make a ratio positive. |
| Can it be funded and delivered? | Resource/cash profile, commitments, dependencies, capacity, contingency treatment and relevant approving remit. | Confusing an attractive whole-life return with available funds or deliverable benefit. |

This applies to incremental improvements and renewals as well as replacements. A full major-project model is not required for every small delegated decision. A necessary outcome can have several delivery options, and an unmonetised benefit can remain material without fabricated financial precision.

The comparison baseline and a feasible recommended option are different. BAU can evolve through already-planned maintenance, staffing or regulatory preparation; it is not automatically a frozen world with no spending. If an applicable requirement makes unchanged operation unacceptable, describing that counterfactual does not recommend remaining noncompliant. Establish the requirement for the actual case, then compare credible ways to meet it; necessity does not prove that an expensive replacement is the best choice.

## Keep the measures intelligible

| Label | What to establish | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-life cost/TCO | Included resource categories, service boundary, horizon, volume, timing and any excluded costs; state whether discounted. | Benefit or affordability merely because a total is lower. |
| ROI or ARR as reported | Actual definition, numerator, denominator, horizon, cash/profit basis and whether gross or net. Do not silently rename ARR, benefit-cost ratio or time-value estimate as cash ROI. | A comparable percentage when definitions differ; actual realised cash or automatic approval. |
| Simple payback | Cumulative relevant cash recovery and timing convention; say if recovery never occurs within the stated period. | Whole-life value; it can omit important later cash flows and does not itself discount. |
| NPV | Dated incremental net cash flows and the agreed rate consistent with price/tax/cash basis. Positive/negative is forecast under assumptions. | Available cash, a delivery plan, non-monetary outcomes or authority to proceed. |

No universal return target, hurdle rate, appraisal horizon or cash-conversion factor is prescribed. Borrowing the Green Book's social discount rate for a private employer would mix perspectives. A fixed allocation or depreciation may be needed in an accounting/resource view even though it is not an additional cash payment; keep the views reconciled instead of erasing costs from every presentation.

## Useful distinctions for original case coding

Record the benefit's status and the basis of knowledge: a hoped-for feature, an estimated effect, an approved benefit plan, a capability enabled, a result observed, or a cash effect supported by expenditure evidence. These are analyst distinctions, not a new validated maturity score. Do not infer the later states from the earlier ones.

Preserve the same distinction when costs change: a budget, supplier quote, contractual commitment, invoice, expenditure and forecast-to-complete describe different states. Historical spend remains relevant to accountability and funding reconciliation, even when it cannot be changed by the next decision. Recoverable amounts, future cancellation or exit charges and resources with alternative uses need their actual treatment established; do not label all legacy cost sunk.

Avoided hiring or avoided spend requires a credible counterfactual, timing and an actual action/assumption. It is not the same claim as an observed reduction in current payroll cost. A compliance/control objective can be material in its own right; the study should identify the relevant requirement and alternative ways to meet it, not manufacture fines avoided.

## Handoff to R05 synthesis

Carry a short evidence requirement into the central checklist: decision/alternative, baseline/counterfactual, horizon/perspective, full resource/cash boundary, benefit type/status, owner and realisation conditions, measurement/attribution basis, uncertainty and funding timing. Actual missingness remains visible. R05 does not calculate a payroll company's ROI or establish why a particular buyer declined; original case evidence and R06 will address those questions in context.
