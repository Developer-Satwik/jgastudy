0066 The live phase is about supporting the service in a sustainable way, and continuing to iterate and make improvements. You’ll also:
0067 continue to address any constraints you identified at beta
0068 continue to develop the service and work with other organisations providing services that are part of the same journey, so that you’re iterating towards solving a whole problem for users
0069 transition or integrate any existing transactions that meet a similar need to yours - making sure that what you end up with has a scope that makes sense to users
0070 You’ll have your live assessment at the end of the public beta phase. Spend public beta preparing for live.
0071 You can download a poster that explains what the live phase is for.
0072 Running your service during live
0073 You’ll need to work out how to manage and improve your service during live. This does not necessarily mean having an agile team on the service 100% of the time. Spend time during public beta working out what level of continuous improvement it makes sense to support, and who you’ll need on the team.
0074 As in beta, improvements you make during live should be:
0075 based on user research
0076 tested to make sure they work with different browsers and devices
0077 tested for accessibility
0078 quality assured
0079 You should also make sure you are clear on the effects that changes will have on offline channels, or any related services - and make sure none of your changes will have a negative impact.
0080 You’ll also need to spend time during public beta reviewing the performance metrics you set to check the data you’re collecting will tell you whether your service is performing as it should.
0081 Meeting the standard to move into live
0082 Before the service moves into the live phase, you’ll need to show that you’ve used the beta phase to build a service that meets the needs you identified at discovery and alpha, testing and iterating based on what you learn.
0083 Solving a whole problem for users
0084 Once you’re in live, you’ll need to continue to develop your service so it forms an intuitive part of the user’s wider journey.
0085 This means continuing to work with teams responsible for related services, and continuing to address any constraints affecting how you can iterate the service.
0086 During public beta, start developing a roadmap for this work. Items in your roadmap should be ambitious, at the level of things like:
0087 continuing to make sure that transactions are scoped in a way that makes sense to users
0088 starting a service community (or joining an existing one) to support others working in your problem space
0089 continuing to address any constraints you identified at beta (for example, with legacy technology)
0090 Providing a joined up experience across channels
0091 Before you move into live, you’ll need to make sure the people who support your users understand the online part of the service.
0092 This might involve things like updating call centre scripts, providing training or finding a way for operations staff to walk through or familiarise themselves with the service.
0093 Other things to consider before you move into live
0094 Before the service goes live, you’ll also need to make sure:
0095 you’re securing the service’s information
0096 you’ve got appropriate metrics in place to measure the success of the service, based on what you’ve learned during beta
0097 the service meets accessibility requirements
0098 you’re able to maintain uptime and availability and monitor the status of the service
0099 you’re able to continue with vulnerability and penetration testing
0100 you’re able to continue testing service performance
0101 you’re able to maintain quality assurance
0102 you have addressed, or have plans to address, any pain points that might lead to people being excluded
0103 If you’ve created any new design patterns - or learned anything useful about an existing design pattern - you should share what you’ve learned through the GOV.UK Design System.
0104 When you need to retire your service
0105 You need to retire your service if you find out users do not need it anymore - or that so few users need it that it’s not cost effective to keep running it in its current form.
