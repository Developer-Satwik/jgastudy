# R05 delivery evidence: make the proposal executable

29 October 2026. Internal desktop strand. Two new guidance routes and four reused primary sources; no product trial, practitioner contribution or organisation-specific go-live decision.

## Judgment

A business case needs evidence that the chosen change can be delivered while essential payroll work continues, and then operated with the people, controls and support it requires. A risk list, training catalogue, agreed timetable or product demonstration is not evidence that the relevant condition has been met. Conversely, readiness does not require pretending that every issue is absent: the actual decision needs scoped results, known exceptions, owners and a basis for proceeding.

Use R04's decision object and effective authority. Evidence proportionate to permission to investigate differs from evidence for contractual commitment, a migration release or a live operating handover. Do not convert the following analytical checklist into compulsory gates for every configuration change, renewal or process improvement. The R03 options remain alternatives and complements; a supported incumbent or smaller change can be the appropriate response.

## Selective business-case and readiness checklist

The following rows are researcher-designed applications of the evidence, not a discovered universal process. Ask for an account of what existed and mattered; do not require participants to provide confidential test packs or internal contracts.

| Area | Evidence worth establishing for the actual decision | Distinction to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope and operating result | Relevant entities, people, cycles, tasks, interfaces and outputs; current option and what will remain unchanged; what successful operation means. | A branded solution, module list or supplier promise is not an accepted operating scope. |
| Data and migration | Relevant identity/mapping rules, data quality, reconciliation and exception ownership; rehearsal results where migration is involved. | Data loaded or a migration script completed is not correct downstream payroll, reporting or reconciled balances. |
| Testing and acceptance | Requirements and representative normal/exception cases, appropriate data/environment, results, unresolved items and the relevant business acceptance. | Planned test cycles, a supplier demo and a ticked template are different from executed checks. If parallel comparison is part of the chosen plan, establish scope, differences and acceptance; no fixed number of runs is imposed. |
| Cutover and continuity | The actual sequence, period/cut-offs, responsible parties, verification, remaining activities, contingency/recovery approach and authority to proceed. | A calendar date or go-live approval does not establish all issues are closed. The case determines which recovery route is feasible; do not assume a universal rollback. |
| Operating and change capacity | Who keeps current payroll reliable while migration, testing and training occur; the contribution required from payroll, HR, finance, technology, managers and providers; availability and cover where needed. | An allocated budget or named team is not protected working time or a tested delivery capability. Cover is a resource question, not automatically a recruitment conclusion. |
| Practical learning and user readiness | Time to learn, role-relevant practice, usable guidance and the ability to complete tasks; relevant employee/manager support arrangements. | Training being available, attended, understood and usable in complex cases are different states. |
| Support and handover | Incident/exception ownership, priority, resolution, escalation, access and knowledge transfer; transition from project support to sustainable operation. | A response target is not resolution; temporary support pressure is neither automatically permanent nor automatically gone at the end of a named support period. |
| Dependencies and risk | External/internal dependency, current status, owner, interaction, planned versus operating mitigation, residual condition and escalation/decision route. | A colour/confidence score or generic register does not by itself show tolerability, mitigation effectiveness or accountable ownership. |
| Benefits transition | When the required functionality and adoption are expected, baseline/measure/owner and what observed result would support the claimed benefit. | Launch, usable service, released capacity, spending reduction and demonstrable benefit are different claims. The economics strand should value only the appropriate category. |

The checklist can apply proportionately to current-configuration, process, delegated-service or capability changes. It does not assume every case involves a platform migration, a support surge or an additional hire. R5DEL-C10 records the analytical status of this design.

## Readiness as evidence, not a label

For a material condition, retain the evidence state: proposed plan; work in progress; executed result; unresolved exception with owner; accepted residual condition; or not established. The same test name can conceal different scope, data or environment. Record the as-of date and which decision the evidence was meant to support, then what was actually authorised. Do not infer that a reviewer controlled funding or go-live merely because they produced assurance.

At proposal stage, some delivery assumptions legitimately remain to be resolved. The useful business-case question is which ones are decision-critical, who owns them and when stronger evidence is required before the next commitment. A conditional approval is not evidence of completed delivery. No universal test completion percentage, pay-error tolerance, parallel-run duration, recovery target or residual-risk score is created.

## Implications and remaining work

For practitioners, connect the proposed benefit with the real work and capacity required to reach it. For suppliers, make scope, dependencies, evidence and handover explicit rather than rely on broad implementation or support labels. For JGA, this can distinguish operating cover, change expertise, enduring leadership and a delivery team without presupposing which response the employer needs.

The Scottish positives remain alongside the limitations, and the adequate-incumbent alternative remains in the comparison. These selected public cases and guidance cannot establish private-employer failure rates or typical costs. R06 will later examine reasons for staying, switching or delay; this strand does not attribute such outcomes or infer a staffing market.
