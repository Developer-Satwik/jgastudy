# Detailed execution plan

Version 0.1 | 28 October 2026 | Owner Satwik Kumar and Riseklix

## The outcome

Produce the finished research asset Nick commissioned and give him a defensible decision about The Table. The report must help payroll leaders understand how to convert needs into an investable case, while helping suppliers understand actual purchasing barriers. The feasibility work must establish whether monetising that intelligence, with or without events, is commercially credible and manageable.

The approach is a structured, documented evidence review plus a small purposive qualitative study. It is not a representative market survey or a claim to have exhausted every publication. We will investigate problems one by one, compare perspectives, preserve conflicting evidence and keep the final recommendations connected to their sources.

## What is fixed and what we still need to decide

The latest agreement defines two workstreams: US$1,000 for the inaugural report and US$500 for The Table. It targets contributions from 6–10 distinct verified senior payroll professionals. The Table includes up to four exploratory conversations, ideally two buyers and two prospective council members. Relevant council-member conversations can serve both purposes with permission. Two buyers do not automatically count as senior payroll practitioners.

The report title is the 2027 edition/outlook. Evidence and fieldwork will retain their actual dates. The final length and format details will be agreed at the first-week checkpoint, not silently replaced with an arbitrary page limit.

The user reports signature and the US$750 upfront payment. The local agreement is the generated version; the executed DocuSeal copy and audit record should be retained when available. We can begin desktop work now. The formal kickoff, checkpoint and delivery dates remain to be confirmed with Nick, rather than inferred from the date of payment.

Working proposal for the first-edition population: senior employer-side payroll decision-makers, with UK relevance and international responsibilities where appropriate. This is a proposal to test with Nick, not an approved geographic restriction. Sources from other countries can inform context if their populations and transferability are explicit. Commercial research buyers and practitioners are separate audiences with different questions.

## Stage 1 Map what is already known

Start with R01. Review current professional-body research, vendor-funded studies, analyst research, implementation evidence and available buyer decision material. Use discovery pages to locate original reports, then examine their actual methods and relevant tables before relying on their numbers.

For each existing study, ask:

- Which payroll population, country, employer size and delivery model does it cover?
- Does it measure actual behaviour, past experience, attitudes, future preference or a funded plan?
- What does it say about needs and what does it leave unexplained about approval or non-purchase?
- Are the questions, dates, recruitment and denominators available?
- Is this a distinct dataset, or a press release repeating a study already in our register?
- Does it serve the same reader and commercial decision as JGA's planned report?

Build a topic-by-source map. Search both for evidence that supports a proposed gap and for evidence that the gap is already well served. The novelty hypothesis is not established by finding a catchy title or failing to find a source in one search.

Sources already queued include CIPP, Moorepay, SD Worx and Payroll Influences. Earlier research also suggests Deloitte, Bright and Finity as discovery routes. These are candidates, not endorsements of their statistics. CIPP's current index mixes a 2026 heading with 2027 link labels, so the original report's cover, fieldwork and method must establish its edition.

Output: a concise landscape memo, screened source register, reader-decision map and a shortlist of questions where original practitioner examples would materially add value.

## Stage 2 Prepare the first-week validation pack

Prepare five connected items, rather than sending Nick a pile of separate requests:

1. Proposed research brief and report outline, including intended reader, population, geography, exclusions and practical decisions it will support.
2. Core practitioner questionnaire and routed interview guide, with a question-to-decision map. Each question must have an analytical purpose.
3. A short participant brief and introduction specification so Nick can identify useful contributors without needing to interpret our method.
4. A short sample/prospectus for commercial and practitioner feedback. It explains the proposition and planned outputs. Any illustrative content is labelled; it does not invent interview findings.
5. A compact decision list for Nick: approve or adjust focus, confirm introductions and brand assets, and confirm the working timetable and collection route.

Design around a recent concrete decision: approved, implemented, delayed, abandoned, renewed or still being considered. Ask about successful purchases and sensible reasons to stay, not only dissatisfaction. Separate budget owner, internal sponsor and final approver. Include non-response and not-applicable routes.

Test comprehension and flow with one or two eligible people if access permits. Do not quietly add two more interviews to the agreed scope; an eligible permissioned pilot contribution may count toward the cohort if question comparability is maintained. Otherwise do an internal walkthrough, record that external testing has not yet happened and prepare a short participant testing route.

Client approval of structure is needed before we freeze the final instrument and publication shape. User review comes before any external message. Public evidence collection and financial-model planning can continue while feedback is pending.

7. R07 Investment priorities and legitimate contextual differences.

Each module will have its own research memo when work starts. It will contain the decision, definitions, search log, linked evidence table, contradictory findings, practitioner probes, provisional synthesis and next action. We will not create a separate document for every webpage or duplicate full reports into several topic folders.

Every module ends with three reader views: what a payroll leader can do, what a supplier can learn and what the finding means for JGA's research product. An employer need is not automatically a vendor sales opportunity; procurement constraints and willingness to fund it must be examined.

## Stage 4 Collect practitioner evidence

Aim for 6–10 distinct verified senior payroll contributors through JGA's introductions. Seek variation in actual decision outcomes, employer context and delivery models where the accessible network permits. This is purposive recruitment, not a statistical quota sample.

Use one stable participant ID across both workstreams. Keep identity and permission records in approved restricted storage outside the source repository. Analytical notes use participant IDs and only the context needed to interpret the evidence. Broad professional details can still identify a person in a small council; removing the name alone is insufficient.

Record eligibility, mode, date, guide version, questions answered, missing/not-applicable responses, note status and attribution permissions. Avoid unnecessary exact budgets, employee records or commercially sensitive details. Recording is optional and permissioned, not a condition of participation.

After each contribution, write a structured note, distinguish exact quotations from paraphrase, code relevant observations and record any contradiction. Where permission or factual checking is needed, route it through the agreed process. Do not turn researcher paraphrases into participant quotations.

No raw identifiable or confidential material goes into AI/transcription services merely because a tool is available. The collection arrangement, permitted tools and any cross-border safeguards must be ready first. Public desktop research is independent of that gate.

## Stage 5 Assess The Table

Work through the Table modules in order, using the report's evidence rather than repeating it:

1. T01 Map six commercial models and free substitutes.
2. T02 Establish the reader/buyer problem and the value of membership.
3. T03 Conduct up to four introduced commercial/member conversations and record reservations as well as interest.
4. T04 Compare Model A, syndicated research without events, and Model B, event-supported research.
5. T05 Build the pilot and annual cost/revenue/cash model.
6. T06 Develop the partner package, licence options, invitation and funded pilot route.
7. T07 Write the proceed/revise/pause recommendation and 90-day roadmap.

The six reviews must span useful business mechanisms, not six superficially similar dinner clubs. Evaluate sponsored research, licensed analyst intelligence, payroll decision-support membership, a practitioner research community, a senior payroll forum and sponsor-funded executive events. The exact six will be chosen after inspecting current product terms and relevance.

For buyers, distinguish an interesting topic from a budgeted use case, approval route and credible next step. For members, distinguish accepting a premium meal from contributing useful research. Do not describe two buyer opinions as proven demand or build a price-elasticity estimate from them.

The financial model will show both business routes, one UK pilot and the proposed annual four-location programme. Include research, production, sales, staff time, recruitment, hospitality, travel, moderation, administration and contingency. Separate resource cost, cash cost, accounting profit and funding timing. Avoid double counting one annual licence across multiple event revenues.

Cash must cover commitments before they are made. Public menu prices, sponsorship packages and list prices are not achieved sales or firm private-event quotations. Record currencies, VAT/service treatment and exchange-rate dates as inputs to verify. No venue enquiry, paid quote request, booking or partner approach is authorised in this planning pass.

## Stage 6 Connect the findings and challenge the case

Build a visible chain: operational problem → desired outcome → decision participants → evidence required → implementation constraints → approval, delay or abandonment → practical response.

The Decision Framework is an original analytical tool informed by the evidence. It is not a validated predictive score or the future Confidence Index. Retain a consistent core so a later edition can be compared, with question changes logged. New groups in different cities do not constitute a longitudinal panel.

Use a challenge review before writing the final conclusion. Search for cases that contradict the main story, test whether attractive claims rely on incompatible studies and consider whether free research is already sufficient for the intended buyer. A negative Table recommendation is acceptable if supported; the fee buys reliable work, not a predetermined launch verdict.

Produce two distinct narratives: a publishable report for readers and confidential commercial advice for JGA. The first must not accidentally disclose buyer conversations, sponsor negotiations, participant identities or JGA's financial assumptions.

## Stage 7 Produce the finished package

The contracted formats and deliverable IDs are in `00-control/SCOPE-AND-DELIVERABLES.md`. The package includes:

- Designed JGA report PDF and editable Word narrative.
- Executive summary and two-page commercial preview.
- Reusable original graphics, editable chart assets and underlying Excel data.
- Evidence register, references, method, core questionnaire, coding and participant-permission templates.
- Table feasibility/launch plan and executive recommendation in PDF and editable Word or PowerPoint.
- Functional Excel workbook with pilot/annual scenarios and sensitivity checks.
- Founding partner package, council invitation, pilot specification and 90-day roadmap.

Prepare charts only after their question, data and use are clear. Use a bar chart for comparable magnitudes, a process diagram for decision stages, a matrix for trade-offs, and cash-flow/scenario charts for the business model. Do not force numerical charts onto qualitative themes or decorate weak evidence with false precision.

Every graphic has a readable title, units/base/context, source note, rights status and editable data. Provide source tables and ensure meaning does not depend on colour or hover. Motion, an interactive site and extra promotional production are not necessary to meet this scope.

## How we will report progress

At the end of each work session, update `CURRENT-STATUS.md`, the work-package tracker and relevant registers. Tell the user what was investigated, what evidence changed the view, what remains unresolved and the next problem. Do not count links as findings or drafts as approved outputs.

Before moving a module to complete, require adequate support for its conclusions, a search for serious counterevidence, and a usable decision implication or explicit evidence gap. Reopen it when new evidence changes the conclusion. Stop adding sources when they only repeat established information and all important decisions are addressed; do not stop merely because an arbitrary link quota was reached.
