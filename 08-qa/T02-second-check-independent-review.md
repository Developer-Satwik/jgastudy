## What is supported

- Actual released evidence must fit a particular proposed use. A reading path or later briefing cannot create missing cases, geographic coverage or current findings. This does not impose named-case quotas or require every eligible contributor to supply a publishable detailed account. Usable permissioned aggregate analysis and the complete contracted report remain intact.
- Edition/publication, evidence and service dates describe different objects. ISG's dated guide and context-inquiry service are a bounded analogy, not a universal expiry rule or an adopted JGA subscription.
- ONS's distinction between later-data/method revisions and mistake corrections is correctly treated as an editorial analogy. The current agreement's section 6 still governs included defects and correction obligations. No unilateral fee, service-level period or reduced remedy is created.
- A briefing may tailor interpretation of existing material. Requests for genuinely absent evidence or a new client-specific study require an actual scope decision; brevity of a request does not prove that it is a formatting change. No new advisory service is promised.
- Acknowledgement placement, an agreed asset or a briefing can be specified as delivered benefits. Those objects do not prove readership, sales, lead access or research impact. Oakleaf's webinar-to-article/recording route supports the format counterexample only; its legal/tax claims and audience outcomes were not imported. The design does not falsely require a unique format or conclude that free material makes paid interpretation unviable.
- The stage-specific partner script accurately describes support for an already commissioned/completed edition rather than a new study. Legitimate licence/partner revenues are not prohibited; costs and bundled entitlements must be counted once.
- Feedback is attributed to the actual concept and task shown. Unshown variants remain unknown, and an unaided need, feature reaction, conditional proposal request and agreement are contextual statements rather than an ordinal conversion funnel.
