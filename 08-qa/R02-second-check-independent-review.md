## Findings from the challenge

- Historical comparison preserves positive modernisation and exception mechanisms without inventing a common incident measure, deterioration trend or realised forecast. Prevention, legacy correction and consequence closure are appropriately presented as analytical distinctions.
- Recruitment providers remain separate from payroll execution/software suppliers. The comparator material supplies role/task contrasts, not a claim of shortage, completed hiring, superior results or inevitable recruitment demand. A published request and a supplier-reported case remain attributed evidence classes.
- The prior AI study is used for selected question contrasts and source leads. Tool answers, repeated runs and recommendation counts are not recast as employers, transactions, market share or observed needs. Incomplete prior source capture and changing live wording are explicitly preserved.
- Price and scope are not conflated. Licence/add-on/transaction charges are distinguished from managed processing and retained effort. No total-cost or affordability conclusion is calculated from the illustrative offers. This section is self-reviewed, as noted above.
- Correct bank payment, reporting, records, explanation, authorisation and continuity remain distinct. The British Library example is not used to claim payroll-data exposure or prove cloud causation. Salary-diversion guidance is not turned into incidence or a universal delay policy. The Bacs cycle is not generalised to all payment methods or corrections.

- Context tags do not silently enlarge the proposed cohort or add mandatory interview items. The micro-employer example remains a market boundary. The four capacity contexts are optional analytical categories, not a validated segmentation or scored model.
